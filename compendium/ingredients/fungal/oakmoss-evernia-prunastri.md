# Oakmoss

Oakmoss is a lichen, a partnership of a fungus and an alga, that grows mostly on the trunks and branches of oak trees in mountainous temperate forests across the Northern Hemisphere. Wikipedia says its flat, strap-like, branching thalli form bushy clumps, green to greenish-white when dry and darker when wet, and that it is harvested in south-central Europe and sent to Grasse in France for perfume, where its absolute is used as a fixative. Kynes treats it under the heading Lichen, together with Florida beard lichen, and records that baskets of oakmoss were found in royal tombs of Egypt and that in fifteenth-century Europe it scented hair. She places it at Samhain, ties it to luck, prosperity, psychic abilities and spirit guides, and has you hold a branch for clairvoyance. The AHPA handbook records that the hot alcohol extract is toxic and not for internal use, and that allergic reactions to it as a fragrance have been reported.
