# Lingzhi

Lingzhi, also called reishi from its Japanese pronunciation, is a polypore or bracket fungus of the genus Ganoderma, native to East Asia. The page names Ganoderma sichuanense as the currently accepted name. It has a reddish-brown, varnished, kidney-shaped cap with bands and a stem set at the edge, lacks gills and releases its spores through fine yellow pores. In nature it grows at the base and stumps of deciduous trees, especially maples, and it can be cultivated on hardwood logs, sawdust or woodchips. It is used in traditional Chinese medicine and is the ancient mushroom of immortality, revered for over 2,000 years. In Chinese art it symbolises health and longevity. Which Ganoderma species is the true lingzhi is still debated, and several similar species were probably treated as interchangeable.

#### Questions for the Curator

- The Wikipedia page says "as of 2023 there is an ongoing debate on which one of the described Ganoderma species is the true lingzhi mushroom". It gives Ganoderma sichuanense as the currently accepted name, treats Ganoderma lingzhi as a later synonym (Du et al. 2023), and says Ganoderma lucidum is a distinct species although the old name persists. Should the entry carry Ganoderma sichuanense? I recommend yes, with a note that older sources and products say G. lucidum, and that any other compendium entry citing G. lucidum for reishi be checked.
