# Lion's Mane

Hericium erinaceus, commonly called lion's mane, yamabushitake, bearded tooth fungus or bearded hedgehog, is a species of tooth fungus native to North America and Eurasia. It tends to grow in a single clump with dangling spines longer than a centimetre, and the fruit body is a large, irregular, bulbous mass 5 to 40 centimetres across. It is common in late summer and autumn on hardwoods, particularly American beech and maple, and is usually saprophytic, feeding on dead trees, though it is also found on living trees at a wound. It is an edible mushroom and is used in traditional Chinese medicine. The Japanese name yamabushitake refers to the yamabushi, the mountain ascetics of Shugendo. Cultivation is widespread in Asia, on logs, bottles or bags of sawdust. Its medicinal effectiveness, safety in supplements and active compounds are described as unproven.
