# Musk

Musk is a class of aromatic substances used as base notes in perfumery. It includes glandular secretions from animals such as the musk deer, plants with similar fragrances, and artificial substances with similar odours. The name was first given to a strong-smelling substance from a gland of the musk deer, and it comes from the Late Greek moskhos, from Persian mushk and Sanskrit muṣka. Musk has been a popular perfume fixative since ancient times and is one of the most expensive animal products in the world. The musk pod is a gland in a pouch under the abdominal skin of the male deer; once dried, the paste inside turns into black granules called musk grain, which are tinctured with alcohol and used heavily diluted. The compound mainly responsible for the odour is muscone. Natural musk gave way to synthetic musk in perfumery by the late nineteenth century, and traditional Chinese medicine now uses a synthetic version, except for specially exempt drugs.
