# Bezoar

A bezoar is a mass, often found trapped in the gastrointestinal system though it can occur elsewhere. Wikipedia says there are several varieties, some with inorganic constituents and some organic, and that the word has both a modern medical sense and a traditional one. The listed kinds include food boluses, which carry the archaic and positive meaning of the word, phytobezoars of indigestible plant matter, trichobezoars of hair and lactobezoars of thickened milk. Ox bezoars, called niu-huang or calculi bovis, are gallstones or gallstone substitutes formed from the bile of cattle, and are used in Chinese herbology to treat various diseases. The same page records that Islamic physicians such as Ibn Zuhr described the bezoar stone as an antidote. A pseudobezoar is an indigestible object put into the digestive system on purpose.
