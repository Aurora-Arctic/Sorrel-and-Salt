# Cicada Shells

Cicada shells are the cast-off skins, called exuviae, that cicada nymphs leave behind when they moult into adults. Cicadas are large insects, a superfamily of the order Hemiptera with more than 3,000 species in the family Cicadidae, known for the loud song of the males. The nymphs live underground, feeding on sap from the roots of trees, and climb out to moult. Being lightweight and hook-legged, the shells can be used as hair or clothing accessories. The source says shells of cicadas are used in traditional Chinese medicine, which claims anti-convulsive, sedative and hypothermic (temperature-lowering) effects. The page does not say which species the medicine uses. Cicadas themselves appear in literature from Homer, in Chinese decorative art from the Shang dynasty, and in Japanese haiku, and they are eaten in selected regions of China and elsewhere.
