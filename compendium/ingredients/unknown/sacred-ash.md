# Sacred Ash

Vibhuti, also called bhasmam or tirunīru, is sacred ash made of burnt dried wood, burnt cow dung and, the page says, sometimes cremated bodies, used in Agamic rituals in Hinduism. Devotees of Shiva traditionally apply it as three horizontal lines across the forehead, known as tripundra, and on other parts of the body. For ash to count as vibhuti it must be made of specific ingredients and also under ritually significant circumstances. The page describes collecting fresh cow dung, preferably from Indian cattle, forming it into cakes and drying it in sunlight for about a week. The dried dung is layered in a pit or pile with plant material such as rice husks and hay, and burned over several days until it is reduced to a dark, or ideally pale grey or white, ash. Ashes of plants with religious significance may be used instead, and herbs or perfumes may be added for fragrance, though adulteration is frowned upon.
