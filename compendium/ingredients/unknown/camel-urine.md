# Camel Urine

Camel urine is a liquid by-product of metabolism in a camel's body, and it comes out as a thick syrup. The page says the camel's kidneys and intestines reabsorb water very efficiently, which limits the volume of urine in desert conditions. It has been used in medicine for centuries as part of Ayurvedic and Islamic Prophetic medicine. The Caraka-Samhita calls it slightly bitter and recommends it for hiccups, cough and haemorrhoids. A hadith in al-Bukhari's collection, narrated by Anas ibn Malik, tells of people of the 'Ukl or 'Uraina tribe whom Muhammad directed to the herd of milch camels to drink their milk and urine as medicine. Avicenna noted that a mixture of camel milk and urine can help in dropsy and jaundice. Some Muslims see its use as najis, ritually unclean. In Yemen it is sold bottled, and it is traditionally mixed with milk. The World Health Organization says its use as a medicine lacks scientific evidence.
