# Bamboo Salt

Bamboo salt, jugyeom in Korean, is a Korean condiment and traditional remedy. It is made by packing sea salt into thick bamboo stems sealed with yellow clay and roasting them in a pine fire at about 800 degrees Celsius, which burns away the bamboo and leaves a column of salt. The columns are ground, repacked in bamboo and roasted again, nine times in all. The ninth roasting, above 1,000 degrees in a special kiln, melts the salt, which is drained into a mould and cools into a blackened, rock-like mass. The salt takes up constituents of the bamboo and a sweetness the article calls the gamrojung flavour. Salt roasted above 1,500 degrees is called purple bamboo salt. It is mostly sodium chloride, 85 to 98 per cent. In Korean folk medicine it has been used as a digestive aid, a styptic, a disinfectant and a dentifrice, and today it also goes into cooking, toothpaste and soap.
