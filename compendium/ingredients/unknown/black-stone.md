# Black Stone

The Black Stone, in Arabic al-Hajar al-Aswad, is a rock set into the eastern corner of the Kaaba in the Masjid al-Haram in Mecca. Most Muslims revere it as a relic that tradition dates to Adam and Eve. It was venerated at the Kaaba before Islam, and it is sometimes called a baetyl, an unshaped sacred stone. Tradition says Muhammad set it into the wall in 605, five years before his first revelation. It has since broken into fragments, which are held in a silver frame on the side of the Kaaba. It looks like a dark fragmented rock, polished smooth by pilgrims' hands. It has often been described as a meteorite, but it has never been analysed with modern methods, so its origin remains a matter of speculation. Pilgrims circling the Kaaba during the tawaf in the Hajj try to kiss it, as Muhammad is recorded to have done. Theologians stress that it has no divine significance and matters for its history.
