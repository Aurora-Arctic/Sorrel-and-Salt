# Carbuncle

Carbuncle is another name for a deep red almandine gemstone cut with a smooth, convex face, a method called cabochon. Traditionally the term referred to any red gemstone, most often a red garnet. The page says that until the end of the eighteenth century, when crystal-chemical definitions arrived, the name covered an intermingling of red stones: sardonyx and carnelian, garnets, ruby and spinel. In medieval texts a carbuncle is a stone with magical properties, usually able to light a dark room. Prester John's letter of 1165 says carbuncles lit his palace at night. Some English translations of the Bible, such as the Jewish Publication Society version, use carbuncle for the Hebrew nopekh, the fourth stone in the breastplate of the Hoshen in Exodus 28:18 and 39:11. It is also named in Ezekiel 28:13 and Isaiah 54:12. In South American folklore a carbuncle is instead a small elusive animal that carries a shining gemstone.
