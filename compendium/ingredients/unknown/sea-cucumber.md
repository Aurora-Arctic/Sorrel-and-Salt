# Sea Cucumber

Sea cucumbers are echinoderms of the class Holothuroidea, benthic marine animals found on the sea floor worldwide, with about 1,786 known species and the greatest number in the Asia–Pacific region. This entry has no single formal name because the cited page describes a whole class, and the many species sold as the ingredient are not settled here. The animals have leathery skin and an elongated body, and are named for their resemblance to a cucumber. Many species are foraged as food, and some are farmed. They are a delicacy seafood, especially in Asian cuisines, and the harvested product is called trepang, namako, bêche-de-mer or balate. Makassar trepangers traded with Indigenous Australians of Arnhem Land from at least the 18th century. Gamat is the Malay traditional medicinal use, and extracts are made into oils, creams and cosmetics. Overfishing in the Great Barrier Reef is threatening their population.

#### Questions for the Curator

Which sea cucumber species does this row mean? The cited page covers the whole class Holothuroidea and names several traded species (for example Holothuria scabra, Apostichopus japonicus and Isostichopus fuscus) without printing an IUCN status for any. A species rated Endangered or Critically Endangered by the global IUCN assessment would be left out under the no-endangered-species rule, and this lane has not read an assessment for any of them.
