# Mumia

Mummia, mumia or mummy was any of several preparations in the history of medicine, from mineral pitch to powdered human mummies. It began as Arabic mūmiyā, a type of resinous bitumen found in western Asia and used curatively in traditional Islamic medicine. In medieval European medicine the word was written mumia and meant both a bituminous medicine from Persia and a mummy. Apothecaries sold costly mummia bitumen as a cure-all and an aphrodisiac. From about the 12th century, when imported bitumen ran short, the word was misread as mummy and a trade in material scraped from embalmed Egyptian bodies grew. After Egypt banned the shipments in the 16th century, some European apothecaries sold fraudulent mummia made from fresh corpses. Belief in its value faded by the 18th century, and artists used ground mummies for the paint mummy brown. Medieval Persian physicians used the bitumen as a salve and for stomach ulcers.
