# Onycha

Onycha was, with equal parts of stacte, galbanum and frankincense, one of the components of the consecrated ketoret, the incense in Exodus 30:34-36, used in Solomon's Temple. The page says what the onycha of antiquity actually was cannot be determined with certainty. The Hebrew word was shecheleth, related to Syriac words for a tear or exudation, and the Septuagint substituted the Greek onycha, meaning fingernail or claw. One contender is the fingernail-like operculum, the trapdoor, of sea snails such as Strombus lentiginosus and Murex anguliferus, which are still used in North African and Middle Eastern bakhoor and in East Asian incense. Another is labdanum, the black resin of the rock rose (Cistus), whose petals are said to resemble fingernails. Objections to the snail include that shellfish were unclean in the Bible, and the Talmud says onycha is a ground plant, not a tree.
