# Bdellium

Bdellium, also spelt bdellion and called false myrrh, is a semi-transparent oleo-gum resin, meaning a resin that holds a water-soluble gum and an essential oil. Wikipedia says it is taken from Commiphora wightii plants and from Commiphora africana trees of sub-Saharan Africa. Pliny the Elder placed the best quality in Bactria and named Arabia, India, Media and Babylonia as other sources. African bdellium is the name for the Commiphora africana resin. It is used in perfumery, as incense and in traditional medicine, and it is used to adulterate the dearer myrrh. The Hebrew word bedolach, translated bdellium, occurs in Genesis 2:12, as a product of Havilah, and in Numbers 11:7, where manna is said to be the colour of bdellium. The page says there is no agreement that bedolach meant the resin: the Septuagint reads a precious stone in both places, and Rashi calls it a precious stone, crystal.
