# Stacte

Stacte (staktē) and nataph (nāṭāf) are names for one component of the Ketoret, the incense of Solomon's Temple specified in the Book of Exodus. Some translations render the word as stacte and others as an unspecified gum resin. It was to be mixed in equal parts with onycha, galbanum and pure frankincense, and some of it beaten very small for burning on the altar of the tabernacle. The Hebrew word nataf means drop, and the Septuagint translates it as stacte, a Greek word for an oozing substance. The page gives three strongest candidates: myrrh exuded naturally from the tree, a rare myrrh called opobalsamum, and myrrh scented with styrax. It records ancient accounts, including Theophrastus and Pliny, that describe stacte as a product of myrrh, and the view of Rabban Shimon ben Gamliel that it is the sap dripping from the balsam tree.
