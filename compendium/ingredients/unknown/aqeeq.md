# Aqeeq

Aqeeq, also spelt akik or aqiq, is an Arabic and Turkish word for quartz or agate. In the context of rings it usually means a ring set with a chalcedony stone. The page names carnelian, agate and onyx as well-known kinds of chalcedony. It counts as a semi-precious gemstone, and rings set with the golden-orange carnelian or sard have special significance in Islam. To make one, the stone is ground to shape, often a cabochon or a short wide cylinder, and polished. Its flat face is sometimes engraved with a religious motto in Arabic, which may be inlaid with gold, and the finished stone is mounted in a ring. Men and women both wear aqiq rings. Wearing one is considered sunnah, the practice of the Prophet Muhammad, who is said to have worn a carnelian ring set in silver to mark the removal of idols from the Grand Mosque in Mecca in 630 CE. Many Muslims do the same, including Shia and Sunni clergy.
