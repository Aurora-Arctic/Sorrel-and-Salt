# Bitumen

The source for this entry is the Wikipedia article on beauty and cosmetics in ancient Egypt, which names it once. It says that during mummification, ointments, resins, bitumen and beeswax were applied to the body to perfume it, soften the skin, seal it or reshape it at different stages of treatment. The article treats these as materials of the ritual preparation of the dead, alongside the oils, ointments and aromatic resins that ancient Egyptians used for body care, scenting and the protection of the skin. It says that the ingredients of Egyptian cosmetics could be mineral, plant-derived or animal-derived. The article gives no further detail on where the bitumen came from or how much was used. The entry is therefore limited to that one recorded use until a fuller source is added.
