# Turbah

A turbah, also called mohr, khāk-e shefā or prayer stone, is a small piece of soil or clay, often a clay tablet, used during salah, the Islamic prayer, to symbolise earth. The Twelver branch of Shia Islam recommends it, and the page calls it a unique practice of that sect. Many ahadith mention the benefits of prostrating on soil or an alternative natural material. The most recommended soil is that of Karbala, the site of the martyrdom of Husayn ibn Ali, though soil from anywhere may be used, and where none is available plants, items made from them, or paper may be substituted. Shia Muslims have made small tablets from the earth of Karbala to share it worldwide. Many Sunnis reject the turbah as an innovation, and the page says it is stigmatised in most Muslim-majority countries outside Iran, Azerbaijan, Bahrain and Iraq.

#### Questions for the Curator

Identity: the page describes a made object (a soil or clay tablet), not a plant or a named material, so I recorded nomenclature unknown. The Karbala soil it prefers is a place, not a formal name. I recommend the owner decide whether a prayer tablet belongs in the compendium at all, and if so whether it should be one entry for the tablet or one for Karbala soil.

Sacred soil: the page says Karbala dust is considered sacred. It does not say taking or selling it is restricted, so I recorded no closed practice. Nothing in the page limits who may use a turbah: it says soil from anywhere may be used.
