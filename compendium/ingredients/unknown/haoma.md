# Haoma

Haoma is a divine plant in Zoroastrianism and in later Persian culture and mythology. It comes from Indo-Iranian religion and is the cognate of Vedic soma. The Avesta describes a plant with stems, roots and branches that is tall, fragrant, golden-green and can be pressed. It grows on mountains. Its effects are said to include healing, sexual arousal, physical strength and alertness, and its mildly intoxicating extract is consumed without bad effects. In present-day Zoroastrian practice, the twigs are pounded with a little water to make parahaoma. Indian Zoroastrians import the twigs because they believe the plant cannot grow on the subcontinent. The page says Yazd Zoroastrians used the genus Ephedra, locally hum or homa, and that Parsis use a variant of Ephedra, usually Ephedra procera, from the Hari River valley in Afghanistan. Some scholars, including Falk and Houben, conclude that Ephedra is the logical identity. Haoma is also a divinity, the Yazata Haoma, the first priest.
