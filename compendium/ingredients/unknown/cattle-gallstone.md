# Cattle Gallstone

Calculus bovis, niu-huang in Chinese, is the name for the dried gallstones of cattle used in Chinese herbology. In China and Japan it has long been used for illnesses that include high fever, convulsion and stroke. In Asian countries the stones are sometimes taken when steers are slaughtered: the gall bladder is removed, the bile is filtered, and the stones are cleaned and dried. Vets can also remove them surgically from sick working cattle. In Western countries they are usually thrown away. Hindu culture has an equivalent in gorochana. The stones range from golden yellow to brownish yellow and can be round, oval, triangular, tubular or irregular. Natural stones are scarce and costly. Artificial stones, made from cholic acid, bovine bile powder and other ingredients, are used as substitutes, though Chinese regulations forbid the artificial kind in 42 drugs. Stones can also be cultivated in living cattle or in vitro, and these may replace the natural stone under Chinese rules.
