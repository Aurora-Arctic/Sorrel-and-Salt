# Red Lead

Lead(II,IV) oxide, also called red lead, lead tetroxide or minium, has the formula Pb3O4. It is a bright red or orange solid used as a pigment, in making batteries and in rustproof primer paints. It is a mixed-valence compound, with lead(II) and lead(IV) in the ratio of two to one. It is made by heating lead(II) oxide in air at about 450 to 480 degrees Celsius. Natural minium is uncommon; the best known specimens come from Broken Hill in New South Wales, where they formed in a mine fire. It is almost insoluble in water but dissolves in the hydrochloric acid of the stomach, so it is toxic when swallowed. Because of its toxicity its use is being limited. In traditional Chinese medicine it is used to treat ringworm and ulcerations, though this is limited because of the toxicity.
