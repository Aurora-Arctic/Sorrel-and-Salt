# Murex Snail

Hexaplex trunculus is the sea snail that the Tekhelet page names as the identified source of tekhelet, the blue dye of the Hebrew Bible. Tekhelet coloured the fringes, called tzitzit, of Jewish prayer garments and the clothing of the High Priest, and the page says it was expensive and hard to obtain. Rabbinic writing records that it came from a marine animal called the ḥillāzon, and the practical knowledge of making it was lost over time. In 1913 Yitzhak HaLevi Herzog proposed the snail as the most likely source but could not reliably get a blue dye from it. In the 1980s the chemist Otto Elsner found that exposing the dye solution to sunlight turns the red dibromoindigo extracted from the snail's hypobranchial gland into a mixture of blue indigo and blue-purple 6-bromoindigo. A rabbi dyed tekhelet from the snail for tzitzit in 1988.
