# Seahorse

A seahorse is any of 47 species of small marine bony fish in the genus Hippocampus, of the family Syngnathidae, with pipefishes and seadragons. It has a head and neck like a horse, segmented bony armour, an upright posture and a curled prehensile tail, and lives in seas, brackish estuaries and lagoons. The male carries the eggs in a brood pouch on the front of the tail after the female deposits up to 1,500 eggs in it. The genus name comes from the Greek for horse and sea monster. Seahorses are consumed widely in traditional Chinese medicine, primarily in connection with impotence, wheezing and nocturnal enuresis, though the page notes a lack of scientific studies or clinical trials. The page says populations are thought to be endangered through overfishing and habitat destruction, and that pills are replacing whole seahorses.
