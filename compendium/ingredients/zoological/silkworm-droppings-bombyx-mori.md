# Silkworm Droppings

Bombyx mori, the domestic silk moth, is a domesticated moth of the family Bombycidae, the closest relative of the wild silk moth Bombyx mandarina. Silkworms are its larvae and are the primary producer of silk. The silkworm eats only mulberry leaves. Silk moths depend entirely on people for reproduction after millennia of selective breeding. Sericulture has existed for at least 5,000 years in China. The page says the silkworm droppings, cánshā, are secretions used in traditional Chinese medicine. It adds that carp on Chinese silk farms were fed silk moth larvae and faeces. This entry is the droppings.

#### Questions for the Curator

- The ingredient is the droppings of Bombyx mori larvae, not the moth. The Wikipedia page 'Bombyx mori' (oldid 1381559562) prints the formal name for the animal and says only 'The silkworm droppings (cánshā, 蠶沙/蚕沙) are secretions used in traditional Chinese medicine.' Should the entry carry the animal's formal name for a product of it? I recommend yes, with the droppings as the variant, but a second, whole-silkworm entry may be wanted.
