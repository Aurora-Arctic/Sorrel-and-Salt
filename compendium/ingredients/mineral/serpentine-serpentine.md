# Serpentine

Serpentine is the name of a subgroup of related minerals; Wikipedia describes them as greenish, brownish or spotted, with a name from the Latin for snake-like, and both magic books print it as a single heading. Hall lists its colours as red, green, brown, black-green and white, mottled and often polished, from Cornwall, Norway, Russia, Zimbabwe, Italy and the United States. Cunningham gives the folk name Za-tu-mush-gir (Assyrian), Saturn and Fire, and warns that these correspondences are tentative. He says Assyrian seals made of serpentine were carried so that the gods would send double blessings, that nursing women wear it to regulate their milk, and that it is mainly used to guard against poisonous creatures. Hall associates it with meditation, psychic abilities, wisdom, memory of past lives, cleansing and longevity. Wikipedia warns that asbestos from serpentine can be released into the air and is a long-term hazard to breathe.

#### Questions for the Curator

Both headings (Cunningham SERPENTINE, Hall SERPENTINE) name a group; Wikipedia titles its article 'Serpentine subgroup' and lists antigorite, lizardite and chrysotile (infobox line 18). No single mineral species is meant, so 'Serpentine' may need a different nomenclature.
