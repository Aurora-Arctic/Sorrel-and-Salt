# Pearl

A pearl forms inside the soft tissue of a living shelled mollusc and is hard and lustrous. Wikipedia says it is made mainly of calcium carbonate laid down in concentric layers, and that irregular shapes are called baroque pearls. Cunningham says nearly all pearls are now cultured, that cultured pearls are mostly shell and, in his view, less magically potent than natural ones, and that freshwater pearls share the properties of sea pearls. He notes that taking the pearl means killing the oyster, that some believe this leaves a debt on those who trade in or wear pearls, and that he does not advocate their use. Cunningham ties pearls to the Moon, to water and to a range of goddesses, among them Isis and Freya, and says women usually wear them. He records their use in love magic, in a money spell, for protection from sharks and fire, and, at various times and places, to lengthen life, promote fertility and lend courage and strength. He also says colour matters: pink pearls, for example, are worn for an easy life.
