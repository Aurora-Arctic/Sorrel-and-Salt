# Sunstone

Sunstone is a feldspar that glitters when light strikes it from certain angles, an effect known as aventurescence. Wikipedia names it as a variety of either oligoclase or microcline; oligoclase is the usual one, but orthoclase sometimes shows the effect too, so the trade names two kinds after those two minerals. The sparkle comes from tiny flat flakes of copper, hematite or goethite lying in parallel inside the crystal, which is why it has also been called aventurine-feldspar. It was long best known from Tvedestrand in southern Norway, and turns up near Lake Baikal, in South Australia and in several American states; Oregon's copper-bearing sunstone, darker the more copper it holds, became that state's gemstone in 1987. Cunningham writes that two stones go by the name, and that only the old feldspar kind, once brought from India and fiery like an orange opal, was used in magic; Renaissance magicians wore it mounted in gold to take in the Sun's influence. He gives it to the Sun and fire and uses it for protection, for strengthening healing herbs, and for physical and sexual energy. Hall calls it a joyful stone that sharpens intuition, lifts low moods, builds confidence and helps cut unwanted emotional ties.
