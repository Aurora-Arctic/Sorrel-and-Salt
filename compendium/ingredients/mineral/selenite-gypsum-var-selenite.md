# Selenite

Selenite is the clear, see-through variety of gypsum, a calcium sulphate mineral. Wikipedia says the name comes from the Greek for moon stone, because people historically believed the mineral waxed and waned with the Moon. Cunningham calls it a clear, layered mineral that resembles calcite, gives it the Moon and Water, and says it is named for Selene, an ancient Moon goddess. He says lovers exchange it for reconciliation and that it is worn to give the body energy. Hall lists its colours as pure white, orange, blue, brown and green, easily obtained from many countries, and associates it with clarity of mind, calm and deep peace, meditation, and protecting a house with a grid of pieces or one large piece. She also mentions scrying and telepathy, and notes that it dissolves when wet. Hall treats satin spar and desert rose as other names for selenite; Wikipedia lists them as other varieties of gypsum, so this entry leaves them out.

#### Questions for the Curator

Neither book prints 'Gypsum var. selenite'. Wikipedia (Selenite (gypsum), line 36) says selenite is a 'variety of the sulfate mineral gypsum', which supports the roster name.

Hall's heading SELENITE says 'also known as satin spar, desert rose' and describes coloured and fishtail sorts under it; Wikipedia (lines 36-38) treats satin spar and desert rose as other gypsum varieties.

Selene is included as a deity because Cunningham says the stone is 'Named for Selene'; he has no Deity line for it. The curator may drop it.
