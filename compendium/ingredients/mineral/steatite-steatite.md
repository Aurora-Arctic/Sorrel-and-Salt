# Steatite

Steatite, also known as soapstone or soaprock, is a talc-schist, a metamorphic rock composed largely of the magnesium-rich mineral talc. Wikipedia says it has been a carving medium for thousands of years, and that the definitions of steatite and soapstone vary with the field of study: in geology, steatite is a rock very largely composed of talc, while the mining industry defines it as a high-purity talc rock. It is relatively soft because of its high talc content, and softer grades may feel like soap. It is easy to carve, durable and heat-resistant. The article says ancient Egyptian scarab signets and amulets were most commonly made from glazed steatite, and that the Yoruba of West Nigeria used soapstone for statues, notably at Esie. This entry rests on that one article.
