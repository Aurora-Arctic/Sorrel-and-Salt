# Moonstone

Moonstone is a feldspar, a sodium potassium aluminium silicate, valued for the soft milky sheen that seems to drift over its surface as it is turned. Gemmologists call this glow adularescence: light is scattered by very thin alternating layers of two different feldspars inside the stone, and the thinner those layers, the bluer the light. Wikipedia's infobox classes it as a variety of orthoclase, and most moonstone is adularia, the orthoclase named after Mount Adular in Switzerland, though oligoclase, a plagioclase feldspar, can produce it too. Sri Lanka supplies most of today's trade, and it is also found in India, Myanmar, Madagascar, Australia, Norway and elsewhere. The Romans thought it was moonlight made solid, and they and the Greeks tied it to their moon deities. Cunningham describes it as an opalescent feldspar in shades of blue, white and pink, gives it to the Moon and water, and dedicates it to Diana, Selene, Isis and the other lunar goddesses. He uses it for love, for mending quarrels between lovers, for sleep, for gardens and fruit trees, for protection when travelling over water, for divination and psychism, and for youth and dieting. Hall calls it a stone of new beginnings, tied to the moon and to intuition, that above all calms the emotions; she says it has long been used to strengthen psychic abilities and credits it with deep emotional healing.
