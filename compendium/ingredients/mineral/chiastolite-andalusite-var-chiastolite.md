# Chiastolite

Chiastolite is a kind of andalusite, an aluminium silicate. Its mark is a dark cross, made by graphite inclusions, which shows when a crystal is cut across. Wikipedia records that from the sixteenth century pilgrims who had visited Santiago de Compostela carried it as an amulet or souvenir, and that it was once called the cross stone. Hall describes pieces that are usually small and often tumbled, in browns, greys, reddish-browns and olive green, found in Chile, Russia and Spain. Hall calls it a strongly protective stone, used against ill wishing and curses, and links it to turning dissension into harmony and to creativity. She also associates it with dissolving illusions, easing fears and guilt, and releasing worn-out patterns, and with easing rheumatism and gout. These are Hall's claims, not established medicine.

#### Questions for the Curator

The roster lists Cunningham as a source for Chiastolite, but Cunningham has no entry for it, so only Hall and Wikipedia are cited.

Wikipedia calls chiastolite a variety of andalusite, which supports the formal name. Hall does not print 'var.'; her heading is CHIASTOLITE, also known as Cross Stone and Andalusite.
