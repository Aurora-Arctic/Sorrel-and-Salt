# Mica

Wikipedia identifies muscovite as common mica, also called isinglass or potash mica, a hydrated phyllosilicate mineral, white, grey or silvery. Cunningham treats mica as a general term for minerals with paper-thin flexible sheets of crystals, gives its planet as Mercury and its element as air, and uses it for divination and psychic powers, and for general protection. Hall's entry is for muscovite, which it also calls mica, and it describes pearl-like layers in pink, grey, brown, green, violet, yellow, red or white. Hall calls it the commonest form of mica and associates it with angelic contact, opening intuition and psychic vision, unconditional love, and integration and transformation. Hall names Pink and Violet Muscovite as separate sorts, which are not covered here.

#### Questions for the Curator

Cunningham's heading MICA (p. 113) says 'a general term for minerals exhibiting paper-thin flexible sheets', so it covers several minerals and does not name muscovite. Hall's MUSCOVITE heading says 'also known as mica', and Wikipedia (Muscovite, line 48) calls muscovite 'common mica'. The label 'Mica' therefore maps to more than one mineral; Cunningham is cited with this doubt. Lead to decide.
