# Limonite

Limonite is an iron ore made of a mixture of hydrated iron(III) oxide-hydroxides in varying proportion, often written FeO(OH)·nH2O, though that is not exact. It is one of the three principal iron ores, with hematite and magnetite, and has been mined for iron since at least 400 BC. The name comes from the Greek for wet meadow or marshy lake, because it occurs as bog iron ore. In its brown form it is sometimes called brown hematite or brown iron ore. It is medium to dark yellowish brown, with a yellowish-brown streak that tells it from red-streaked hematite and black-streaked magnetite, and a hardness varying from 1 to 5. It is now regarded as a field term for a mixture of goethite, lepidocrocite, akaganeite and jarosite, and does not form crystals itself.
