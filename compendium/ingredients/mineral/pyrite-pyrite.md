# Pyrite

Pyrite is an iron sulphide, the most abundant sulphide mineral, and is also called iron pyrite or fool's gold. Wikipedia says the metallic shine and the pale brass-yellow colour make it look a little like gold, hence the nickname. Cunningham gives it Mars, fire and a projective energy, and says it is used for money, divination and luck: pieces set on an altar among green candles to draw wealth, or carried for money and luck, and a flat polished face as a scrying glass for stirring psychic impulses. He adds that ancient Mexicans made polished mirrors from it, which may have been used for divining. Hall calls it an excellent energy shield that blocks negative energy and, worn at the neck, guards the subtle and physical bodies. Hall also says it boosts confidence and self-worth, improves memory and recall, and balances instinct with intuition. These are the claims of those authors.

#### Questions for the Curator

Hall prints the heading "Iron Pyrite", not "Pyrite". Cunningham lists Iron Pyrite as a folk name of Pyrite and Wikipedia says "pyrite, or iron pyrite", so the headings are taken as the same mineral; confirm.
