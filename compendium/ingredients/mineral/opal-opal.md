# Opal

Opal is a hydrated form of silica without a crystal structure. Wikipedia classes it as a mineraloid rather than a true mineral, says it can form in cracks in almost any rock, and says the precious kind shows a play of colours. It also derives the name from a Sanskrit word for a jewel. Cunningham lists Cupid as its deity and says it holds the colours and qualities of every other stone, so that it can be charged for nearly any purpose. He rejects the popular idea that opals bring misfortune and traces it to a novel by Sir Walter Scott. Cunningham says opals were once wrapped in a bay leaf to bring invisibility, and are worn for astral projection, for recalling past lives, for developing psychic powers and for bringing out inner beauty. Hall calls it a delicate stone and associates it with cosmic consciousness, creativity, transformation, and love and passion, adding that it may amplify fickleness. Hall also says that, once programmed, it makes the wearer unnoticed, and that it strengthens memory. These are the claims of those authors.

#### Questions for the Curator

Wikipedia classes opal as a mineraloid because it is amorphous, not as a mineral; the roster gives nomenclature mineral. Confirm that mineral is the right nomenclature.
