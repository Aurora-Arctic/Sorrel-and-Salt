# Cacao

Theobroma cacao, the cacao or cocoa tree, is a small evergreen tree in the mallow family, Malvaceae. Its seeds, called cocoa beans once dried and fermented, are used to make chocolate liquor, cocoa powder, cocoa butter and chocolate. The tree is native to the tropics of the Americas and is widely distributed from southeastern Mexico to the Amazon basin; the largest producer of cocoa beans in 2022 was Côte d'Ivoire. DNA studies suggest it was first domesticated around Iquitos, in modern Peru and Ecuador, more than 5,000 years ago, probably for the pulp around the seeds. Ancient texts describe several cacao mixtures for ceremonial, medicinal and culinary purposes, some with maize, chilli, vanilla and honey, and traces of theobromine and caffeine have been found in vessels from Honduras and Belize dating back to 1100 BC. Cacao seeds cannot be stored in a conventional genebank, so its diversity is kept in field collections.
