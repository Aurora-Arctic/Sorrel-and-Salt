# Frostwort

Frostwort, or longbranch frostweed, is a flowering perennial of eastern North America, 8 to 24 inches tall. Wikipedia says it blooms from May to June, each stem usually bearing one yellow five-petalled flower, 3/4 to 1 1/4 inches across, that lasts a single day; the leaves and stems are hairy. Grieve says the genus name comes from the Greek for sun and flower, and that two forms are known, flowering early and late. She names the dried herb as the part used and calls it antiscrofulous, astringent, alterative and tonic, taken for diarrhoea and ulcerations, used as a wash for prurigo and a gargle for scarlatina. She adds that an oil helpful in cancer is said to have been got from it.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve prints "Helianthemum Canadense (MISCH.)", which matches the roster name apart from capitalisation and the authority. The cited Wikipedia page says the species is also called _Crocanthemum canadense_. GBIF (evidence only, not an authority): https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Helianthemum%20canadense gives _Helianthemum canadense_ (L.) Michx., SYNONYM, accepted key 2874071, which is _Crocanthemum canadense_ (L.) Britton (ACCEPTED). Should the entry keep the name _Helianthemum canadense_ as the roster has it, or move to _Crocanthemum canadense_? My recommendation: keep the roster name for now and decide the accepted name when POWO can be read.
