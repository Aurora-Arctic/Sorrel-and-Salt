# Lavender Cotton

Lavender cotton is a small evergreen shrub of the daisy family, native to the western and central Mediterranean, growing to about 50 cm tall and broad. Wikipedia describes dense, aromatic, grey-green leaves and, in summer, masses of yellow, button-like flower heads on slender stems, and says it is grown as groundcover or as an edging plant for a hot, dry spot. Grieve says it is not a true lavender, and that its finely cut grey leaves are rather disagreeably scented, somewhat like chamomile. She records it as a children's vermifuge, once esteemed as a stimulant, and as a moth deterrent among linen, and says a perfume oil is extracted from it. Culpeper places it under the dominion of Mercury.
