# Chinese Lantern

Alkekengi officinarum is the only species of the genus Alkekengi, in the nightshade family. It is native from southern Europe to South Asia and north-east Asia, and is called bladder cherry, Chinese lantern, Japanese-lantern, strawberry groundcherry, winter cherry, alchechengi berry or Klabuster cherry. It is easy to recognise by the large, bright orange to red papery calyx that covers its fruit like a paper lantern. It is a perennial herb with white flowers, widely grown as an ornamental, and it can be invasive because its roots spread. It was once placed in the genus Physalis. The page says it has been used in traditional medicine for about two millennia, with ancient Chinese books such as the Erya and the Shen Nong Ben Cao Jing recording it for fever, mental serenity and childbirth. It contains physalins, which showed antibacterial and leishmanicidal activity in vitro. In Japan the lantern-like calyces are offerings at the Bon Festival, and a market, hōzuki-ichi, is held each July.
