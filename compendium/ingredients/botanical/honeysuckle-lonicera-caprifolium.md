# Honeysuckle

Here honeysuckle means Lonicera caprifolium, the Italian honeysuckle or Italian woodbine, a vigorous deciduous climber of up to eight metres. Wikipedia says it is native to parts of Europe and naturalised in south-east Britain and north-eastern North America, and that its topmost leaves are perfoliate: the stem seems to grow through the leaf. It bears masses of very fragrant cream flowers tinged with pink in midsummer. Cunningham gives it for money, protection and psychic powers: ring green candles with the flowers to attract money, or rub the crushed fresh flowers on the forehead to heighten psychic powers. A plant growing near the home brings good luck and, over the door, keeps fevers away. Kynes records that in Scotland and Ireland it guarded a house against witches, and that Fenland girls were said to dream of lovers if the scent was in the house. Both books also cover another species, so those citations are left for the curator.
