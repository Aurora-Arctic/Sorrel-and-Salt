# Betel Leaf

Betel is an evergreen, dioecious vine of the pepper family, native to Southeast Asia, with glossy heart-shaped leaves and white catkins. It is grown for its leaves in South and Southeast Asia, from India to Papua New Guinea, on a tree or pole for support, in moist, well-drained, fertile soil. The leaves flavour the betel quid, in which they wrap areca nut with calcium hydroxide and, historically, catechu. In India and Sri Lanka a sheaf of betel leaves is traditionally offered as a mark of respect and auspicious beginnings, at weddings, at the New Year, and as thanks to physicians and astrologers. Red betel leaf is sometimes used as an antibacterial mouthwash. The quid, not the leaf alone, is the health hazard.
