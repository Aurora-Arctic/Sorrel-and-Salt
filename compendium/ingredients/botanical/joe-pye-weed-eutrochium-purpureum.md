# Joe-Pye Weed

Joe-Pye weed, or sweet Joe Pye weed, is a clump-forming herbaceous perennial of the daisy family, native to eastern and central North America. Wikipedia says it has thick purple stems with whorls of leaves, large loose domed clusters of purplish flowers in mid to late summer, and that many butterflies, moths and bees visit it. Kynes says the only folklore about it concerns its name, which may come from a Native American medicine man called Joe Pye or from the word jopi, possibly Algonquin for typhoid fever, since it was a fever remedy. Cunningham has leaves placed in the mouth when making love advances, and carried to be looked on with respect and favour. Kynes carries a piece of dried root for luck and makes a sachet of leaves and flowers for a bath before an interview. The AHPA handbook (Safety Classes 2a, 2b and 2c) says it is for external use only and not for use in pregnancy or lactation. Cunningham's heading names the genus as well as the species, so that citation is left for the curator.
