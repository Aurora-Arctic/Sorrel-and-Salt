# Western Hemlock

Tsuga heterophylla, the western hemlock or western hemlock-spruce, is the largest hemlock, a conifer in the pine family native to the northwest coast of North America, from the Kenai Peninsula in Alaska to northern Sonoma County in California. It is a main part of Pacific Northwest temperate rainforest and a source of timber, tannin and edible cambium. Native Alaskans of the southeast, including the Tlingit, scraped the cambium from removed bark and ate it fresh or dried and pressed it into bread. Some Native American tribes ate the inner bark as an emergency food and cooked the bark into medicinal extracts. The bark also gives a dark red dye. The Tlingit submerge boughs to collect herring eggs. The tender new needles are chewed or made into a tea rich in vitamin C.
