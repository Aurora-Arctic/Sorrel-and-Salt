# Sage-Leaved Germander

Sage-leaved germander, or wood sage, is a hairy perennial of the mint family, native to western Europe and Tunisia. Wikipedia says it grows 30 to 60 centimetres tall, with wrinkled, toothed leaves and one-sided spikes of pale green or yellowish flowers, and prefers sandy woodland soils and acid heaths. Grieve says the wrinkled, sage-like leaves give it the names Wood Sage and Sage Germander, that it tastes and smells like hops and is called Ambroise in Jersey, where it substituted for hops in beer, and that the herb is alterative, diuretic and astringent. She calls it an appetizer of the first order, and Culpeper says the herb is under Venus.
