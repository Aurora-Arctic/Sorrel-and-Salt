# Coca

Coca is the name for four cultivated plants in the family Erythroxylaceae, native to western South America, and the species Erythroxylum coca is one of them. The plant resembles a blackthorn bush, with oval, thin, opaque leaves marked by two curved lines either side of the midrib, small yellowish-white flowers and red berries. Its leaves contain cocaine, which acts as a mild stimulant when chewed or taken as tea. Chewing coca leaves dates back at least 8,000 years in South America, and across the Andes the leaves have been used for medicinal, nutritional, religious and social purposes. The Incas integrated coca deeply into labour, religion and trade. Leaves are offered to the apus, which are mountain spirits, to Inti, the sun, and to Pachamama, the earth, and are read in a form of divination. Chewing is most common in Indigenous communities across the central Andes.
