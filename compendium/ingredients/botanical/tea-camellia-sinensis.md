# Tea

Tea is the evergreen shrub of the family Theaceae whose leaves make white, yellow, green, oolong, dark and black tea. Wikipedia names two varieties, sinensis and assamica, and says Linnaeus first put it in the genus Thea before the genera were merged in 1818. Cunningham calls it masculine, with the Sun and fire, and lists riches, courage and strength. He has the leaves burned for future riches, added to money mixtures and made into talismans for courage and strength, and the infusion used as a base for lust drinks. His heading also names other Camellia species, so it is marked unresolved. The American Herbal Products Association has separate monographs for black and green tea, both printing Camellia sinensis, and records caffeine precautions, interactions and, for green tea extracts, cases of liver toxicity. Cunningham gives Black Tea, China Tea and Cha as other names. The hazards are set out in the safety notes.
