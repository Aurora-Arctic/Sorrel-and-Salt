# Mullein

Great mullein is a hairy biennial native to Europe, North Africa and Asia, which grows from a large rosette of leaves in its first year to a tall, pole-like stem, often two metres or more, ending in a dense spike of small yellow flowers. Wikipedia says all of it is covered in star-shaped hairs, thickest on the leaves, which look silvery. Kynes records that the Romans dipped the dried flower stalks in tallow for torches, that in Ireland carrying a piece protected against enchantment and witchcraft, and that the Anglo-Saxons used a stem as a charm against wild beasts and evil. Cunningham says it is worn when hiking in untamed areas to keep wild animals away, and that it instils courage. He adds that in India it is regarded as the most potent safeguard against evil spirits and magic, and that witches and magicians once used its downy leaves and stems as lamp wicks. Kynes uses the top of a stalk as a wand in banishing spells and holds a leaf between her palms to heighten awareness before divination. The AHPA handbook heads its monograph with the genus, so that citation is left for the curator.
