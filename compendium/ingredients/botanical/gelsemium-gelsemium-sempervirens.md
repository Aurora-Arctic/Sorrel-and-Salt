# Gelsemium

Gelsemium is a twining evergreen vine of the south-eastern United States and Mexico, reaching three to six metres on suitable support. Wikipedia describes glossy lanceolate leaves and clusters of yellow trumpet-shaped flowers, and notes it is not a true jasmine and is the state flower of South Carolina. Grieve says the drug of the pharmacopoeias is the dried rhizome and root, collected in autumn, bitter from its alkaloids, and that Parkinson grew the plant from seed sent by Tradescant from Virginia in 1640. She warns that the drug is a powerful spinal depressant and gives it a POISON heading. Wikipedia says every part contains toxic strychnine-related alkaloids, and that children who mistook the flower for honeysuckle have been poisoned.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve heads the page "Gelsemium nitidum (MICH.)" and lists "Gelsemium Sempervirens (Pers.)" among her synonyms.
