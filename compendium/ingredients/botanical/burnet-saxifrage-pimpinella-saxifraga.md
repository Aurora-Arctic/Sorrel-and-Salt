# Burnet Saxifrage

Burnet-saxifrage, also called solidstem burnet saxifrage or lesser burnet, is a member of the carrot family native to the British Isles, temperate Europe and western Asia. Wikipedia says it is neither a burnet nor a saxifrage, makes up much of the turf on some southern English chalk downs, and was once grown as fodder. Gerard, quoted there, commends it for the heart, plague and wounds. Cunningham lists it under Pimpernel (Pimpinella saxifraga, P. spp.), calling it masculine, with Mercury and air, and giving protection and health. He has it carried for protection and to keep people from deceiving you, and placed in the home to ward off illnesses and prevent accidents. Magical knife blades are rubbed with its juice to purify and empower them. His heading reaches beyond one species and gives two folk names that belong to scarlet pimpernel, so the citation is an open question for a curator.

#### Questions for the Curator

<!-- audit:cunningham-herbs:Pimpernel -->

Does cunningham-herbs under "Pimpernel", cited on the entry and both its variants, describe Pimpinella saxifraga alone? The heading reads "Pimpinella saxifraga, P. spp.", and two of its folk names, Poorman’s Weatherglass and Shepherd’s Weatherglass, are names Kynes gives to scarlet pimpernel (Anagallis arvensis), a different plant. The two weatherglass names are left off this file. Settle it as `stated` or `resolved` from the source, or replace the citation.
