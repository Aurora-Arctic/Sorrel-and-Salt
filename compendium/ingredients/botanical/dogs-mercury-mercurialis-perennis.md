# Dog's Mercury

Dog's mercury is a downy, perennial woodland herb of the spurge family, common in woods and shady places across Europe and Russian Asia, and spreading by creeping rootstocks. Grieve describes stems about a foot high, with pairs of roughish leaves and small green flowers, male and female on separate plants, flowering from the end of March to the middle of May. Wikipedia says it favours alkaline soils, often carpets ancient woodland, and is highly poisonous. Grieve says it is extremely acrid, has a disagreeable odour, and has proved fatal to sheep. The old herbals once praised it, but it has been abandoned as a dangerous remedy for internal use. The name Dog's Mercury is thought to mean false or inferior, set against the edible Good King Henry and Annual Mercury.
