# Aspen

Quaking aspen, Populus tremuloides, is a deciduous tree of the cooler parts of North America and the most widely distributed tree on that continent, found from Canada south to central Mexico. It grows tall and fast, with smooth, pale bark scarred with black, and nearly round leaves on flattened stalks, which twist in the wind: the habit behind its names quaking and trembling aspen. In autumn the leaves turn gold or yellow. A grove often rises from one shared root system, every trunk a clone of the rest, and one such colony, Pando, is thought to be the heaviest and oldest living organism. Its bitter bark was taken against fevers in place of quinine and Peruvian bark, and Grieve judged it probably to share the salicin and populin of the European aspen's. In magic Cunningham has it guard against theft, worked into spells or planted in a garden or field, and a leaf held under the tongue is said to make the speaker eloquent; under the name poplar he has its buds and leaves carried to draw money.
