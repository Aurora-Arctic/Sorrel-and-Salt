# Safflower

Safflower, Carthamus tinctorius, also called false saffron, is a highly branched, thistle-like annual herb of the daisy family and one of the world's oldest crops. Plants are 30 to 150 centimetres tall, with globular flower heads of yellow, orange or red flowers and 15 to 20 seeds per head, and a deep taproot suited to arid places with seasonal rain. Today it is grown mainly for vegetable oil from the seeds, but it was long used to colour and flavour food, in medicines and to make the red dye carthamin and yellow dyes. The petals contain one red and two yellow dyes, and dried flowers are a natural dye source for textiles. The flowers are occasionally used in cooking as a cheaper substitute for saffron, and the dried petals make a herbal tea. Dyes from safflower were identified in ancient Egyptian textiles, and garlands of safflower were found in the tomb of Tutankhamun.
