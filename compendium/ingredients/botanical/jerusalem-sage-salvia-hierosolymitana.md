# Jerusalem Sage

Salvia hierosolymitana, Jerusalem sage, is a herbaceous perennial native to the eastern Mediterranean, with populations in Cyprus, Israel, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria and the West Bank. It grows in open fields, rocky soils and among low native shrubs. The evergreen leaves are mid-green and ovate, with scalloped margins, and the flowers are wine-red with a white, spotted lower lip. Unlike many salvias, it has no odour when the leaves are crushed. In Jordanian and Palestinian cooking the leaves are stuffed with meat and rice and cooked with lamb riblets. The page also says that in Jordan and Palestine it is used in folk herbal medicine, and that the seeds are used to prepare a remedy for skin cancer. The same page says there is no known medicinal use of the plant.
