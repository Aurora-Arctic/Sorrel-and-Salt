# Bryony

Bryonia is a genus of tendril-climbing herbs of the gourd family, native to western Eurasia and nearby regions, with palmately lobed leaves and smooth, globular berries. Bryony is its best-known common name. Kynes writes about it under one Bryony heading, which covers red and white bryony of this genus and also black bryony, which is in another genus, so her details are left for the curator to settle. She says white and red bryony were tied to witchcraft in England and Europe and were thought unlucky indoors, and that medieval herbalists sold their roots as substitutes for mandrake. A necklace of white bryony root beads was worn against the evil eye, and in Cambridgeshire a dried root kept with money in an old stocking increased savings. Her spells use a pouch of root with financial papers, a polished root worn as a talisman, and a root buried under the front steps for protection. Cunningham has no bryony entry. Kynes and Wikipedia record hazards, set out in the safety notes.
