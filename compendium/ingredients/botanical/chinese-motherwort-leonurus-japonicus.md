# Chinese Motherwort

Leonurus japonicus, called oriental motherwort or Chinese motherwort, is a herbaceous flowering plant of the mint family, native to Asia from Korea and Japan through China to Cambodia. It is an annual or biennial that grows from a taproot, with upright stems 30 to 120 centimetres tall and white to reddish-purple flowers from June to September. It has escaped cultivation and become naturalised in North and South America, Europe and Africa. It is one of the 50 fundamental herbs of traditional Chinese medicine, where it is called yìmǔcǎo, literally the beneficial herb for mothers. It is used for menstrual and childbirth disorders attributed to blood stasis, such as painful or absent periods. Compounds reported from it include guanosine, rutin, syringic acid and stigmasterol. The Wikipedia page heads the plant under Leonurus japonicus but gives Paraleonurus japonicus as the current name, which is why the formal name is queried.
