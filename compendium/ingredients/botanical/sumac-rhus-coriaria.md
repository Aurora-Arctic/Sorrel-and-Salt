# Sumac

Rhus coriaria, called Sicilian sumac, tanner's sumach or elm-leaved sumach, is a deciduous shrub to small tree in the cashew family, Anacardiaceae, native to southern Europe and western Asia, including the eastern Mediterranean, Crimea, the Caucasus and northern Iran. It is hardy and drought-tolerant, prefers full sun, and grows in any deep, well-drained soil. The dried, crushed fruit has a sour taste and is a popular spice in the Middle East, especially in the mixture za'atar; immature fruits and seeds are also eaten. The page says the ancient Romans used the sour fruit in vinaigrettes as lemons are used now. The leaves and bark were used in leather tanning and contain tannic acid, dyes in red, yellow, black and brown can be made from different parts, and oil from the seeds can make candles.
