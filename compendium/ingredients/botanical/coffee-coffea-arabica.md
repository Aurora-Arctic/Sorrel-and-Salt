# Coffee

Coffea arabica, also known as Arabica coffee, is a species of flowering plant in the coffee and madder family. It is believed to be the first species of coffee to be cultivated and is the dominant cultivar, about 60% of global production. Its natural populations are restricted to the forests of South Ethiopia, South Sudan and Yemen. Wild plants have an open branching system, with glossy dark green leaves that have prominent veins and wavy margins. The flowers are white and grow in clusters, and the fruit is a drupe, commonly called a cherry, that ripens bright red to purple and usually holds two seeds, which become coffee beans. Linnaeus published the name in 1753. The species is an allotetraploid, the result of a hybridisation between Coffea canephora and Coffea eugenioides. Arabica coffee grows at high elevations with moderate temperatures and well-drained soils, and in places such as Hawaii and Queensland it has become an invasive weed.
