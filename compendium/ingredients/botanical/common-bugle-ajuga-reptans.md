# Common Bugle

Common bugle is a creeping perennial of the mint family, native to Europe and abundant in damp, shady pastures and woods. Wikipedia describes a sprawling herb 10 to 35 cm tall with square stems, runners spreading over the ground, and whorled spikes of blue flowers with dark veins on the lower lip. Grieve says it and self-heal stood in equal esteem for many centuries as wound herbs, and that the name may be a corruption of the generic name Ajuga. She names the whole herb, gathered in May and early June and dried, as the part used medicinally. Culpeper gives it to Venus and recommends a syrup, ointment and plaster of it. Wikipedia adds the old name St. Lawrence plant.

#### Questions for the Curator

Culpeper prints only the English heading "BUGLE" and no Latin name, so his page is cited with `nameBasis: unresolved`. The roster ties it to _Ajuga reptans_; no authority I read maps Culpeper's heading to that formal name. He gives the planet: "This herb belongs to Dame Venus". His page lists Brown Bugle, Middle Comfrey and Sicklewort as names and does not state a species. Do you accept Culpeper's "BUGLE" as _Ajuga reptans_? My recommendation: accept it only if you are content to rest the Culpeper-sourced fields on the common name; otherwise drop the Culpeper citation and the fields that rest on it.

Grieve quotes the herb as lowering the pulse like digitalis and as "one of the mildest and best narcotics in the world". I have recorded this as a caution and set no toxic flag, because no source I read calls the plant poisonous or toxic. Is a flag wanted? My recommendation: no flag.
