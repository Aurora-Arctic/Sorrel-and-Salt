# Bush Basil

Bush basil is a low, bushy basil, seldom above six inches tall and much smaller than sweet basil. Its ovate leaves have whole edges and its white flowers sit in whorls towards the tops of the branches, seldom followed by ripe seeds in England. Grieve says there are two varieties, one with black-purple leaves and one with variable leaves, and that it came from India and was introduced to Britain in 1573. It may occasionally survive an English winter, though sweet basil never does. The leafy tops are used like sweet basil, for seasoning and in salads. Wikipedia describes the plant as a perennial, called manjerico in Portuguese, and says it is sold in small pots in Portugal each June for the Midsummer celebrations.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve heads this page "Basil, Bush" and prints the botanical line as "Ocymum minumum", a misspelling of _Ocimum minimum_, so the Grieve citation is `unresolved`. No authority I read maps the misspelling.

GBIF (evidence only, not an authority): https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Ocimum%20minimum gives _Ocimum minimum_ L., SYNONYM, accepted key 8254455, which is _Ocimum basilicum_ var. _minimum_ (L.) Alef., ACCEPTED. So GBIF treats the roster name as a synonym of a variety of sweet basil.

Do you keep _Ocimum minimum_ as its own entry? My recommendation: keep it only if Wikipedia's separate page is enough for you; otherwise fold the row into sweet basil.
