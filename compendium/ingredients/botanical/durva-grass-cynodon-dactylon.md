# Durva Grass

Durva grass, widely called Bermuda grass, is a warm-season grass of warm and tropical climates worldwide, native to Europe, Africa, Australia and much of Asia. It creeps along the ground by stolons and rhizomes, forming a dense mat, with grey-green blades 2 to 15 centimetres long and a deep root system. It can be an aggressive invasive weed. In Hinduism it is important in the worship of Ganesha, and the festival Durga Ashtami is dedicated to it. Known as arugampull in Tamil, garikehullu in Kannada and karuka in Malayalam, it is one of the Dashapushpam, the ten sacred flowers of Kerala. In Nepal, where it is called dubo, a garland of it is worn by the bride and groom at a Hindu wedding.
