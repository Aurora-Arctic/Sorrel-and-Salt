# Parsley Piert

Parsley piert is a tiny annual of arable fields and waste ground in dry sites, found across Europe and North Africa. Wikipedia says it stands 2 to 20 centimetres high, with fan-shaped, three-lobed leaves and minute green flowers in clusters, with no petals, held in cups formed by the leaf stipules. It says the name has nothing to do with parsley but comes from the French perce-pierre, meaning stone-piercer. Grieve records it as a diuretic and demulcent, chiefly for gravel, stone, dropsy and complaints of the bladder and kidneys, and quotes Culpeper recommending it as a pickled salad herb, as the pickled samphire was kept for the winter.

#### Questions for the Curator

Both cited sources print _Alchemilla arvensis_ (Grieve: "Alchemilla arvensis (SCOP.)"; the Wikipedia revision is titled so), so both are `stated`. The Wikipedia page lists _Aphanes arvensis_ L. as a synonym, but GBIF (evidence only, not an authority): https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Aphanes%20arvensis gives Aphanes arvensis L., ACCEPTED. GBIF matching "Alchemilla arvensis" returns only the family Rosaceae. Which name do you want as the identity? My recommendation: keep _Alchemilla arvensis_ because both cited sources print it, and decide after checking POWO.
