# Purslane

Common purslane, also called little hogweed or pursley, is a succulent plant of the Portulacaceae family whose stems mostly lie along the ground. Wikipedia says all parts of the plant are edible raw or cooked, and that the leaves are very high in oxalates. Cunningham calls it feminine, with the Moon and water, and lists sleep, love and protection. He says it laid on the bed keeps nightmares away, carried it draws love and keeps evil afar, and soldiers carried it for protection in battle. Kynes adds Cancer, Libra and Pisces, and says medieval Europeans held it an aphrodisiac and a protection against nightmares and evil spirits. She has a sachet of seeds hung on the bedpost and dried leaves burned to purify outdoor ritual space. Her heading covers the common and golden purslane, so her citation is left for the curator. The American Herbal Products Association records cautions, set out in the safety notes.
