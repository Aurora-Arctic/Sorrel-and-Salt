# Burdock

Greater burdock is a Eurasian biennial of the daisy family, with large wavy-edged heart-shaped leaves and a fleshy taproot that is cultivated as a vegetable. Wikipedia says it is also an invasive weed of nitrogen-rich soils in North America and Australia. Cunningham calls it feminine, with Venus and water, and lists protection and healing. He has it cast around the home to ward off negativity, and the roots, gathered in the waning Moon, dried, cut and strung on red thread, worn against evil. Kynes covers greater and lesser burdock together, and adds Libra, Sagittarius and Taurus, faeries and pixies. She says the Burry Man of an English village tradition wore a costume of burrs to catch evil, and that burdock leaves were used in Albanian exorcism rites. She has burrs rolled between the hands, broken up and tossed to the wind to remove something unwanted. Her heading pools two species, so her citation is an open question for a curator.
