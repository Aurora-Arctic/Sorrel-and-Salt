# Kou

Cordia subcordata is a species of flowering tree in the family Cordiaceae. It grows in eastern Africa, South Asia, Southeast Asia, northern Australia and the Pacific Islands, and is known by many names, including kou, beach cordia, sea trumpet and kerosene wood. It is a coastal tree, and the special characteristics of its fruit allowed successful dispersal across the ocean. Subfossil evidence from Makauwahi Cave on Kauai shows it was abundant in Hawaiian lowland forests before humans arrived. The seeds are edible and have been eaten during famine. In ancient Hawaii kou wood was used to make umeke, bowls, utensils and large calabashes for storing and fermenting poi, because it did not impart a foul taste to food, and to carve kiʻi, images. The flowers were used to make lei, and a dye for kapa cloth and fishing lines came from the leaves. Fijians obtain fibre for baskets and garlands from the inner bark. Javanese folklore considers the tree to contain spiritual power.
