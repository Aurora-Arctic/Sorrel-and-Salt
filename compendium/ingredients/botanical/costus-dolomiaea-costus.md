# Costus

Dolomiaea costus, formerly Saussurea costus, is a thistle native to South Asia and China, called costus, Indian costus, kuth or putchuk. Costus is the root, and the essential oil from it has been used in traditional medicine and in perfumes since ancient times. It is a perennial with long lyrate leaves and heads of purple florets, grown as a medicinal crop mainly in Himachal Pradesh and Kashmir. Its name is kuṣṭha in Sanskrit, kust or qust in Arabic and Persian, and kosht in Hebrew. The rabbinic writings name the root kosht and say it was used in the ketoret, the consecrated incense of the Tabernacle and the Temples. A hadith records Muhammad recommending Indian incense, which is sniffed for throat trouble. Pliny described it as having a burning taste and an exquisite scent. In Ayurveda, kushta is a plant from heavenly sources, linked in the Atharvaveda to fever. The root oil smells of violets at first, then of goat.
