# Horse Chestnut

The horse chestnut is a large deciduous tree of the soapberry family, native to the Balkans and planted widely in Europe and North America. Wikipedia lists its other names as European horsechestnut, buckeye and conker tree, and says it is not related to the sweet chestnut. The glossy brown seeds are called conkers, and in Britain and Ireland they are used in the children's game of conkers. Cunningham marks it Poison and has it carried to ward off rheumatism, backache, arthritis and chills, and three carried to guard against giddiness. A buckeye wrapped in a dollar bill and carried in a sachet attracts money. Kynes treats it under the shared heading Chestnut, with the sweet chestnut. She repeats an English legend that its flowers light up like candles so faeries can find their way home, and says a horse chestnut in the pocket was a good luck charm. Both books also cover other species, so those citations are left for the curator.
