# Chaste Tree

Vitex agnus-castus, called the chaste tree, vitex, chasteberry, Abraham's balm, lilac chastetree or monk's pepper, is a plant of the Mediterranean region. It is one of the few temperate-zone species of Vitex, a genus that is otherwise tropical and subtropical. Theophrastus mentioned the shrub several times as ágnos. It has long been believed to be an anaphrodisiac, which gave it the name chaste tree, but the page says this effect remains unproven. The shrub was used in religious rituals in ancient Greece, among the Philistines in modern Palestine and elsewhere in the Levant. The name Vitex comes from Pliny the Elder and from a Latin verb meaning to weave, for its use in basketry, and the page says the small tree was considered sacred to the virginal goddess Hestia or Vesta. It is widely grown for its aromatic foliage and for spikes of lavender flowers in late summer, in full sun and well-drained soil.
