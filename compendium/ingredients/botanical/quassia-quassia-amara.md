# Quassia

Quassia, also called Suriname quassia, bitter-ash or bitterwood, is a small evergreen shrub or tree of the Simaroubaceae family from the tropics of the Americas. Wikipedia says it was named after Graman Quassi, a Surinamese freedman and healer who showed Europeans its use against fevers, and that it is used as an insecticide, in traditional medicine and as a food additive. Cunningham lists only love: quassia is used in love mixtures, both to draw and to keep love, and the powdered wood goes into incense bases. Kynes adds earth and Saturn, and says hoodoo uses it in mojo bags to draw money and in spells for control over a person. She has initials written on bark chips for a love charm, and powdered bark tossed outside to release something. Both Cunningham's heading and Kynes's cover Jamaica quassia too, so their citations are left for the curator. Kynes and the American Herbal Products Association record cautions, set out in the safety notes.
