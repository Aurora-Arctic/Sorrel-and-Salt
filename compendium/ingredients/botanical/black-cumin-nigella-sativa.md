# Black Cumin

Nigella sativa is an annual flowering plant of the buttercup family, native to western Asia, from the Levant, Cyprus, Turkey, Iran and Iraq, and to Bulgaria and Romania in eastern Europe. It is grown or naturalised in much of Europe, North Africa and eastward to Myanmar. The name Nigella comes from the Latin for black, for the colour of its seeds. It stands a short way off the ground, with finely divided leaves and pale blue and white flowers of five to ten petals. The fruit is a large inflated capsule of three to seven joined follicles that hold many seeds. The seeds are a spice in many cuisines. They are dry-roasted for curries, vegetables and pulses, scattered on some breads such as nan-e barbari, used in the Bengali mix panch phoron, ground into Palestinian qizha paste, and used in the braided Lebanese cheese majdouleh.
