# Spikenard

Spikenard here means the Himalayan plant Nardostachys jatamansi, the only species in its genus, a perennial 10 to 50 centimetres tall with pink bell-shaped flowers. Wikipedia says it grows in a belt through Kumaon, Nepal, Sikkim and Bhutan, and that its rhizome is distilled into a thick, intensely aromatic amber oil used since ancient times as a perfume, an incense, a sedative and a herbal medicine. It is also called nard, nardin and muskroot, and is considered endangered through overharvesting, overgrazing and habitat loss. Kynes has one spikenard entry for three plants, the American Aralia racemosa, ploughman's Inula conyza and true Nardostachys jatamansi, with Venus and water and powers of harmony, loyalty and fidelity, love and purification. She warns that the true plant is critically endangered. Cunningham's spikenard entry names Inula conyza and Aralia racemosa and not Nardostachys, so it is not cited. The American Herbal Products Association records hazards, set out in the safety notes.
