# Bristly Hollyhock

Alcea setosa, the bristly hollyhock, is an ornamental plant of the mallow family, native to the Levant and neighbouring lands from Crete and Turkey through Lebanon and Syria to Israel and Jordan. The part above ground dies back in summer. In winter it forms a rosette of leaves, and a flowering stem then rises from it. The numerous large flowers are pink, darker in the Galilee and Carmel and paler in Gilboa and Samaria, and range from dark pink to white on Mount Lebanon. The whole plant is bristly, which gives it its name, and it flowers from April to June. In folk medicine the liquid of the plant treats injuries, burns, coughs and inflammation. The flower buds are edible raw or cooked and are thought to ease airway illness. In Lebanese villages the flowers are dried in the sun, mixed with other herbs and wild flowers, and served as a tea.
