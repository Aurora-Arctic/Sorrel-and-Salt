# Stinking Gladwyn

Stinking gladwyn is an evergreen iris native to western Europe and north Africa, found in woods, hedgebanks and shady places in the south and west of England. Wikipedia says it forms tufts of dark green leaves, bears dull leaden-blue or buff-yellow flowers in June and July, and holds scarlet seeds in capsules that last through winter; it is a garden plant that has won the Royal Horticultural Society's Award of Garden Merit. Grieve says bruised leaves smell of hot roast beef at a distance and are disagreeable close to, and that the root is antispasmodic, cathartic and anodyne. Culpeper says the plant is under the dominion of Saturn and describes it as one kind of flower-de-luce.
