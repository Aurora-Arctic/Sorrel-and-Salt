# Garden Arrach

Garden arrach, or orache, is a hardy annual of Europe and Asia with an erect, branching stem two to six feet tall, widely naturalised in Canada, the United States, Australia and New Zealand. Wikipedia says it is still grown as a warm-weather alternative to spinach, and describes thin, slightly acidic, oblong leaves in red, white and green varieties. Grieve says it came to England from Tartary in 1548 and is little grown there now, but is eaten in France under the name arroche. It is a substitute for spinach, though far inferior to it, and corrects the acidity of sorrel. She records that heated with vinegar, honey and salt it was applied for gout, and that the seeds excite vomiting. Culpeper puts it under the Moon, cold and moist.
