# Ox-Tongue

Anchusa strigosa, strigose bugloss or prickly alkanet, is a perennial herb in the borage family, Boraginaceae, endemic to the eastern Mediterranean, in Greece, Cyprus, Turkey, Lebanon, Israel, Jordan and Iran. It forms a large rosette of leaves in winter, and in late spring a few flowering stems rise from the base to about half a metre or more. The leaves are rough as the tongue of a ruminant, which gave it its Arabic name lisān eth-thawr and its Hebrew name leshon-par, both reflecting the Greek bouglossos, meaning ox-tongued. The flower is blue, but a white-flowered variety is gradually displacing it. It grows in heavy soils in semi-steppe shrubland and Mediterranean woodland, flowers between March and May in Israel, and in Ottoman Palestine honey bees harvested its pollen.
