# Calamint

Calamint is a hardy, bushy perennial of the mint family, rarely more than a foot high, with square stems and opposite pairs of leaves that are downy with soft hairs like the stems. Grieve says the small, light purple flowers open in July and August and droop gracefully before opening, and that the plant grows by waysides and in hedges, especially in dry places. The whole herb smells sweet and aromatic, and an infusion of the dried leaves was a cordial tea. Wikipedia describes lesser calamint, with grey-green oregano-like leaves smelling like a cross between mint and oregano, as a culinary herb of Italian and Corsican cooking, used in Rome with artichokes, and as a folk medicine for cramps and the digestive tract.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve heads this page "Calamintha officinalis (MOENCH)". Wikipedia (the cited revision) lists only _Calamintha officinalis_ var. _nepeta_ (L.) Rchb. & Rchb.f. among the synonyms of _Clinopodium nepeta_, not _Calamintha officinalis_ Moench itself, so the Grieve citation is `unresolved`. GBIF (evidence only, not an authority): https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Calamintha%20officinalis gives _Calamintha officinalis_ Moench, SYNONYM, accepted key 7757431, which is _Clinopodium nepeta_ subsp. _spruneri_ (Boiss.) Bartolucci & F.Conti, ACCEPTED. So GBIF puts Moench's name under a subspecies of the roster species, not the species itself..

Culpeper prints only the English heading "CALAMINT, OR MOUNTAIN-MINT" and no Latin name, so his page is cited with `nameBasis: unresolved`. The roster ties it to _Clinopodium nepeta_; no authority I read maps Culpeper's heading to that formal name. Culpeper gives the planet: "an herb of Mercury".
