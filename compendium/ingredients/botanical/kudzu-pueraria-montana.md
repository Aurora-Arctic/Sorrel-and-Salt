# Kudzu

Kudzu, also called Japanese arrowroot or Chinese arrowroot, is a group of climbing, coiling deciduous perennial vines of the pea family, genus Pueraria, native to much of East Asia, Southeast Asia and some Pacific islands. The name comes from the Japanese kuzu, the name of the East Asian arrowroot, Pueraria montana var. lobata. Where it is naturalised, mainly in North America, it is invasive and a noxious weed, smothering other plants. The roots hold starch, used traditionally as a food in East and Southeast Asia and made in Korea into chikcha, arrowroot tea. Kudzu powder makes the Japanese tea kuzuyu, and kakkonto, a herbal drink from traditional Chinese medicine, is named for the root. It contains isoflavones, chiefly puerarin. In traditional Chinese medicine, where it is called gě gēn, it is one of the 50 fundamental herbs.

#### Questions for the Curator

- The Wikipedia page "Kudzu" describes a group of Pueraria vines: "The name kudzu describes one or more species in the genus Pueraria that are closely related, and some of them are considered to be varieties rather than full species." It prints Pueraria montana only as the species of three varieties (var. chinensis, var. lobata and var. thomsonii) and names var. lobata as the source of the word kuzu. It does not give a single formal name for kudzu. Should the entry carry Pueraria montana, or Pueraria montana var. lobata, or be left at genus level? I recommend Pueraria montana until a plant authority such as POWO is checked by someone who can reach it.
