# Melilot

Melilot, or yellow sweet clover, is a legume of Eurasia, introduced elsewhere as a forage crop. Wikipedia says it is an annual or biennial 120 to 180 cm tall, with leaves of three leaflets, yellow flowers in spring and summer, pods usually holding one seed, large taproots and a characteristic sweet smell. Grieve says the melilots were once grouped with the clovers, are not truly native to Britain, and were widely grown for fodder. She says the dried herb is intensely fragrant, with coumarin the only important constituent, and that it was formerly much esteemed as an emollient and digestive, used externally as a plaster for abdominal and rheumatic pains. She records it scenting snuff and linen and flavouring Swiss Schabzieger cheese.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve's page is headed with three species, "Melilotus officinalis (LINN.), Melilotus alba (DESV.), Melilotus arvensis (LAMK.)", and its text treats them together. I cite it as `stated` for _Melilotus officinalis_ because the exact name is printed, but the uses and constituents apply to the group. I left out her folk names "White Melilot" and "Corn Melilot", which belong to the other two species. Do you accept the page as a source for this one species? My recommendation: yes.
