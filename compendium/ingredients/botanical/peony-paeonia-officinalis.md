# Peony

The common peony, or garden peony, is a flowering plant native mainly to the mountains of southern Europe, grown first for medicine and then as an ornamental. Cunningham calls it masculine, with the Sun and fire, and gives it the powers of protection and exorcism. He says it is worn to guard the wearer, kept in the home against evil spirits, planted in the garden against evil and storms, and that seeds or roots hung around a child's neck guard the child. Kynes adds the sign Leo, Jupiter, the deity Pan, and uses against nightmares, for peace and for release. She cautions that the roots, flowers and seeds are toxic if ingested, and the American Herbal Products Association records contact dermatitis in people who work with the plant. Kynes puts the common and wild peony under one heading, so her citation is left for the curator.
