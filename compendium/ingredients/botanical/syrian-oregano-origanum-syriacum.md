# Syrian Oregano

Origanum syriacum, Syrian oregano, bible hyssop or Lebanese oregano, is an aromatic perennial herb in the mint family, Lamiaceae, growing to about a metre, with small white or pale pink flowers pollinated by bees. It is native to the Middle East, and a very rare subspecies grows in Sinai. It is a preferred main ingredient of the spice mixture za'atar, a blend of dried herbs, sesame and sumac used for flavouring and garnish; in a typical Lebanese blend the dried leaves of O. syriacum and Thymbra spicata are added to ground sumac fruit, toasted sesame and salt, and the mix is spread with olive oil on flatbreads. The page says the plant was used in ceremonies in the Temple in Jerusalem, such as sprinkling the purification waters. In Lebanon the dried herb is used against pharyngitis, stomach aches and cough. It is gathered wild between May and August and has recently entered cultivation.
