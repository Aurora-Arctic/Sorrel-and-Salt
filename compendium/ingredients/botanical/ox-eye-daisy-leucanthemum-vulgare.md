# Ox-Eye Daisy

The ox-eye daisy is a perennial herb with a creeping underground rhizome, native to Europe and the temperate regions of Asia and introduced to North America, Australia and New Zealand. Wikipedia says each flower head has fifteen to forty white ray florets around yellow disc florets, and that it flowers from May to October. Wikipedia also gives dog daisy and marguerite as other names. Cunningham and Kynes both treat it together with the European daisy under one heading, so both citations are left for the curator. Kynes gives the element water, the signs Cancer and Taurus, and the deities Aphrodite, Artemis, Freya and Thor, and says to use the ox-eye on an esbat altar, as it is associated with Artemis and the Moon. Her powers include divination, dream work, fertility, healing, love, protection and strength. Cunningham marks the daisy Poison, which in his key means never to be used internally or on the skin.
