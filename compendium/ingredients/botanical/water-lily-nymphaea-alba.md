# Water Lily

Water Lily here means the white water lily, an aquatic plant of ponds, ditches, lakes and canals with still or slow water, native to North Africa, Europe and temperate Asia. Its round dark green leaves and white flowers float on the surface, and the flowers open by day in summer. Kynes names it white lotus and records that the ancient Egyptians called it the bride of the Nile and set fresh flowers in graves as a sign of the pharaohs' spiritual power. In India it stood for the moon and was linked to Shiva, and in German lore water faeries called nixies hid beneath it. Scottish legend has faeries using water lilies to strengthen spells. Kynes treats several water lilies under one heading, so the entry is flagged for the curator to settle. Wikipedia gives the botanical description and the common names.
