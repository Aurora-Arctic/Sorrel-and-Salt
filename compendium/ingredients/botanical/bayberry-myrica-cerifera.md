# Bayberry

Bayberry, or southern wax myrtle, is an evergreen shrub or small tree, up to 14 metres in Wikipedia's account, though Grieve gives three to eight feet for the plants in the northern sand-belt. It is native to eastern North America, Central America and the Caribbean and grows in thickets near swamps and marshes, on dunes and in pine barrens. The leathery leaves are dotted with glands and smell sweet when rubbed. Male and female flowers are borne in catkins on different plants, and the female ones become small round fruits coated in a greenish-white wax. Boiled off, the wax makes the fragrant, long-burning bayberry candle. Grieve names the dried bark of the root as the official part, and describes its taste as astringent, bitter and very acrid.

#### Questions for the Curator

AHPA lists the species under the name _Morella cerifera_ (L.) Sm. and prints "syn: Myrica cerifera L."; Wikipedia (the cited revision) and Grieve both use _Myrica cerifera_. GBIF (evidence only, not an authority): https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Morella%20cerifera gives _Morella cerifera_ (L.) Small, ACCEPTED, so GBIF favours the AHPA name. The two names are taken as one species; which one does the compendium use? My recommendation: keep _Myrica cerifera_ as the roster has it, since two of the three sources I cite print it.
