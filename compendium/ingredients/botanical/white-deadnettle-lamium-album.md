# White Deadnettle

White deadnettle, usually written white dead-nettle, is a flowering plant of the mint family, native across Europe and Asia and found in open grassland and woodland, generally on moist fertile soils. Its leaves look like those of the stinging nettle but do not sting. Kynes lists the folk names bee nettle, Adam and Eve, archangel, fairy boots and fairy feet. The dark ends of the four stamens under the flower's hood look like tiny shoes; English legend says they were Cinderella's slippers, and that faeries kept their shoes there. Held upside down the flower can look like two people in bed, so it was regarded as lucky for love. Kynes covers the white and purple deadnettles under one heading, so the entry is flagged for the curator.
