# Cascara Amarga

Cascara amarga, Spanish for bitter bark, is a tree of Mexico, Central America and the Greater Antilles, with male and female flowers on separate trees. Grieve describes alternate, spreading branches, black furrowed bark on old trees, smooth grey bark on young ones, and leaves of several nearly elliptical leaflets, deep green above and whitish below. The bark is very bitter without being unpleasant, yellow within when fresh and paler when dry, and she links it to the drug sold as Simaruba bark. It was brought from Guiana in 1713 as a remedy for dysentery, and Grieve calls it a bitter tonic and purgative, an emetic in large doses. Wikipedia credits an enslaved woman called Majoe with using the plant for yaws.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve prints "Picramnia antidesma (S. W.)", which matches the roster name, but in her Constituents paragraph she says the drug "belongs to an undetermined species of picrammia". The same page also calls the bark "Simaruba bark", which other sources give to a different genus. Is this the right species for the entry? My recommendation: keep the row with this question open, and let a curator who can read a pharmacopoeia settle it.
