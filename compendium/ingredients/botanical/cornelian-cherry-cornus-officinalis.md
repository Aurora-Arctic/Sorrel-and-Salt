# Cornelian Cherry

The cornelian cherry, Cornus officinalis, is known in Chinese as zhuyu. Wikipedia's page on the Double Ninth Festival names it as the plant worn on that holiday, which falls on the ninth day of the ninth month of the Chinese calendar. Nine is a yang number in the I Ching, so the double nine carries extra yang and is thought an auspicious date, and the festival is also called the Double Yang Festival. Customs include climbing a mountain, drinking chrysanthemum liquor and wearing the zhuyu plant. The source says chrysanthemum and zhuyu are both considered to have cleansing qualities and are used on other occasions to air out houses and cure illnesses. Some families also visit ancestral graves, and Chongyang cake is eaten. By legend, the customs began when a Han-dynasty man advised his disciple to climb a mountain with chrysanthemum wine on that day, and so saved his family from a death that struck their livestock.
