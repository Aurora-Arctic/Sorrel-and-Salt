# Holly

Holly, here the English or European holly, is an evergreen tree or shrub native to western and southern Europe, north-west Africa and south-west Asia. Its glossy, prickly leaves and bright red berries, which only the female plant bears, are long associated with Christmas and with the Roman festival of Saturnalia. Cunningham calls it a protective herb above all: it guards against lightning, poison and evil spirits, and planted around a home it shields the household from sorcerers. Holly water is sprinkled on newborn babies, and men carry holly for luck. After midnight on a Friday, nine smooth leaves wrapped in white cloth with nine knots and put under the pillow are said to make dreams come true. Kynes records that Romans decorated their homes with it at Saturnalia, and that in the Middle Ages branches were placed on houses and barns against lightning and witchcraft. In the Scottish Highlands it hung over the cowshed door, and in Ireland it was a faery tree. A flaming holly branch was carried through towns in north-west England on Twelfth Night.
