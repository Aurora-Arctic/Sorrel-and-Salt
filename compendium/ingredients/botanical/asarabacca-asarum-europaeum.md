# Asarabacca

Asarabacca is a creeping evergreen perennial of temperate Europe, and the only British member of the birthwort family, though possibly not native there. A very short fleshy stem carries two large, dark green, glossy, kidney-shaped leaves, and a single dull purplish-green flower hangs hidden beneath them in May. Its roots smell of ginger, but it is not closely related to the culinary ginger. It grows in woods, is rare in Britain, and is also grown in gardens. Grieve gives the root and herb as the parts used; the dried leaves were powdered into snuffs for headaches and weak eyes, and the herb was taken to clear mucus from the airways. Culpeper placed it under Mars and found it strong enough to provoke vomiting and purging.
