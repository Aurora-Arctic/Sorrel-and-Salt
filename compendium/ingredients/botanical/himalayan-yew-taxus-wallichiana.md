# Himalayan Yew

The Himalayan yew is a medium-sized evergreen coniferous tree growing to 10 metres, native to the Himalaya and parts of Southeast Asia, and sometimes treated as a subspecies of Taxus baccata. Its shoots are green at first and brown after three or four years, and its thin, flat, slightly sickle-shaped leaves are spiralled on the shoots. It grows in montane to submontane forests between 900 and 3,700 metres. The page says it is used in Ayurveda and Tibetan medicine, is a source of the chemical precursors to the anticancer drug paclitaxel, and is used for tea by the Bhotiya community in the Garhwal Himalaya, who call the stem bark thuner. It is also used as fuelwood. The page says heavy exploitation of leaves and bark has caused declines of up to 90 per cent in India and Nepal.
