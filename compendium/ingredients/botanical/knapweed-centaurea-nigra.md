# Knapweed

Black knapweed, or common or lesser knapweed, is an upright herbaceous perennial of the daisy family, native to central Europe, which grows to about 1 m. Wikipedia says it forms a basal rosette of leaves, sends up stems that turn purple when mature, and is rich in nectar and pollen for insects and birds. In North America and Australasia it is usually regarded as a noxious weed. Kynes explains that Middle English knopweed comes from knop, meaning bud, and knap, meaning button, and that many folk names refer to the hard flower buds. In England it was widely used for love divination: a woman placed a floret in her blouse and thought of a prospect, and if the flower bloomed, so would love. Kynes places two flowers on the altar to boost divination about love and carries a picture of knapweed as a talisman for confidence and determination. Her heading covers greater knapweed (C. scabiosa) as well, so that citation is left for the curator.
