# Camwood

Baphia nitida, also known as camwood, barwood and African sandalwood although it is not a true sandalwood, is a shrubby, leguminous, hard-wooded tree from central west Africa. It is a small, evergreen understorey tree, often planted in villages, and is known as osun in Yoruba. The wood is of a very fine colour and is used in woodturning for knife handles and similar articles. The bark and heartwood are commonly used to make a brilliant but non-permanent red dye, which is soluble in alkali. Osun, or camwood, extract is also used in some soaps and skin treatments, mainly among the Yoruba people of West Africa. Wikipedia's article on West African Vodún lists camwood among the materials, with charcoal, kaolin and mud, that some shrines to the spirit Gbǎdù must contain. The article warns that it should not be confused with Osyris lanceolata, which is also called African sandalwood.
