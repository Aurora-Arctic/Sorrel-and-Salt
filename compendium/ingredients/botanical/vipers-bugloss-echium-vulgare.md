# Viper's Bugloss

Viper's bugloss is a biennial or monocarpic perennial of the borage family, native to Europe and western Asia and naturalised in north-eastern North America, south-western South America and New Zealand. Wikipedia says it grows 30 to 80 centimetres tall with rough, hairy leaves and red-flecked stems that resemble snake skin, and flowers that open pink and turn vivid blue. It adds that the plant is toxic to horses and cattle if eaten, through pyrrolizidine alkaloids. Grieve says it was long held to cure viper bites and is a good cordial on infusion. Culpeper calls it a most gallant herb of the Sun.

#### Questions for the Curator
