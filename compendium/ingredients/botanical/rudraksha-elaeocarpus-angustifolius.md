# Rudraksha

Elaeocarpus angustifolius is a large evergreen tree, native from India to New Caledonia and northern Australia, often with buttress roots, wavy-serrated leaves, creamy white flowers and bright blue, roughly spherical, edible fruit. It is known as the utrasum bean tree in India, woodenbegar and Indian bead tree in Sri Lanka, and Indian oil fruit and genitri in Australia. The blue colour comes from structural colouration, not a pigment. In India the cleaned pits of the fruit are called rudraksha, meaning Rudra's teardrops or eyes, and are widely used as prayer beads, particularly in Hinduism. The page says rudraksha might be produced by more than one species of Elaeocarpus, though E. angustifolius is the principal species used. The page adds that the Hindi name comes from Rudra, which it glosses as Shiva, and that smaller stones are traditionally worth the most in mala garlands.
