# Stinking Arrach

Stinking arrach, or stinking goosefoot, is a small, prostrate annual of waste ground and roadsides, native to Europe and parts of Asia and Africa. Wikipedia says it has grey-green, mealy leaves and a strong smell of rotten fish, and that mediaeval herbals give it mainly gynaecological uses following the doctrine of signatures. Grieve says it is called Stinking Motherwort because its leaves were used in hysteria and in complaints of women, describes it as emmenagogue and antispasmodic, and says it was once thought to cure barrenness. She lists trimethylamine among its constituents. Culpeper says it is under the dominion of Venus and the sign Scorpio, and praises it as cheap and common, growing on every dunghill.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve prints the formal name _Chenopodium vulvaria_ only as a synonym with the authority "S. Wats.", under the heading _Chenopodium olidum_ (Linn.). I cite her page as `stated` because it prints the formal name. GBIF (evidence only, not an authority): https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Chenopodium%20olidum gives _Chenopodium olidum_ Curtis, SYNONYM, accepted key 3083932, which is _Chenopodium vulvaria_ L. (ACCEPTED). Do you accept Grieve's page as _Chenopodium vulvaria_? My recommendation: yes.

Culpeper prints only the English heading "ARRACH, WILD AND STINKING" and no Latin name, so his page is cited with `nameBasis: unresolved`. The roster ties it to _Chenopodium vulvaria_; no authority I read maps Culpeper's heading to that formal name. He says "Stinking Arrach" is "under the dominion of Venus, and under the sign Scorpio". Grieve says Culpeper gives its Latin name as _A. olida_, not _Chenopodium_. Do you accept Culpeper's "ARRACH, WILD AND STINKING" as _Chenopodium vulvaria_? My recommendation: accept it only if you are content to rest the Culpeper-sourced fields on the common name; otherwise drop the Culpeper citation and the fields that rest on it.
