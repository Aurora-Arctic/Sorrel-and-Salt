# Butcher's Broom

Butcher's broom is a low, shrubby evergreen, about knee height and occasionally up to three feet, growing in woods and on waste and bushy ground, especially in the south of England. Grieve calls it the only liliaceous shrub in Britain. Its tough, striated stems carry rigid, spine-tipped leaf-like organs that are really flattened branches. Small greenish-white flowers, male and female on different plants, grow from the middle of these and the female plants carry scarlet cherry-sized berries that last through the winter. Its name comes from its stiff branches having been used to make small brooms for cleaning butchers' blocks. Grieve records many folk names, among them Knee Holly and Jew's Myrtle, and names the root and herb as the medicinal parts.
