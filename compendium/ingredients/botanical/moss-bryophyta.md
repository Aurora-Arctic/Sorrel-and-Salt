# Moss

Moss here means the mosses as a group: small, non-vascular, flowerless plants of the division Bryophyta that usually form dense green clumps or mats in damp or shady places. Wikipedia says they have no seeds, reproduce by spores, and number about 12,000 species. Cunningham says that carrying moss of any type taken from a gravestone is a good ensurer of luck, especially financial luck, and that moss is used to stuff general-purpose poppets. Kynes heads her entry with two species, common haircap moss and sphagnum moss. She records that moss was used medicinally and in mats and padding across the British Isles and Europe, that in rural England a wreath of moss, ivy and red berries was laid on a loved one's grave on All Saints' Day, and that German moss folk, a kind of faerie, clothed themselves in moss taken from the roots of a maple. She ties a handful of moss with green and gold ribbons in a money spell. Because her heading names two species, that citation is left for the curator.
