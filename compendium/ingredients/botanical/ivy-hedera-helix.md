# Ivy

Ivy, or common, English or European ivy, is an evergreen clinging vine of the ginseng family, native to most of Europe and parts of western Asia. Wikipedia says it climbs by aerial rootlets up trees, walls and fences, bears greenish-yellow flowers rich in nectar from late summer to late autumn, and has purple-black to orange-yellow berries. Its berries are somewhat poisonous to humans, though leaf extracts are part of current cough medicines. Kynes says all parts, especially the leaves and berries, are toxic if ingested and the sap may irritate the skin. She records that Plutarch called it the plant of Osiris, that Bacchus and Dionysus wore crowns of it, and that in medieval England a crown of ivy worn on Beltane Eve was said to let a person see witches. Cunningham has women carry it for good luck, brides wear it, and strands strewn where they guard against negativity, and he pairs it magically with holly. Kynes places sprigs on the altar for guidance and winds a small ivy wreath round a candle for a binding spell.
