# Poplar

The black poplar is a large deciduous tree of the willow family, native to Europe, south-west and central Asia and north-west Africa, with diamond-shaped leaves, rapid growth and a liking for low, moist ground. Wikipedia says the trees are dioecious and pollinated by wind. Kynes names it as the Italian or Lombardy poplar and puts it under one poplar heading with the white poplar. She records the Greek belief that black poplars lined the path to the underworld, a Sumerian grave crown of white poplar, and the English name devil's fingers for a profusion of catkins taken as a sign of misfortune. Her powers include ancestor work, divination, prophecy and prosperity, and she sells dried bark and dried buds. Her heading covers two species, so her citation is left for the curator. Cunningham's poplar is a different species and is not cited.
