# Ashwagandha

Ashwagandha is a short evergreen shrub of the nightshade family, 35 to 75 centimetres tall, with dull green elliptic leaves, small green bell-shaped flowers and orange-red ripe fruit. It is native to dry regions of Africa, the Middle East, southern Europe, the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia, and is grown in the drier parts of India. The name joins the Sanskrit words for horse and smell, after the strong horse-like odour of the root. The root powder has been used for centuries in traditional Indian medicine, and today the root, leaf powder or extracts are sold as a supplement. Its main compounds are withanolides, which is why it is nicknamed Indian ginseng. It is generally well tolerated for about three months, but it should be avoided in pregnancy.
