# Honesty

Honesty, or annual honesty, is a flowering plant of the cabbage family, native to southern Europe. Wikipedia lists its other names as money plant, moneywort, penny flower, silver dollar, money-in-both-pockets, Chinese money and Chinese coins. Its flat, round, papery seed pods look like silver coins, which is why Cunningham says it is put in money spells. Kynes says it grew in medieval gardens and that newlyweds in the Channel Islands carried it. It was used to ward off evil, to open doors and break chains, and to un-shoe horses, and it was held to cure lunacy. The name honesty comes from the Victorian language of flowers. Kynes places a seed disc over each eye before psychic work, uses seed discs in lunar magic, and keeps one in a wallet. Cunningham heads his entry Lunaria spp., which names a genus and not one species, so that citation is left for the curator to settle.
