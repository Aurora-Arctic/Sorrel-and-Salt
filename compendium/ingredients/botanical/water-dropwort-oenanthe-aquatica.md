# Water Dropwort

Water dropwort, or fine-leaved water dropwort, is a biennial of the carrot family growing in ditches and by the sides of ponds in Europe and western Asia. Wikipedia says it has stems up to two metres long, finely divided leaves that are thread-like under water, and compound umbels of white flowers from June to September in northern Europe. It says the plant can be confused with more deadly species and is not widely used because of the danger of poisoning. Grieve heads her page with a poison warning, says it is less poisonous than Oenanthe crocata but that both produce ill effects if eaten, and records old use of the fruit for chest complaints.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve heads her page "Oenanthe phellandrium (LANK.)" and lists _Phellandrium aquaticum_ (Linn.) as a synonym.
