# Bael

Bael is a deciduous tree up to 13 metres tall, native to the Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia, with slender drooping branches, spiny bark and three-part leaves. Its globose fruit has a hard woody rind and aromatic orange pulp, and takes about eleven months to ripen. Hindus and Buddhists consider the tree sacred, and Hindus use its leaves in rites, including the worship of the lingam, the icon of Shiva. The Rigveda's Sri Sukta reveres it as the home of Lakshmi, and the tree is also taken to be an incarnation of the goddess Sati. Among the Newar of Nepal, girls take part in a fertility ritual called Bel Bibaaha in which they are "married" to the bael fruit. The fruit also appears in the Buddhist Daddabha Jataka.
