# Oil Palm

Elaeis guineensis is a species of palm commonly called oil palm, and sometimes African oil palm or macaw-fat. It is native to west and southwest Africa, between Angola and The Gambia, and is the principal source of palm oil. Mature palms are single-stemmed and grow to 20 metres tall, with pinnate leaves 3 to 5 metres long. The fruit is reddish, about the size of a large plum, and grows in large bunches; its oily, fleshy outer layer surrounds a single seed, the palm kernel, which is also rich in oil. In traditional African medicine, Wikipedia says, different parts of the plant are used as a laxative and diuretic, as a poison antidote, and to treat headaches, rheumatism and skin infections. In Yoruba religion it is associated with the creation myth as the first tree Ọbatala finds descending to earth, and as Ọrunmila's axis mundi. Its fronds mark sacred areas, and its kernels are prepared as a tool for receiving Ọrunmila's words to the babalawo.
