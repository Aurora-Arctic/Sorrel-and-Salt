# Passionflower

Passionflower here is the purple passionflower, also called maypop, a fast-growing perennial vine of the southern United States with large, intricate flowers and an edible fruit the size of a hen's egg. Wikipedia says the name maypop comes partly from the fruit popping when stepped on. Cunningham calls it feminine, with Venus and water, and says that, contrary to its name, it is placed in the house to calm problems and bring peace. Carried, it attracts friends. Placed under the pillow, it aids sleep. His other names are Grandilla, Maracoc, Maypops and Passion Vine. Kynes puts the purple and the blue passionflower under one heading, and her citation is left for the curator. She links the plant to the Passion of Christ and to funeral wreaths.
