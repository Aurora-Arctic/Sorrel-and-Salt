# Black-Eyed Pea

The black-eyed pea, or black-eyed bean, is a legume grown around the world for its medium-sized edible bean. Wikipedia calls it a subspecies of the cowpea, an Old World plant domesticated in Africa, and says it is sometimes simply called a cowpea. It originates in West Africa and has been cultivated in China and India since prehistoric times. African slaves brought it to Virginia in the 17th century along with other plants from their homelands. All the peas are green when freshly shelled and brown or buff when dried, and the eye may be black, brown, red, pink or green. In African-American Hoodoo, eating black-eyed peas or Hoppin' John, a traditional soul food, on New Year's Day is thought to bring prosperity in the new year. The peas are typically cooked with a pork product such as bacon or ham bones and diced onion. Several legends explain the link between peas and good luck in the American South. In Nigeria and Ghana the peas are mashed into the fried dish akara or koose.
