# Horsetail

Horsetail, here field horsetail, is a fern relative found across the arctic and temperate Northern Hemisphere. Wikipedia describes a perennial that spreads by an underground rhizome and has two growth forms from the same root: a fertile spore-bearing stem in April that soon withers, and a sterile green stem that lasts until the first autumn frosts. It can be mistaken for mare's tail. Cunningham says whistles made from the stems, when played, will call snakes to the musician, and that horsetail is used in fertility mixtures or placed in the bedroom for the same purpose. His heading also names Equisetum telmateia and the genus, so it is left for the curator. The AHPA handbook (Safety Class 2d) says horsetail is not for use by people with kidney disease, and that it is a diuretic. Wikipedia says the plant is toxic to livestock, particularly horses.
