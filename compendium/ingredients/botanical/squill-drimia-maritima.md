# Squill

Squill, also called sea squill, sea onion or maritime squill, is a plant of the Asparagaceae that grows from a large bulb up to 20 centimetres wide, native to southern Europe, western Asia and northern Africa. Wikipedia says the leaves come in spring and a tall raceme of white flowers in autumn, and that the bulb has long been used both as a medicine and, because of its toxic cardiac glycosides, as a rat poison. Cunningham heads his entry with three names, Urginea scilla, U. maritima and Scilla maritima, and marks it Poison. He calls it masculine, with Mars and fire, and lists money, protection and hex-breaking. It is hung over a window to protect the home, kept in a jar with silver coins to draw money, and carried by a hexed person to break the spell. Wikipedia lists Urginea maritima and Scilla maritima as synonyms but not Urginea scilla, so the match is unresolved; see the questions. The hazards are set out in the safety notes.
