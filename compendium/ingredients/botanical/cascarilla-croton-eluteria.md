# Cascarilla

Cascarilla is a small tree or tall shrub of the Bahamas and the wider Caribbean, rarely above twenty feet. Its leaves are scaly, silver-bronze below and white-scaled above, and its small white flowers are very fragrant, opening in March and April. Grieve names the bark as the part used: aromatic, bitter, and a stimulating tonic for dyspepsia, diarrhoea and chronic bronchitis. Burned, it smells faintly of musk, and was used in fumigating pastilles and sometimes mixed with tobacco. The leaves can be infused as a digestive tea and the bark gives a black dye. Wikipedia says the bark aids digestion and flavours Campari and Vermouth, and that it contains volatile oils and cascarillins.
