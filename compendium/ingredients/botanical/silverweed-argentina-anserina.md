# Silverweed

Silverweed is a low perennial of the rose family, native across the temperate Northern Hemisphere on river shores, meadows and roadsides. Wikipedia says it grows to about 20 centimetres, spreads by creeping red stolons up to 80 centimetres long, and has pinnate leaves covered in silky white hairs that give it its name, with single yellow five-petalled flowers. Grieve says it is a favourite food of cattle, horses, goats, pigs and geese, calls the herb mildly astringent and tonic, and says the roots were eaten in the Hebrides. Wikipedia says the root has been cultivated as a food and records folklore that the plant was put in shoes to absorb sweat and could ward off witches.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve heads her page "Potentilla anserina (LINN.)". The cited Wikipedia revision is titled _Argentina anserina_, writes "Argentina anserina (synonym Potentilla anserina)" and lists _Potentilla anserina_ under "Synonyms", so I cite Grieve as `resolved` through Wikipedia, which is not one of the authorities I treat as settling a name alone. Wikipedia adds that the move to Argentina "remains controversial and is not accepted by some authorities". GBIF (evidence only, not an authority): https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Potentilla%20anserina gives Potentilla anserina L., SYNONYM, accepted key 7272211, which is Argentina anserina subsp. anserina (ACCEPTED). Do you accept the Grieve citation as _Argentina anserina_, and the entry under that name? My recommendation: yes.
