# Beach Morning Glory

Ipomoea pes-caprae is a creeping vine that grows on beaches across the tropics. It is a halophyte, a plant that tolerates salt, and is listed as pantropical. The name means "goat's foot" in Latin and refers to the leaf outline, which resembles the footprint of a goat. In Australia it is a commonly used Aboriginal medicine, applied as a poultice for stingray and stonefish stings. In Brazil the subspecies brasiliensis is known as salsa-da-praia in folk medicine and is used to treat inflammation and gastrointestinal disorders. In the Philippines it is called bagasua and is used to treat rheumatism, colic, oedema, whitlow and piles. The page does not say which part of the plant is used in each case, so this entry records the whole plant.
