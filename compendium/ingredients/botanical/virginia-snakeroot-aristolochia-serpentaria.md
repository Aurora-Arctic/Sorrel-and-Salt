# Virginia Snakeroot

Virginia snakeroot is a plant native to eastern North America, with the older name Endodeca serpentaria, according to Wikipedia. Wikipedia says the Cherokee chewed the root for snake bites, that the root has been used in bitters, and that the plant is endangered or threatened in some states. Cunningham heads his entry Snakeroot, with the Latin name Aristolochia serpentaria. He lists luck and money, and says the root is carried as a talisman and to break hexes and curses. His folk names include Pelican Flower, Snagree, Snakeweed and Virginian Snakeroot. Kynes has a Snakeroot entry that covers A. serpentaria and a second plant, A. rotunda, and gives earth, Gemini, Virgo, Jupiter and Mars. She lists banishing, luck, prosperity and secrets, and says both plants are poisonous if ingested. Her entry is cited with a question for the curator. The hazards are set out in the safety notes.
