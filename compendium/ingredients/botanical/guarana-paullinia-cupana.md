# Guarana

Guarana is a climbing plant of the soapberry family native to the Amazon basin and especially common in Brazil. Wikipedia says the seeds can be up to about 6 per cent caffeine, and that the fruit, brown to red, splits to show black seeds partly covered by white arils. Grieve says the part used is the prepared, crushed seed, made into hard cylindrical sticks by the Guaranis, a South American people, and grated into sugar and water as a drink. She names its principal constituent as guaranine, identical with caffeine, and calls it nervine, tonic and a slightly narcotic stimulant. Wikipedia says guarana plays an important role in Tupi and Guarani culture, with a myth among the Sateré-Maué about its origin.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve prints the botanical heading "Paullinia Cupana, Kunth. (H. B. and K.)" and lists "Paullinia Sorbilis" as a synonym and says its seeds are often mixed with those of _P. cupana_. Wikipedia lists _P. sorbilis_ as a synonym of _P. cupana_. I cite Grieve as `stated` for _Paullinia cupana_. Do you accept? My recommendation: yes.
