# Wild Rice

Zizania palustris, northern wild rice, is an annual aquatic grass native to the Great Lakes region and the boreal regions of Ontario, Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba, and Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan and Idaho. The common names manoomin, Canada rice, Indian rice and water oats are given by the source for the whole genus Zizania. It grows in shallow water in small lakes and slow streams, and the grain is eaten by ducks. Native Americans harvest it by canoe, bending the heads with wooden knockers so the seed falls into the boat. The Ojibwe, Ottawa and Potawatomi have used this species, and the Ojibwe consider wild rice a sacred part of their culture. In 2018 the White Earth Nation granted wild rice rights to exist and flourish.
