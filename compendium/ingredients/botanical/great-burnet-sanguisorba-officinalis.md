# Great Burnet

Great burnet is a herbaceous perennial of grasslands in the cooler parts of Europe, Asia and North America, growing to about a metre, flowering from June to September, with oblong heads of deep purple-brown flowers. Grieve says the herb and root are the parts used, the herb gathered in July and the root dug in autumn. She calls it astringent and tonic and formerly in high repute as a vulnerary, and gives the generic name as from the Latin for blood and staunch. Both herb and root are given internally for diarrhoea, dysentery and abnormal discharges, and the herb is used in herb beer. Wikipedia says it is used in traditional Chinese medicine as Di Yu, said to cool the blood and stop bleeding. Culpeper gives burnet to the Sun.

#### Questions for the Curator

Culpeper prints only the English heading "BURNET" and no Latin name, so his page is cited with `nameBasis: unresolved`. The roster ties it to _Sanguisorba officinalis_; no authority I read maps Culpeper's heading to that formal name. He gives the planet: "the Sun challenges dominion over". His page covers the common garden burnet and a "great wild Burnet" and gives the old names Sanguisorbia and Pimpinella, with no binomial. Grieve quotes this passage under her great burnet page. Do you accept Culpeper's "BURNET" as _Sanguisorba officinalis_? My recommendation: accept it only if you are content to rest the Culpeper-sourced fields on the common name; otherwise drop the Culpeper citation and the fields that rest on it.
