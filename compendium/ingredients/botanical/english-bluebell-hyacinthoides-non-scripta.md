# English Bluebell

The English bluebell is a bulbous perennial of the Atlantic coasts of Europe, from the Iberian peninsula to the British Isles, often carpeting ancient woodland in spring with nodding, sweet-scented, violet-blue flowers on one-sided stems. It is protected under UK law. Grieve says the bulb is diuretic and styptic, and its sticky juice served as a starch for ruffs and as bookbinders' gum and arrow-fletching glue. Kynes calls it one of the most powerful faery plants in the British Isles, marking a place of faery magic, with a legend that a human who heard the flowers ring would soon die. Wearing the flowers is said to prompt a person to speak from the heart. The bulbs are poisonous, and Wikipedia says the whole genus is normally considered toxic.

#### Questions for the Curator

Kynes prints the formal name _Hyacinthoides non-scripta_ and is cited as `stated`. Grieve heads the page "Scilla nutans (S. M.)" with "Hyacinthus nonscriptus (LINN.)" beneath. The cited Wikipedia page lists _Scilla nutans_ Sm. as a synonym, so Grieve is cited as `resolved` via Wikipedia, which is not a nomenclatural authority such as POWO. GBIF (evidence only, not an authority): https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Scilla%20nutans gives _Scilla nutans_ Sm., SYNONYM, accepted key 5304283, which is _Hyacinthoides non-scripta_ (L.) Chouard ex Rothm. (ACCEPTED). Do you accept the mapping? My recommendation: yes.

Kynes's bluebell entry also covers the Virginia bluebell (_Mertensia virginica_) as a second plant.
