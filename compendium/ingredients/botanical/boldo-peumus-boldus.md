# Boldo

Boldo is an evergreen shrub or small tree of the Andean foothills in Chile. Its opposite, leathery leaves are covered in small glands and smell strong and rather unpleasant when crushed. Grieve says the yellowish-green fruit is eaten in Chile, the bark is used for tanning, and the wood is made into charcoal. The leaves, the part used in herbal medicine, contain a volatile oil and an alkaloid called boldine. Boldo is often confused with a different plant, Plectranthus ornatus, sold in South America as false boldo, so the use and toxicity of the two have been mixed up. Wikipedia and AHPA record cautions on liver and bile-duct conditions.
