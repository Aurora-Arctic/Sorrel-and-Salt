# Black Bryony

Black bryony is a climbing perennial of woods and hedges, the only British member of the yam family. Weak twining stems climb over bushes and trees, carrying glossy heart-shaped leaves that turn dark purple or bright yellow in autumn. The stems die back in winter, but a large tuber, black outside, persists. Small greenish-white flowers of separate sexes open on different plants, and the female plants carry red berries when ripe. Every part of the plant is poisonous. Grieve notes the root was an old cathartic and diuretic, but calls it a dangerous remedy, and Wikipedia says it is not typically used internally. The botanical name was Tamus communis until it was moved to the yam genus Dioscorea.
