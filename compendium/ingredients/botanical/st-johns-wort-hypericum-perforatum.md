# St John's Wort

St John's wort is a hairless perennial herb of the family Hypericaceae with yellow flowers marked by black glands and leaves that look perforated because of translucent glands. Wikipedia says it is native to much of Europe, western and central Asia, parts of Africa and China, and is now spread worldwide. Its flowers were gathered at Midsummer, which became St John's Day, and hung on doors to ward off evil spirits, which gave it the name fuga daemonum. Cunningham calls it masculine, with the Sun and fire, and links it to Baldur. He lists health, protection, strength, love divination and happiness. Worn, it wards off fevers and colds and attracts love. A jar of it by a window protects against thunderbolts, fire and evil spirits, and it is burned to banish spirits. Kynes adds Leo and Sagittarius, dream work, nightmares, fertility and release. Cunningham, Kynes, Wikipedia and the American Herbal Products Association all record hazards, set out in the safety notes.
