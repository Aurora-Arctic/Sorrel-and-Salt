# Hedge Hyssop

Hedge hyssop is a rhizomatous perennial native to Europe, which Wikipedia lists with the names gratiole, grace of God and herb of grace. Grieve says it grows in meadows and moist grounds in the south of Europe, from a creeping, scaly rhizome to six to twelve inches, with opposite, stalkless, lance-shaped leaves and whitish or reddish flowers. She says it has no smell and a bitter, nauseous, somewhat acrid taste, and that it was formerly an official drug. Its active constituent is the bitter glucoside gratiolin. She calls it a drastic cathartic and emetic, also diuretic, and says that in large doses it is said to be poisonous. Culpeper makes it a herb of Mars and a violent purge.

#### Questions for the Curator
