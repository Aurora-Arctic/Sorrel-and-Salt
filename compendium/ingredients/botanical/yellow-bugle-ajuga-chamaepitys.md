# Yellow Bugle

Yellow bugle, also called ground pine, is a small annual of the mint family found on sandy and chalky fields in Europe, the Levant and North Africa, and scarce in England outside Kent, Surrey and Essex. Grieve describes a bushy plant three to six inches high with a hairy, viscid, purplish-red four-cornered stem, leaves divided into three very long narrow segments that resemble pine needles, and bright yellow flowers spotted with red on the lower lip, blooming in May and June. She says it smells and tastes highly aromatic and turpentiny. Wikipedia notes the shiny black seeds can stay alive in the soil for up to 50 years. Culpeper says Mars owns the herb.

#### Questions for the Curator

Culpeper prints only the English heading "GROUND PINE, OR CHAMEPITYS" and no Latin name, so his page is cited with `nameBasis: unresolved`. The roster ties it to _Ajuga chamaepitys_; no authority I read maps Culpeper's heading to that formal name. He says "Mars owns the herb." Grieve names the plant "European Ground Pine". Do you accept Culpeper's "GROUND PINE, OR CHAMEPITYS" as _Ajuga chamaepitys_? My recommendation: accept it only if you are content to rest the Culpeper-sourced fields on the common name; otherwise drop the Culpeper citation and the fields that rest on it.
