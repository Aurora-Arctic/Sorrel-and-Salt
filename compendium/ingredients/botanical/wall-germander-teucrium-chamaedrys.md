# Wall Germander

Wall germander is a creeping evergreen perennial of the mint family, native to Europe, the Greek islands and Syria. Grieve says it is scarce and hardly native in England, found on old ruins and as a garden escape, and was once widely grown for medicine. She describes square stems six to eighteen inches high, dark green, oak-like leaves, rose-coloured flowers in June and July, and a garlic-like smell from rubbed fresh leaves. Wikipedia says dietary supplements using germander are highly hepatotoxic and that France prohibited their sale in 1992. Grieve records its old use as a specific for gout, and says it was also used as a tonic in intermittent fevers.
