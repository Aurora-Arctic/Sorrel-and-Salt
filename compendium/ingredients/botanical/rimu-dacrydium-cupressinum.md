# Rimu

Dacrydium cupressinum, known as rimu, is a conifer in the family Podocarpaceae, native to New Zealand, and the country's most common native conifer, found across the North, South and Stewart Islands. It lives 600 to 800 years and sometimes 1,200. Its former name among European settlers was red pine, for the deep red of its sap and wood. In Māori culture it is of great importance. The fruit was a significant food, the Ngāpuhi used the timber for canoes, and long war spears and hunting spears were made from it. Medicinally, the gum was a styptic for bleeding wounds, a decoction of the bark treated wounds, the crushed inner bark was a pulp for burns, and the leaves treated sores. In 1773 James Cook brewed spruce beer from its young branch tips to prevent scurvy. Rimu was once New Zealand's most harvested timber and is now protected from logging under the Forests Act 1949, as amended in 1993.
