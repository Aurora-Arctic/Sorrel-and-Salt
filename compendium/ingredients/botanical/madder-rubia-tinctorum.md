# Madder

Madder is a perennial climbing herb of southern Europe and the Mediterranean, grown since ancient times for a red dye. Wikipedia says it grows to about 1.5 metres, with evergreen leaves in whorls of four to seven, small flowers, and small berries that ripen red to black, while the roots can be over a metre long. Grieve says the stalks are weak, prickly and often lie along the ground, with yellow starry flowers in the second or third year, and the thick fleshy roots are dried and powdered. She says coal-tar products have displaced the natural dye, that the root is not generally used medicinally, and that it has been reputed useful in amenorrhoea, dropsy and jaundice. Culpeper calls it an herb of Mars.
