# Star Anise

Star anise is a medium-sized evergreen tree native to south China and north-eastern Vietnam. Wikipedia says its star-shaped, reddish-brown fruit, picked just before ripening, is a spice that tastes much like anise, and that the oil is used in cooking, perfumery and toothpaste. It is also called badian and Chinese star anise. Cunningham prints the name as Illicum verum, a spelling that is not the formal name, calls the plant masculine with Jupiter and air, and lists psychic powers and luck. The seeds are burned as incense to increase psychic powers, worn as beads, set on the altar one to each of the four directions, and carried as a luck-bringer. Kynes covers Chinese star anise and Japanese star anise (Illicium anisatum) in one entry. The American Herbal Products Association, Kynes and Wikipedia all warn that Japanese star anise is toxic and is confused with this plant; the hazards are set out in the safety notes.
