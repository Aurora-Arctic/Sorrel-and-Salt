# Babul Acacia

Babul acacia, also called the gum Arabic tree, is a thorny tree of five to twenty metres with a dense rounded crown, dark fissured bark and pairs of straight grey spines. It is native to Africa, the Middle East and the Indian subcontinent, and it is the type species of the old genus Acacia, which botanists later moved to Vachellia, so books still call it Acacia nilotica. Round golden-yellow flower heads give way to pods. The gum that seeps from the trunk after the rains is the part used: it is collected, sorted and sold as acacia gum, and in pharmacy it is dissolved to make a soothing mucilage for coughs. Kynes writes that acacia gum served as sacred incense across the ancient world, and the tree stood for immortality in Egypt.
