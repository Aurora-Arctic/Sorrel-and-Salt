# Jaborandi

Jaborandi is a species of flowering plant in the citrus family, native to north-eastern Brazil, which Wikipedia names as a source of the drug pilocarpine. Grieve describes a shrub of four to five feet with smooth greyish bark, small reddish-purple flowers, and large pinnate leaves of two to four pairs of leaflets, chiefly exported from Ceara and Pernambuco. Only the leaflets are used, brownish-green, hairless and leathery with large oil glands, and slightly aromatic when crushed. She says the name is applied to many pungent plants of two families, and that opinion differs on the origin of the drug. The crude drug is rarely used, its virtues being due to pilocarpine, which she calls antagonistic to atropine, and which brings on sweating and free salivation. She also records a warning about overdose.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve has a section headed "Poisons with Antidotes" that says "An overdose may cause flushing, profuse sweating and salivation, nausea, rapid pulse, contracted pupils, diarrhoea, and even fatal pulmonary oedema." It does not call the leaf poisonous or toxic, so I did not set `toxic: true`. Should the flag be on? My recommendation: yes, because a cited source classes the drug under poisons and says an overdose can be fatal, but that goes beyond the source words.

The AHPA monograph covers three species, _Pilocarpus jaborandi_ Holmes, _P. microphyllus_ Stapf and _P. pennatifolius_ Lem., under one safety entry. I cite it as `stated` for _P. jaborandi_ because it prints the exact name, but its safety statement applies to all three. Do you accept? My recommendation: yes.
