# Walnut

Walnut is a large tree, native to Eurasia, grown for its nuts and also known as the common walnut or Persian walnut. Cunningham gives it the Sun and fire, and lists its powers as health, mental powers, infertility and wishes. He records that witches are said to dance beneath walnut trees in Italy, that a walnut carried is believed to strengthen the heart, and that the tree is thought to attract lightning. Leaves placed in a hat are said to prevent headaches and sunstroke, and roasted walnuts were eaten to delay children. His folk names include Tree of Evil and Caucasian Walnut. Cunningham marks it with a caution about long-term internal use, and a toxicology monograph on its relative black walnut notes that the hulls of English walnut have caused skin irritation; both are set out in the safety notes. Wikipedia supplies the species' range and common names.
