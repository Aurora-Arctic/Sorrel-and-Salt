# Kelp

Kelp here is Laminaria digitata, a large, tough, leathery brown seaweed of the northern Atlantic Ocean, also called oarweed. Wikipedia says it grows mostly on exposed shores in the lower littoral, may reach about 4 m long, has a holdfast with root-like rhizoids and a hand-shaped blade of finger-like segments. It adds that the dried stalks, called sea-tangle tents, were historically used in traditional medicine to induce labour mechanically. Cunningham gives it the planet Jupiter and the element water, with powers of travel and protection, and uses it for protection on long sea voyages. The AHPA handbook (Safety Class 2d) says it is not for use in people with hyperthyroidism, should be taken with at least 250 ml of liquid, and that other drugs should be taken an hour before it or several hours after, as its mucilage may slow their absorption. The AHPA entry covers several Laminaria species and the page cited gives Laminaria digitata its own entry with kelp as its standard common name.
