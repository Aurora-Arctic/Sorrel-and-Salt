# Daidai

The daidai is a variety of bitter orange native to Asia, written Citrus × daidai on its Wikipedia page. It originated in the Himalayas, spread to the Yangtze valley and later to Japan. The fruit is very bitter and not usually eaten, but its dried peel is used in Kampo, the Japanese adaptation of Chinese medicine. The dried peel of young fruit, kijitsu, is used as a stomachic, expectorant and laxative, and the peel of ripe fruit, tōhi, as a fragrant stomachic and expectorant. Two main cultivars are named, kabusu and kaiseitō. The Japanese word for the colour orange, daidai-iro, comes from the fruit. The name daidai means several generations, because the fruit stays on the tree for years, so that a tree bears fruit of several seasons. In Japan the fruit decorates New Year displays such as shimekazari and is set on top of kagami mochi, as a symbol of a family continuing for generations.
