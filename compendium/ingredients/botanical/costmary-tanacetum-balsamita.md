# Costmary

Costmary is a perennial herb native to the Orient, naturalised in southern Europe and long grown in English gardens, where Lyte in 1578 called it very common. Wikipedia describes oval serrated leaves, plants up to two metres tall, and summer clusters of small yellow button-shaped flowers. Grieve contrasts its entire, finely toothed leaves with the feathery leaves of tansy, its near relative, and says the whole plant gives off a soft balsamic odour. Its leaves flavoured ale, which gave the name Alecost, and went into salads and pot-pourri. Wikipedia derives the name from costus of Saint Mary and says it was thought a treatment for women's diseases. Culpeper calls it the balsam herb and puts it under Jupiter.

#### Questions for the Curator

Culpeper prints only the English heading "COSTMARY, OR ALCOST, OR BALSAM" and no Latin name, so his page is cited with `nameBasis: unresolved`. The roster ties it to _Tanacetum balsamita_; no authority I read maps Culpeper's heading to that formal name. He gives the planet: "under the dominion of Jupiter". The heading gives three common names and no Latin name. Do you accept Culpeper's "COSTMARY, OR ALCOST, OR BALSAM" as _Tanacetum balsamita_?
