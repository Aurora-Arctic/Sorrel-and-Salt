# Hog's Fennel

Hog's fennel is, by Wikipedia, an almost hairless biennial of wetlands in the carrot family, native to most of Europe and eastwards to Central Asia, with a thin foetid milky latex in its young parts. It is the main food plant of the Old World swallowtail butterfly. Wikipedia says the roots have been used as a ginger substitute in south-eastern Europe and Russia. Grieve describes a plant three to four feet high with large umbels of yellow flowers from July to September and leaves cut into long narrow segments. She says the thick root smells strongly of sulphur and yields a yellowish-green juice that dries to a gummy resin, and that the active constituent of the root is peucedanin, said to be diuretic and emmenagogue. She names the herb as the part used. Culpeper makes it a herb of Mercury.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve's heading prints two names: "Peucedanum palustre (LINN.), Peucedanum officinale (LINN.)", and her text uses _Peucedanum officinale_ and a salt-marsh habitat. Wikipedia (oldid 1360084238) says "hog's fennel (unqualified) and sea hog's fennel" are common names of _Peucedanum officinale_, a different perennial species of drier habitats, and that _P. palustre_ is the marsh hog's fennel of wetlands. I cite Grieve as `stated` for _P. palustre_ because her heading prints it, but her description may describe _P. officinale_. Do you accept Grieve for _P. palustre_? My recommendation: no. Keep the Wikipedia citation only, or move the Grieve fields to a _P. officinale_ entry.

Culpeper prints only the English heading "SOW-FENNEL, OR HOG'S-FENNEL" and no Latin name, so his page is cited with `nameBasis: unresolved`. The roster ties it to _Peucedanum palustre_; no authority I read maps Culpeper's heading to that formal name. He gives the planet: "also an herb of Mercury". He places it in the salt marshes near Faversham, which fits P. officinale as Wikipedia describes it. Do you accept Culpeper's "SOW-FENNEL, OR HOG'S-FENNEL" as _Peucedanum palustre_? My recommendation: accept it only if you are content to rest the Culpeper-sourced fields on the common name; otherwise drop the Culpeper citation and the fields that rest on it.
