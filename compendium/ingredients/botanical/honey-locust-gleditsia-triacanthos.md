# Honey Locust

The honey locust is a deciduous tree of the pea family, native to central North America, where it grows mostly in the moist soil of river valleys. Wikipedia says the pulp inside its pods is edible and eaten by wildlife and livestock, unlike that of the black locust, which is toxic. Kynes describes small greenish-white flowers, long twisted seedpods that stay on the tree into winter, and thorns with three or more points up to six inches long. Honey refers to the sugary pulp, not to honey production. The Delaware and Cherokee used it as medicine and made bows from the wood, and the thorns served as sewing needles. Kynes treats it under the shared heading Locust, together with the black locust, so the lore, elements and powers there may belong to either tree. She dries large seedpods as rattles for dark moon rituals and places three thorns on a windowsill, pointing at a threat, for protection.
