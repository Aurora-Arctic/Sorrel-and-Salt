# Balsam of Peru

Balsam of Peru comes from the Peru balsam tree, a large tree of Central American forests with a straight smooth trunk and a timber like mahogany. Every part of the tree holds a resinous juice, and the leaves are alternate and pinnate with blunt, notched leaflets. Its flowers are so fragrant that Grieve says they can be smelt a hundred yards away, and a tree begins to yield after five or six years and goes on for thirty. The extraction gives three grades of balsam, and the name Balsam of Peru comes from its being shipped from Peru. Several false Peruvian balsams are sold. Contact allergy to the balsam is common.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve heads this section "Myroxylon Pereir (KLOTSCH)", which looks like a misprint of _Myroxylon Pereirae_ (Klotzsch). Wikipedia (the cited revision) treats _Myroxylon balsamum_ var. _pereirae_ (Royle) Harms as the Peru balsam tree and lists _Myrospermum pereirae_ Royle as a synonym, but I read no authority that maps Grieve's "Pereirae (Klotzsch)" to this variety. AHPA p. 591 prints _Myroxylon balsamum_ (L.) Harms var. _pereirae_ (Royle) Harms with the part oleoresin.

GBIF (evidence only, not an authority): https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Myroxylon%20pereirae gives _Myroxylon pereirae_ Royle, SYNONYM, accepted key 5357190, which is _Myroxylon balsamum_ (L.) Harms, the species, not the variety. The roster name is the variety.

Do you accept Grieve's page for the variety? My recommendation: yes, because it describes the tree that yields Peru balsam, then set the file to approved.
