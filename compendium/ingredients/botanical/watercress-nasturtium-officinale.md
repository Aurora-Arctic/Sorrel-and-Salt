# Watercress

Watercress is a perennial that grows in abundance near springs and open running water, creeping, with shining brownish-green leaves of heart-shaped leaflets and small white flowers. Grieve lists the leaves, flowers and seeds as the parts used, calls it an antiscorbutic and says it is eaten as a salad. Wikipedia calls it a species of aquatic flowering plant in the cabbage family, also named yellowcress. Kynes records that the Greeks and Romans believed watercress improved the brain, that in Devon eating it was thought to make a person cleverer, and that because it grows in wells and streams it was a source of power to some heroes in Irish legends; he also gives it the element water. Cautions from Grieve and the American Herbal Products Association are set out in the safety notes. Kynes covers several cresses under one heading, so the entry is flagged.
