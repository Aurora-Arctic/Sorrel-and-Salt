# Pacific Yam

Dioscorea alata, also called ube, ubi, uwhi, purple yam, winged yam or greater yam, is a species of yam, a tuber. The tubers are usually a vivid violet-purple to bright lavender, but some range from creamy-white to plain white. Its origins are in the Asian and Oceanian tropics. The name ube is derived from Tagalog in the Philippines, while uwhi derives from Maori in New Zealand. It is one of the most important staple crops in Austronesian cultures, and one of the species of yam domesticated independently in Island Southeast Asia and New Guinea. The tubers have a mildly sweet, earthy and nutty taste, reminiscent of sweet potatoes or taro, and the violet cultivars turn dishes vivid violet because of their anthocyanins. Purple yam is most common in Philippine cuisine, where it is widely used in desserts. No clinical evidence shows that it has any therapeutic properties.
