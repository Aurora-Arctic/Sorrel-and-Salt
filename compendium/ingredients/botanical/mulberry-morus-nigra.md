# Mulberry

Black mulberry is a deciduous tree of the fig family, native to south-western Asia, where it has been cultivated so long that its natural range is unknown. Wikipedia says its fruit is a cluster of small drupes, dark purple and almost black when ripe, with a rich flavour, and that its cells carry 308 chromosomes, reported as the highest number of any known seed plant. Kynes records that in China the mulberry was a sacred tree symbolising cosmic order, though one planted before a house was believed to bring sorrow, and that the Greeks and Romans added the fruit to wine to darken it. In Italy a piece of twig was carried in the pocket as a good-luck amulet. Cunningham says mulberry protects the garden from lightning, aids work on the will, and that its wood is a powerful protectant against evil and is made into wands. Kynes defines a ritual circle with a mulberry stick and prepares a protection candle with the juice. Both writers' headings cover more than this one species, so those citations are left for the curator.
