# Black Lovage

Black lovage, better known as alexanders, is a stout hairless biennial growing to about 150 centimetres, with a solid stem that turns hollow and grooved with age and a tuberous taproot. Its large, glossy leaves are divided into threes and have broad, swollen, purple-striped stalks, and the yellowish-green flowers form rounded umbels. The fruit is almost black when ripe, which is why old herbalists called it the black pot-herb. It grows on waste ground and in hedges around the Mediterranean and the Atlantic coasts of Europe and was long grown as a pot herb, but is now mostly appreciated by foragers. Amateur collectors have sometimes mistaken it for wild angelica.
