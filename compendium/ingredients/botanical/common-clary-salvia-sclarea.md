# Common Clary

Clary, or clary sage, is a biennial or short-lived perennial of the northern Mediterranean, North Africa and Central Asia. Wikipedia describes thick, hairy square stems three to four feet tall, large wrinkled leaves, and whorled flowers held in pale mauve or white-pink bracts. Grieve says it reached English cultivation in 1562 and was called Clear Eye or Eyebright. Wikipedia records the old practice, which it notes Culpeper described, of putting a mucilaginous seed in the eye to lift out a foreign body. Its distilled oil is used in perfumery and to give a muscatel note to vermouths and wines, and Grieve says German wine merchants used the herb to make Rhenish wine pass as a muscatel. Culpeper puts it under the Moon.

#### Questions for the Curator

Culpeper prints only the English heading "CLARY, OR MORE PROPERLY CLEAR-EYE" and no Latin name, so his page is cited with `nameBasis: unresolved`. The roster ties it to _Salvia sclarea_; no authority I read maps Culpeper's heading to that formal name. He gives the planet: "under the dominion of the Moon". His page describes the ordinary garden clary and no Latin name. Do you accept Culpeper's "CLARY, OR MORE PROPERLY CLEAR-EYE" as _Salvia sclarea_? My recommendation: accept it only if you are content to rest the Culpeper-sourced fields on the common name; otherwise drop the Culpeper citation and the fields that rest on it.
