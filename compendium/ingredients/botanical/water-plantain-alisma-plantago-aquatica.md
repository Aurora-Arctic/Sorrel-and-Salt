# Water Plantain

Water plantain, also called mad-dog weed, is a perennial aquatic plant found in shallow water across most of Europe and Asia. Wikipedia says it is hairless, with a fibrous root, several long-stalked basal leaves, a triangular stem up to a metre tall and a branched head of small flowers with three white or pale purple petals. Grieve stresses that it is not a plantain at all, only named for the likeness of its leaves, and says it grows by lakes, streams and ditches. She records old use of the root against hydrophobia, which she says repeated experiments did not confirm, and use of the leaves and rhizome as a diuretic.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve prints her botanical name as "Alisma Plantago", which is not the formal name _Alisma plantago-aquatica_, and the cited Wikipedia revision does not list "Alisma plantago" as a synonym, so her page is cited with `nameBasis: unresolved`. GBIF (evidence only, not an authority): https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Alisma%20plantago gives _Alisma plantago_ L., SYNONYM, accepted key 2865100, which is _Alisma plantago-aquatica_ L. (ACCEPTED). Do you accept Grieve's "Alisma Plantago" as _Alisma plantago-aquatica_? My recommendation: yes, because GBIF maps it to exactly that species and her description (a water plant with three-petalled pale pink flowers) fits.
