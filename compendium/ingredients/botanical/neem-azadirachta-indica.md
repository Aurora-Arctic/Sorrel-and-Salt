# Neem

Neem, Azadirachta indica, is a fast-growing evergreen tree of the mahogany family, also called margosa, nimtree or Indian lilac. It reaches 15 to 20 metres and is native to the Indian subcontinent and parts of South-East Asia, though it is naturalised in tropical areas worldwide. Its fruits and seeds are the source of neem oil, and the seeds contain azadirachtin, an insect antifeedant and insecticide. The page says dried leaves are kept in cupboards and grain stores to keep insects away, and twigs are used as teeth-cleaning sticks. The tender shoots and flowers are eaten as a vegetable in India, and the flowers are used in festivals such as Ugadi. Products from the tree have been used in traditional medicine for centuries, but there is insufficient clinical evidence of any benefit. The page warns that neem oil is toxic and can lead to death in small children. It is a declared weed in the Northern Territory of Australia, where buying or moving it is illegal.
