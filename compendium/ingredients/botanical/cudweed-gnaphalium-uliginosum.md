# Cudweed

Marsh cudweed is a small, very woolly annual, 4 to 20 cm tall, found on damp disturbed ground, paths and acid soils across much of Europe, Asia and North America. Wikipedia describes narrow oblong leaves woolly on both sides, clusters of three to ten flower heads with brownish-yellow florets, and flowering from July to September. Grieve gives its habitat as marshy places in most parts of Europe, with branched, diffused stalks and flower heads crowded at the ends of the branches, appearing in August. She names the herb as the part used and records an astringent infusion, one ounce to a pint of boiling water, taken as a wineglassful or used as a gargle for quinsy. Her page lists the names Cotton Weed and March Everlasting. Culpeper puts cudweed under Venus.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve prints the botanical heading "Graphalium uliginosum", which is not the roster name _Gnaphalium uliginosum_ and which no authority I read lists. I cite her page as `unresolved`. GBIF (evidence only, not an authority): https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/match?name=Graphalium%20uliginosum gives _Gnaphalium uliginosum_ L., ACCEPTED (a fuzzy match on the misspelling).. Is "Graphalium" a misprint of _Gnaphalium_? My recommendation: yes, but please confirm before it is relied on.

Culpeper prints only the English heading "CUDWEED, OR COTTONWEED" and no Latin name, so his page is cited with `nameBasis: unresolved`. The roster ties it to _Gnaphalium uliginosum_; no authority I read maps Culpeper's heading to that formal name. He gives the planet: "Venus is Lady of it". His page names Cudweed, Cottonweed, Chaffweed and Dwarf Cudweed and describes "the common Cudweed", with no Latin name. Do you accept Culpeper's "CUDWEED, OR COTTONWEED" as _Gnaphalium uliginosum_? My recommendation: accept it only if you are content to rest the Culpeper-sourced fields on the common name; otherwise drop the Culpeper citation and the fields that rest on it.
