# Syrian Rue

Peganum harmala, called wild rue, Syrian rue, African rue, esfand, espand or harmel, is a perennial herb with a woody underground rootstock, usually growing in saline soils in temperate desert and Mediterranean regions. It has deep roots, a strong smell, finely divided leaves, white flowers and small capsules with many dark, oily seeds that hold a high concentration of beta-carboline alkaloids. It is native across North Africa, southern and eastern Europe, the Middle East, Central Asia and parts of South and East Asia. Its English name comes from a resemblance to rue, to which it is not related. The seeds are used as a dye, as incense and in traditional medicine: the page says esfand is burnt at Persian and Kashmiri weddings to ward off the evil eye and negative energies, and that it is widely used for protection against Djinn in Morocco.
