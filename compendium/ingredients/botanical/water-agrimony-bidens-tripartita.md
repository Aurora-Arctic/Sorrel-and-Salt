# Water Agrimony

Water agrimony, or bur marigold, is an annual of the sunflower family found in wet places such as the margins of ponds and ditches, and native to much of Eurasia, north Africa and North America. Grieve describes an erect, wiry stem about two feet high, spotted with brown, opposite leaves divided into three segments, and brownish-yellow flower heads. She says the burr-like fruits cling to animal coats, which gave the plant its present name, and that its flower heads smell of rosin or cedar when burnt. Wikipedia says it was once highly esteemed for staunching blood flow. Culpeper calls it a plant of Jupiter that belongs to the sign Cancer.

#### Questions for the Curator

Culpeper prints only the English heading "WATER AGRIMONY" and no Latin name, so his page is cited with `nameBasis: unresolved`. The roster ties it to _Bidens tripartita_; no authority I read maps Culpeper's heading to that formal name. He says "It is a plant of Jupiter, as well as the other Agrimony, only this belongs to the celestial sign Cancer." Grieve says Culpeper's plant is now called Bur Marigold, which is a common name, not an authority. Do you accept Culpeper's "WATER AGRIMONY" as _Bidens tripartita_? My recommendation: accept it only if you are content to rest the Culpeper-sourced fields on the common name; otherwise drop the Culpeper citation and the fields that rest on it.
