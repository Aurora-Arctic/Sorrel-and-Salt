# Spurge

Spurge here means the genus Euphorbia, about 2,000 species of herbs, shrubs and trees. Wikipedia says all of them share a poisonous, caustic, latex-like milky sap and a flower head called a cyathium. The common name comes from the Old French word for purge, because the sap was used as a purgative. Kynes has one spurge entry, for cypress spurge (Euphorbia cyparissias) and sun spurge (E. helioscopia). She gives fire, Leo, Mars and Mercury, and powers of defence, hexes and protection, and says the plant is toxic if ingested, that sun spurge can be fatal, that the white sap is toxic to skin and eyes, and that sun spurge may cause photosensitivity. She also lists a long run of folk names. Cunningham has no spurge entry. The hazards are set out in the safety notes. The Kynes entry is unresolved; see the questions.
