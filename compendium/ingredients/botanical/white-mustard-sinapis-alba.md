# White Mustard

White mustard, also called yellow mustard, is an annual of the cabbage family, native to the Mediterranean region and Europe and now widespread. Wikipedia says it grows to about 70 centimetres, with stalkless pinnate leaves, yellow four-petalled flowers and short pods, and is grown for its seeds, which make the condiment mustard, and as a fodder and green manure crop. Grieve describes it as a native of Europe common by roadsides and cultivated, closely resembling black mustard but smaller and less pungent, and records the seeds as rubefacient, used in poultices, and formerly as a fashionable laxative for old people.

#### Questions for the Curator

Grieve heads her section "MUSTARD, WHITE" and prints "Brassica alba (BOISS.)" as her botanical name, giving _Sinapis alba_ (Linn.) only as a synonym. The cited Wikipedia revision lists _Brassica alba_ as a synonym of _Sinapis alba_, so I cite Grieve as `stated` through the synonym she prints. Do you accept this? My recommendation: yes.
