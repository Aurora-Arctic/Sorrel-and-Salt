# White Hellebore

White hellebore is Veratrum album, a plant of the family Melanthiaceae native to Europe and parts of western Asia, also called false helleborine and white veratrum. It is not a true hellebore. Wikipedia calls it a poisonous plant and says all parts of it are poisonous; in drinks it has been mistaken for yellow gentian or wild garlic. Kynes, under a heading that joins black and white hellebore, says the Greeks and Romans used hellebore to protect cattle from spells and that in the Middle Ages it was strewn on floors to banish evil spirits and used in exorcisms. Both hellebores were used to ward off witches. The hazards are set out in the safety notes. Because Kynes pools the two plants, the entry is flagged for the curator.
