# Buckthorn

Common buckthorn is a thorny shrub or small tree, called purging buckthorn on Wikipedia because its seeds and leaves are mildly poisonous and have a laxative effect. Kynes describes common buckthorn as having oval, toothed, inward-curling leaves, yellow-green four-petalled flowers and black berry-like fruit, and groups it with alder buckthorn under one heading. Cunningham calls buckthorn feminine, with Saturn and water, and lists protection, exorcism, wishes and legal matters. He says branches near doors and windows drive away enchantments and sorceries, and that buckthorn is carried or worn to court. Kynes adds fire, Libra, Mars and Samhain. She says that in ancient Athens branches were placed at entrances to keep the walking dead out, and that a necklace of the bark was believed to prevent possession. She has powdered bark cast away in a banishing ritual or when counteracting a hex. Both Cunningham and Kynes cover more than one species, so both citations are open questions for a curator.
