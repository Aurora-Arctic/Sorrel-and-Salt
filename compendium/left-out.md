Each of these is named by one source only, and was cut so the first roster held 614 rows. ### Further species under a heading that already has a row

Kynes often lists two or three species under one heading. The roster keeps one row per heading (the species another source also names, or else the first one listed), so these were left out. A heading with no row at all says so.

**Read again for the addendum (MB.250).** The owner decided on 2026-10-11 that a species listed within another heading is an entry of its own where its source gives it its own paragraph or its own correspondences. Kynes gives no further species a paragraph of its own: each entry's description, lore and spells are shared by every species it lists. So a species qualifies only where a value in her "Magical Connections" table is tagged to it, as "Mars (wolfsbane)" is under Aconite. Eight qualify and are addendum rows: _Aconitum lycoctonum_ (Northern Wolfsbane), _Cornus mas_, _Corymbia citriodora_, _Jasminum nudiflorum_, _Lavandula latifolia_, _Populus alba_, _Salix babylonica_ and _Viola sororia_. _Galium triflorum_ returns too, because Cunningham heads it ("Bedstraw, Fragrant"), and so do three headings the first roster wrongly counted as having a row: Kynes's Acacia (_Vachellia nilotica_, which the first roster had kept as Cunningham's _Senegalia senegal_), Bluebell (_Hyacinthoides non-scripta_, where the roster's bluebell is Cunningham's harebell) and Cacao (_Theobroma cacao_), as does the Goosefoot heading. Every other species below stays out. ### Headings with no formal name to give

- Cunningham's Grain, Grass, Nuts, Roots and Straw name a category, not a plant, and give no binomial.
- Cunningham's Toadstool names no species. The fungi in the roster are the ones Cunningham and Kynes name by species.
- Cunningham's Molukka gives no binomial. Read again for the addendum, Wikipedia's "Nickernut" describes the Hebridean "Molucca beans" worn as charms, the white ones called "St. Mary's beans", and ties them to several species, so Molukka is an addendum row with `unknown` and no formal name.
- Cunningham's Sagebrush gives only _Artemisia_ spp., which would sit as a genus row over Mugwort, Wormwood, Southernwood and Tarragon.
- Cunningham's Shallot reached only the genus _Allium_, which already holds Onion, Garlic, Chives and Leek.
- Cunningham's Mint is the genus _Mentha_, whose species have rows of their own (Peppermint, Spearmint, Water Mint, Pennyroyal). It was left out of the first roster, but his heading gives mint its own planet, element, deities and powers and calls mint "a general term for any of the Mentha family", and the owner's list of 2026-10-11 names it, so `_Mentha_ spp.` is an addendum row.
- The printed binomial matched no Wikipedia article: Kynes's Siam Benzoin (_Styrax tonkinensis_) and Johnson's Amaryllis, still unmatched when tried again for the addendum. Cunningham's Cloth-of-Gold (_Crocus angustifolia_ as he prints it) and Fleabane (_Inula dysenterica_) were matched on the second try, to _Crocus angustifolius_ and to _Pulicaria dysenterica_ (Wikipedia's "Inula" lists his name as that plant's synonym), and are addendum rows.
- Single-source and cut for space from the first roster, though a formal name was found, and now addendum rows: Cunningham's Love Seed (_Lomatium foeniculaceum_), Parosela (_Dalea_ spp.), Pilot Weed (_Silphium laciniatum_), Dutchman's Breeches (_Dicentra cucullaria_), Yohimbe (_Corynanthe johimbe_) and Wood Rose (_Ipomoea tuberosa_, now _Distimake tuberosus_ on Wikipedia).
- Thought at first to be the same ingredient as a row already present, and now addendum rows: Cunningham's Wild Plum is _Prunus americana_, a different species from his Plum (_Prunus domestica_), with its own heading; and his Iris ("Iris florentina, I. spp.") is about irises in general, so it is the genus _Iris_ spp. beside Orris, the way Mint is a genus row beside the mints. Wikipedia's "Iris florentina" says the identity of Linnaeus's plant "remains unclear", so the species was not chosen.
- Cunningham's Ebony: the binomial he prints, _Diospyros lotus_, is the date-plum, not an ebony, so no formal name could be given without guessing.
- Cunningham's Mimosa is _Acacia dealbata_; Kynes's _Albizia julibrissin_ (also called mimosa) is listed with the further species above.

### Minerals

- **A named sort of stone is an entry, not a form.** The first roster called Herkimer diamond, phantom quartz, Apache tear, snowflake obsidian, stalagmites and stalactites, and the colour sorts of calcite, jasper, agate and tourmaline forms of a mineral already present. That was wrong: the owner decided on 2026-10-11 that each named sort is an entry of its own ([`design-decisions/mb.240-compendium-seed.md`](../../design-decisions/mb.240-compendium-seed.md), "Addendum"), and the addendum adds every one a source names. **A crystal formation is a form**: Hall's crystal shapes (abundance, activator, aperture, ball, barnacle, bridge, buddha, cathedral, channeling, cluster, companion, diamond window, double terminated, egg, elestial, enhydro, etched, generator, goddess, key, layered, life-path, manifestation, occlusion, phantom as a shape, pyramid, record keeper, sceptre, seer stone, self-healed, sheet, spiral, soulmate, square, tabular, transmitter, wand and Vogel wand) are variants beneath Clear Quartz, and a geode (Hall; Cunningham's GEODES, p. 99) is a form of the stone it holds. Hall's apophyllite pyramids, fishtail selenite and drusy chrysocolla are forms beneath their own stones.
- **The same identity as a row already present**: Hall's Anhydrite is her Angelite (Wikipedia redirects angelite to anhydrite); Cunningham's Olivine was listed here as the roster's Peridot, but read again for the addendum he heads Olivine and Peridot apart, writing that the experts he asked disagree and that he includes "both stones in this book under separate articles" (pp. 117–118), so Olivine is an addendum row; Cunningham's Cross Stone is Hall's Chiastolite.
- **Trade names with no formal name read**: Boji stone, Merlinite and Nebula stone (all Hall). Wikipedia's "Boji stone" redirects to a section of "Concretion" that never names it. Azeztulite is the roster's Clear Quartz: Wikipedia's "List of minerals (synonyms)" gives it as a synonym of quartz.
- **Made in a laboratory**: aqua aura quartz was left out of the first roster because Hall's own heading says it is laboratory-made. The owner decided on 2026-10-11 that the treated stones Hall heads are entries, so Aqua Aura, Opal Aura and Rainbow Aura are addendum rows; the others (below) are named by no Wikipedia page.
- **Chrysanthemum stone** (Hall) was left out of the first roster because Wikipedia gave no mineral or rock name for it. Read again for the addendum, the article's mineral infobox is headed "Chrysanthemum Stone", so it is an addendum row under that name.
- **Asbestos** (Cunningham): he writes that it is no longer recommended for magical use, and Wikipedia describes it as a group of fibrous silicate minerals.
- **Tanzanite**: left out of the first roster because neither crystal source gives it a heading. Hall gives it a paragraph of its own under Zoisite, so it is an addendum row.
