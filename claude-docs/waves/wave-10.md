# Wave 10 — Invitations

M7.1's schema landed in Wave 3; this is the behaviour half, once M6's membership exists. **M7.2's token service blocks M7.3 through M7.7.** M7.3 mails the link, so it also needs MB.65's transport from Wave 6. MB.61 decided delivery by email, rewrote M7.3, M7.4 and M7.5 in place for it, and left M7.1's schema, M7.2's token and hash, M7.6's revoke and M7.7's rejections as written ([`design-decisions/mb.61-email-verification-and-delivery.md`](../design-decisions/mb.61-email-verification-and-delivery.md)). **M7.6 (revoke) lands before M7.7 (rejection)**: the rejection path cannot honour a revoked state that does not exist yet. **MB.70, in Wave 8, builds the accept service, the `/invite/[token]` route and the three rejections first**, with only the admin invitation's branch, so M7.5 adds the workspace branch and M7.7 asserts the rejections on it (2026-10-10, on the owner's call). **M2.8 moves here from M2.** Its criterion, "an invited user lands in the workspace they were invited to", is unmeetable before M7.5 exists, so it follows the M7 run, on the post-sign-in landing MB.57 decided in Wave 6. **MB.118 follows M7.3**, whose invitation is the last of the site's mail to exist, and **MB.119 closes the wave**, reviewing the coven section once invitations land; both are M5.4's design reviews.

MB.30 could have re-scoped the whole wave. Had Better Auth's organization plugin carried invitations, M7.2 through M7.7 would have become hooks over its endpoints, and the hash-only token invariant would have softened to single-use, expiring and CSPRNG. It was not adopted ([`design-decisions/mb.30-organization-plugin.md`](../design-decisions/mb.30-organization-plugin.md)). MW.10 is retired (MB.31).
