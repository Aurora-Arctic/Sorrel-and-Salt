# M0.7 — Category-group and safety tokens

**Status:** decided · **Date:** 2026-09-07 · **Builds on:**
[m0.6-typography-and-palette.md](./m0.6-typography-and-palette.md)

M0.6 committed this task to _deriving_ its colours from the base palette rather than
picking alongside it. This is the record of how that was done and what every resulting
pairing measures, so M0.8's `chip()` and `badge()` mixins — and anything later that adds
a group — inherit the reasoning rather than the hex values alone.

---

## The decision

**Eight category-group colours**, one rotation of `$sorrel` on a half-step offset, with
saturation lifted per theme and lightness trimmed per group to hold 4.5:1.

**Two badge palettes**, deliberately unequal: `safety` is loud and takes the sealing-wax
hue; `low-stock` is quiet and takes the muted ink, spending no hue at all.

**Two chip treatments and two badge treatments**, settled here so M0.8's mixins inherit
the shapes rather than inventing them — see [Chip and badge shapes](#chip-and-badge-shapes)
below, which is also where the one non-obvious call on this page lives.

---

## Category groups

### The hues: one rotation of sorrel, offset by half a step

The eight sit at odd multiples of 22.5° from `$sorrel` (96°). Taking only the **odd**
multiples is the whole trick — it puts every group on a half-step, which guarantees none
of them lands on the accent hue (96°, `$sorrel`) or the secondary hue (8°, `$wax`). Both
are already claimed by UI chrome, and a category chip wearing the accent colour would
read as _selected_ when it is not.

Hue assignment is semantic, not list order:

| Group                | Slug         | Step | Hue    | Reads as     |
| -------------------- | ------------ | ---- | ------ | ------------ |
| Protection & defense | `protection` | 5    | 208.5° | steel blue   |
| Cleansing & release  | `cleansing`  | 3    | 163.5° | teal, water  |
| Prosperity & work    | `prosperity` | 15   | 73.5°  | gold         |
| Love & connection    | `love`       | 11   | 343.5° | rose         |
| Mind & spirit        | `mind`       | 7    | 253.5° | indigo       |
| Wellbeing            | `wellbeing`  | 1    | 118.5° | jade         |
| Craft & change       | `craft`      | 9    | 298.5° | violet       |
| Practice & place     | `practice`   | 13   | 28.5°  | hearth amber |

Slugs are the §6 group names shortened to the identifying word; the category seed's
`group` field matches them.

### Saturation is per theme, and light needs far more of it

`$sorrel` sits at S35, which is too muted to distinguish eight hues at chip size. The
lift is **+14% on dark** (landing near S49) and **+50% on light** (near S85) — the same
lift for all eight in a theme, which is what keeps them reading as one family while
letting each theme set its own intensity.

The asymmetry is not arbitrary and is the single most important thing on this page:

> To clear 4.5:1 against parchment, a colour has to stay **dark**. A dark colour at
> moderate saturation reads as **mud**, not as colour. Near-full saturation is what buys
> the chroma back. Dark theme has the opposite problem — its colours are _lightened_ to
> clear the ratio, and moderate saturation already reads clearly there.

A first pass at +28% on light was rejected in review as washed out, which is exactly this
effect. **Do not "harmonise" the two lifts to one number** — it will re-mud the light
theme.

### Lightness is trimmed per group, only as far as the floor demands

Equal HSL lightness is not equal perceived lightness. Indigo needs a **+34%** lift to
clear 4.5:1 on soot where jade needs **+9%**. On the light side, saturating costs
contrast against a pale ground, so three groups need darkening to stay above the floor.
Saturation and lightness are traded off group by group rather than set once.

### Contrast — every group, both themes

WCAG AA for text is 4.5:1. Nothing sits below it against **either** surface of its own
theme. `dark` and `light` are the lightness trims; ratios are `vs page / vs card`.

quietest, because those hues have the least chroma available at low lightness. That is a
property of the hue circle, not a tuning mistake.

## Badge palettes

Two badges appear on an `IngredientCard`, and they are deliberately **not** equals.

**`safety`** is a warning — an ingredient flagged toxic or unsafe to burn (story 53). It
wears `$wax`, the loudest hue in the palette. M0.6 earmarked `$wax` for exactly this.

> **Amended M0.32.** The saturation lift was originally +36% dark / +35% light (matching
> the groups). At the user's request it was dialled back to **+22% both themes** — the fill
> read as near-fluorescent rather than sealing-wax. Only saturation moved; the +18% dark
> lightness lift is unchanged (the near-black label has little headroom). New fills:
> `#df4e37` dark, `#a02c1a` light. All four pairings still clear AA — the tables below carry
> the new numbers, with the originals struck through where it matters.

Its dark ink is lifted only **+18%** in lightness. A _text_ colour on soot needs far more —
an earlier pass used +30% — but the safety badge is drawn as a solid fill, where the
contrast that matters is its label's rather than its own against the card. At +30% it was
readable and visibly washed out; at +18% it keeps its chroma. (An earlier pass here also
warned that pushing _saturation_ too far reads as a crude primary; the M0.32 amendment
moves the other way — **less** saturation — for the same reason.)

**`low-stock`** is an inventory state, not an alarm (story 54), so it takes the **muted
ink** — `$chalk-muted` / `$ink-muted` — and is left unsaturated. Two things follow, and
both are the reason for the choice:

- Exactly one badge in the app is loud, so "loud" keeps meaning something.
- It spends **no hue**. That matters, because after the eight groups every hue not
  already reserved is spoken for.

This was flagged in review as the arguable call: with the groups now considerably more
saturated, low-stock sits further back than it used to. That is the intended order of
attention. If it ever reads as too recessive, the fix is to give it the hearth amber and
accept that low-stock and `practice` share a hue family — not to desaturate the groups.

### A palette declares its treatment, and emits only that treatment's parts

There are two treatments, and **a palette emits only the parts its own treatment uses**:

| Treatment | Parts                  | What it is                                         |
| --------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| `solid`   | `fill`                 | the ink fills the badge; the label inverts onto it |
| `tinted`  | `fg` · `bg` · `border` | the ink mixed back into the card surface           |

`safety` is `solid`; `low-stock` is `tinted`. This is not tidiness — the two treatments
want **opposite things from an ink**. Solid wants it saturated and near mid-lightness, so
a dark label reads on it. Tinted wants it far from the surface, so it still registers at
12–14% strength. Safety's ink now satisfies the first and fails the second: as a tinted
badge its `fg` on `bg` would measure **3.85** and its edge **2.78**, both under floor. So
those parts do not exist for it, and `badge-token('safety', 'bg')` is a compile error
naming the treatment rather than a token that quietly fails WCAG.

There is deliberately no part for the inverted label — see `$text-on-color` below.

**Tinted mix weights** are per theme, not per palette: a tint over near-black needs
slightly more ink to register than the same tint over parchment. A badge tints the surface
it actually sits on rather than carrying a hardcoded panel colour, so it stays correct if a
surface is ever retuned.

| Part     | Dark | Light | Requirement                        |
| -------- | ---- | ----- | ---------------------------------- |
| `bg`     | 14%  | 12%   | —                                  |
| `border` | 70%  | 75%   | 3:1 vs both surfaces (WCAG 1.4.11) |

The `fg` requirement is 4.5:1 against **its own fill**, not against the card — the ink
never sits on the bare surface.

Every hex and ratio below was read back out of the **compiled CSS**, not from the model
used to choose them — `color.mix()` blends before rounding, so two of the light mixes land
one 8-bit unit off what a naive channel blend predicts.

**`safety`, solid** — the label is `$text-on-color`, and the fill is its own boundary:

| Theme | `fill`                    | Label     | label vs fill   | fill vs card    | fill vs page    |
| ----- | ------------------------- | --------- | --------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| dark  | `#df4e37` (was `#ef4226`) | `#14120e` | 4.71 (was 4.88) | 4.28 (was 4.43) | 4.71 (was 4.88) |
| light | `#a02c1a` (was `#ad230d`) | `#efe9da` | 6.02 (was 5.74) | 6.69 (was 6.38) | 6.02 (was 5.74) |

**`low-stock`, tinted:**

| Theme | `fg`      | `bg`      | `border`  | fg vs bg | border vs card | border vs page |
| ----- | --------- | --------- | --------- | -------- | -------------- | -------------- |
| dark  | `#c7b487` | `#373126` | `#958665` | 6.32     | 4.75           | 5.23           |
| light | `#5e5645` | `#e6e2d6` | `#847d6f` | 5.60     | 3.75           | 3.37           |

## Chip and badge shapes

M0.8 writes the `chip()` and `badge()` mixins. The _shapes_ were settled here, against the
real colours, because a shape that cannot hold 4.5:1 is not a styling decision to discover
later — one of them nearly wasn't.

Both loud treatments make the same move — the colour becomes the ground and the label
inverts off it — and neither carries a dot. A dot in front of a chip or a badge is a second
mark for information the fill and the edge already carry.

### Badges: safety solid, low-stock tinted

A badge is square-cornered. That is what keeps it from reading as a chip now that chips are
pills.

**`safety` is a solid fill of its ink**, label inverted onto it, and it draws **no border**:
a solid fill is its own boundary, clearing 3:1 against both surfaces on its own account
(4.28 dark, 6.69 light after the M0.32 amendment). See the `solid` table above.

**`low-stock` keeps the tinted fill and the 3:1 edge.** It is now the quiet one in shape as
well as in colour, which pushes it further back still. That remains the intended order of
attention and remains the one call worth arguing with.

### Chips: a pill, always edged, loud or quiet

Both variants are the same size and carry the group colour as a 1px edge at
`border-radius: 999px`, so a row can mix them without breaking rhythm. `quiet` leaves the
ground transparent and lets the label wear the colour; **`loud` fills solid with that colour
and inverts the label onto it**.

| Variant | Ground      | Edge  | Label            | Worst label | Worst edge |
| ------- | ----------- | ----- | ---------------- | ----------- | ---------- |
| `loud`  | group       | group | `$text-on-color` | 4.74        | 4.62       |
| `quiet` | transparent | group | group            | 4.62        | 4.62       |

Solid is not just the shape that was asked for; it is the only one that works. **A
low-opacity wash of a group's own colour cannot carry a coloured label:**

> The eight were tuned to land _just_ above 4.5:1 — the tightest is 4.62 — so tinting the
> ground with the same colour closes exactly the gap they were tuned for. The largest wash
> that still clears the floor is **2% on dark, 3% on light**: invisible, which would make
> loud and quiet indistinguishable and defeat the variant. A washed chip therefore needs a
> label that is _not_ the group colour, or a new, deeper token per group. Do not try to buy
> it back by shaving the wash weight.

Going solid sidesteps that, and it introduces no new arithmetic: **a solid chip's label
ratio is the `vs page` column already tabulated above**, because the inverted label _is_ the
page surface. The two questions have the same answer. Worst pairing in the set is
`wellbeing` at 4.74.

Neither variant needs a group token this task does not already ship.

## How it is built

Both sets are **maps plus a resolver function**, not values spelled out twice:

```scss
$category-groups: (
  'protection': (step: 5, dark: 22%, light: 0%),
  // …
);

@function category-group-color($slug, $theme) { … }
@function badge-palette-color($palette, $theme, $part) { … }
@function badge-parts($palette) { … }   // the treatment's parts, in one place
```

`_mixins.scss` has one `semantic-tokens($theme)` mixin that loops both maps and emits
`--group-*` and `--badge-*`. `theme-dark` and `theme-light` each include it with their own
name. **The two themes are generated from one source and cannot drift** — the same reason
M0.6 made `theme-light` a mixin rather than a duplicated block. Adding a ninth group or
retuning a mix weight is one edit in one place.

Components reach the tokens through two functions rather than a variable each:

```scss
color: category-group('mind');
background: badge-token('safety', 'fill');
color: $text-on-color; // the label on that fill
```

A function rejects a typo at compile time, where `var(--group-protectoin)` would fail
silently in the browser. Five error paths are exercised: unknown group, unknown palette,
a part its palette's treatment does not have, unknown theme, and — the one worth having —
`badge-token('safety', 'bg')`, which names the treatment in the message rather than
handing back a token measuring 3.85:1.

### `$text-on-color` is an alias, not a token

The inverted label on any solid fill — the loud chip and the safety badge — adds **no custom
property at all**. It is the ink's own ground: `$soot` on dark, `$parchment` on light, which
is to say `--surface-page`, already shipped by M0.6.

```scss
$text-on-color: var(--surface-page);
```

An intermediate pass gave every badge palette an `on` part. Once chips needed the same
label, that would have been a property per palette **and** per group: sixteen spellings of
two values, sixteen chances to drift. The answer does not depend on what the fill is, so it
is one alias.

Nothing outside `_variables.scss` and the theme mixins names a raw hue — M0.6's rule,
unchanged.

## Alternatives considered

**Eight independently-chosen hues.** Rejected by the task itself, and rightly: eight
colours picked by eye against eight different backgrounds do not read as a set, and every
future addition restarts the argument.

**Even multiples of 45° off `$sorrel`.** The obvious rotation, and it was the first pass.
It puts a group exactly on the accent hue (96°) and another within 2° of the secondary
(8°). The half-step offset costs nothing and removes both collisions.

**One saturation lift for both themes.** Tried at +28%. Dark was fine; light was rejected
in review as washed out, for the structural reason above. The asymmetry is load-bearing.

**Pushing light to +62% (S97).** Holds contrast and is available if more intensity is ever
wanted. Rejected at this size as crude — near-primary colours against parchment stop
looking like a herbal.

**A tinted `low-stock` amber.** Rejected to keep one loud badge and to avoid spending a
hue the groups need. See the note above for when to revisit.

**A washed loud chip** — the group colour at low opacity behind a coloured label. The
obvious reading of "loud", and it fails: 3.90:1 at a visible wash weight, 2–3% at a passing
one. Solid replaced it. Recorded because the failure is not obvious from the swatches — the
group colours look like they have room, and they do not.

**An `on` part per badge palette.** Built, then removed once chips needed the same label.
One alias replaced sixteen properties.

**Emitting `fg`/`bg`/`border` for `safety` anyway,** unused. Rejected: they measure 3.85 and
2.78 with the current ink, so they would be tokens that fail the contrast floor sitting in
the same namespace as tokens that pass. A compile error is better than a footgun.

## What this commits later tasks to

- **M0.8**'s `chip()` and `badge()` mixins consume `category-group()` and `badge-token()`.
  `badge()`'s palette argument is a `$badge-palettes` key — `'safety'` or `'low-stock'` —
  not a colour. Both mixins take a loud/quiet variant, and the shapes are the ones above:
  a pill for a chip, a square corner for a badge, a solid fill for loud safety.
- **A ninth badge palette** declares `treatment: solid` or `treatment: tinted`, and that
  choice constrains its ink: solid needs 4.5:1 against `$text-on-color` (the page surface)
  and 3:1 against both surfaces; tinted needs 4.5:1 of `fg` on `bg` and 3:1 of `border`.
  An ink rarely satisfies both — safety's does not — so pick the treatment first.
- **A ninth category group** is one map entry plus a contrast check; it is not a code
  change anywhere else.
- **The seed's `categories.group` values** must match the eight slugs above.
