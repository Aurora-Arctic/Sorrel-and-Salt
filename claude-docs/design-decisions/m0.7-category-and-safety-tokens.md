# M0.7 — Category-group and safety tokens

**Status:** decided · **Date:** 2026-09-07 · **Builds on:**
[m0.6-typography-and-palette.md](./m0.6-typography-and-palette.md)

M0.6 committed this task to _deriving_ its colours from the base palette rather than
picking alongside it. This is the record of how that was done and what every resulting
pairing measures, so M0.8's `chip()` and `badge()` mixins — and anything later that adds
a group — inherit the reasoning rather than the hex values alone.

---

## The decision

**Eight category-group colours**, one rotation of `$sorrel` on a half-step offset, with
saturation lifted per theme and lightness trimmed per group to hold 4.5:1.

**Two badge palettes**, deliberately unequal: `safety` is loud and takes the sealing-wax
hue; `low-stock` is quiet and takes the muted ink, spending no hue at all.

---

## Category groups

### The hues: one rotation of sorrel, offset by half a step

The eight sit at odd multiples of 22.5° from `$sorrel` (96°). Taking only the **odd**
multiples is the whole trick — it puts every group on a half-step, which guarantees none
of them lands on the accent hue (96°, `$sorrel`) or the secondary hue (8°, `$wax`). Both
are already claimed by UI chrome, and a category chip wearing the accent colour would
read as _selected_ when it is not.

Hue assignment is semantic, not list order:

| Group                | Slug         | Step | Hue    | Reads as     |
| -------------------- | ------------ | ---- | ------ | ------------ |
| Protection & defense | `protection` | 5    | 208.5° | steel blue   |
| Cleansing & release  | `cleansing`  | 3    | 163.5° | teal, water  |
| Prosperity & work    | `prosperity` | 15   | 73.5°  | gold         |
| Love & connection    | `love`       | 11   | 343.5° | rose         |
| Mind & spirit        | `mind`       | 7    | 253.5° | indigo       |
| Wellbeing            | `wellbeing`  | 1    | 118.5° | jade         |
| Craft & change       | `craft`      | 9    | 298.5° | violet       |
| Practice & place     | `practice`   | 13   | 28.5°  | hearth amber |

Slugs are the §6 group names shortened to the identifying word; the category seed's
`group` field matches them.

### Saturation is per theme, and light needs far more of it

`$sorrel` sits at S35, which is too muted to distinguish eight hues at chip size. The
lift is **+14% on dark** (landing near S49) and **+50% on light** (near S85) — the same
lift for all eight in a theme, which is what keeps them reading as one family while
letting each theme set its own intensity.

The asymmetry is not arbitrary and is the single most important thing on this page:

> To clear 4.5:1 against parchment, a colour has to stay **dark**. A dark colour at
> moderate saturation reads as **mud**, not as colour. Near-full saturation is what buys
> the chroma back. Dark theme has the opposite problem — its colours are _lightened_ to
> clear the ratio, and moderate saturation already reads clearly there.

A first pass at +28% on light was rejected in review as washed out, which is exactly this
effect. **Do not "harmonise" the two lifts to one number** — it will re-mud the light
theme.

### Lightness is trimmed per group, only as far as the floor demands

Equal HSL lightness is not equal perceived lightness. Indigo needs a **+34%** lift to
clear 4.5:1 on soot where jade needs **+9%**. On the light side, saturating costs
contrast against a pale ground, so three groups need darkening to stay above the floor.
Saturation and lightness are traded off group by group rather than set once.

property of the hue circle, not a tuning mistake.

## Badge palettes

Two badges appear on an `IngredientCard`, and they are deliberately **not** equals.

**`safety`** is a warning — an ingredient flagged toxic or unsafe to burn (story 53). It
wears `$wax`, the loudest hue in the palette, saturated alongside the groups (+30% dark,
+35% light). M0.6 earmarked `$wax` for exactly this.

**`low-stock`** is an inventory state, not an alarm (story 54), so it takes the **muted
ink** — `$chalk-muted` / `$ink-muted` — and is left unsaturated. Two things follow, and
both are the reason for the choice:

- Exactly one badge in the app is loud, so "loud" keeps meaning something.
- It spends **no hue**. That matters, because after the eight groups every hue not
  already reserved is spoken for.

This was flagged in review as the arguable call: with the groups now considerably more
saturated, low-stock sits further back than it used to. That is the intended order of
attention. If it ever reads as too recessive, the fix is to give it the hearth amber and
accept that low-stock and `practice` share a hue family — not to desaturate the groups.

### Fill and edge are the ink mixed back into the card

A badge tints the surface it actually sits on rather than carrying a hardcoded panel
colour, so it stays correct if a surface is ever retuned. Weights are per theme, not per
palette: a tint over near-black needs slightly more ink to register than the same tint
over parchment.

| Part     | Dark | Light | Requirement                        |
| -------- | ---- | ----- | ---------------------------------- |
| `bg`     | 14%  | 12%   | —                                  |
| `border` | 70%  | 75%   | 3:1 vs both surfaces (WCAG 1.4.11) |

The `fg` requirement is 4.5:1 against **its own fill**, not against the card — the ink
never sits on the bare surface.

Every hex and ratio in the table below was read back out of the **compiled CSS**, not from
the model used to choose them — `color.mix()` blends before rounding, so two of the light
mixes land one 8-bit unit off what a naive channel blend predicts.

| ----------- | ----- | --------- | --------- | --------- | -------- | -------------- | -------------- |
| `safety`    | dark  | `#ee7865` | `#3c2921` | `#b05c4d` | 4.90     | 3.63           | 3.99           |

## How it is built

Both sets are **maps plus a resolver function**, not values spelled out twice:

```scss
$category-groups: (
  'protection': (step: 5, dark: 22%, light: 0%),
  // …
);

@function category-group-color($slug, $theme) { … }
@function badge-palette-color($palette, $theme, $part) { … }
```

`_mixins.scss` has one `semantic-tokens($theme)` mixin that loops both maps and emits
`--group-*` and `--badge-*`. `theme-dark` and `theme-light` each include it with their own
name. **The two themes are generated from one source and cannot drift** — the same reason
M0.6 made `theme-light` a mixin rather than a duplicated block. Adding a ninth group or
retuning a mix weight is one edit in one place.

Components reach the tokens through two functions rather than 14 variables:

```scss
color: category-group('mind');
background: badge-token('safety', 'bg');
```

A function rejects a typo at compile time, where `var(--group-protectoin)` would fail
silently in the browser. All four error paths are exercised: unknown group, unknown
palette, unknown part, unknown theme.

Nothing outside `_variables.scss` and the theme mixins names a raw hue — M0.6's rule,
unchanged.

## Alternatives considered

**Eight independently-chosen hues.** Rejected by the task itself, and rightly: eight
colours picked by eye against eight different backgrounds do not read as a set, and every
future addition restarts the argument.

**Even multiples of 45° off `$sorrel`.** The obvious rotation, and it was the first pass.
It puts a group exactly on the accent hue (96°) and another within 2° of the secondary
(8°). The half-step offset costs nothing and removes both collisions.

**One saturation lift for both themes.** Tried at +28%. Dark was fine; light was rejected
in review as washed out, for the structural reason above. The asymmetry is load-bearing.

**Pushing light to +62% (S97).** Holds contrast and is available if more intensity is ever
wanted. Rejected at this size as crude — near-primary colours against parchment stop
looking like a herbal.

**A tinted `low-stock` amber.** Rejected to keep one loud badge and to avoid spending a
hue the groups need. See the note above for when to revisit.

---

## What this commits later tasks to

- **M0.8**'s `chip()` and `badge()` mixins consume `category-group()` and `badge-token()`.
  `badge()`'s palette argument is a `$badge-palettes` key — `'safety'` or `'low-stock'` —
  not a colour.
- **A ninth category group** is one map entry plus a contrast check; it is not a code
  change anywhere else.
- **The seed's `categories.group` values** must match the eight slugs above.
