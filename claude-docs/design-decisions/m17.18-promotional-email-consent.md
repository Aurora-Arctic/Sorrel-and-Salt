# M17.18 — Promotional Email Consent

Decided by the owner on 2026-11-07 while M17.18 (#1598) was scoped. This record holds each decision, its reason, its sources and the alternatives weighed. The sourced research behind it is [`email/promotional-consent.md`](../email/promotional-consent.md); every source below was fetched on 2026-11-07, and the findings document says which claims rest on a search summary, which are the researcher's own reading and which are unsourced.

The site sends no promotional mail today. Every template is transactional (see the classification in the findings document). This record decides how consent is gathered and recorded before the first promotional mail, and what else the owner chose to add about mail while scoping.

This record does not edit `DESIGN.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `documents/privacy.md` or `documents/code-of-ethics.md`. Task M22.5 makes the architecture correction in CLAUDE.md and DESIGN.md; the two documents under `documents/` change only when the owner edits them, from the lines listed under "Lines for the Site Documents" below.

## 1. One Global Standard

**Decision.** Every user gets the same consent standard, whatever country they live in: a separate unticked box for promotional email, a request that names the sender, the purpose and the right to withdraw, a stored record of who agreed, when and to what wording, an unsubscribe in every promotional mail, and the sender's identity and postal address in every promotional mail.

**Reason.** The site does not record where a user lives, and sign-in is open to anyone invited, so the US, UK, EU and Canadian rules can all reach a user. The strictest combination of the statutes read satisfies all four. This standard is a synthesis by the researcher and the owner, not a quotation from any source.

**Sources.**

- FTC CAN-SPAM guide: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business (fetched 2026-11-07)
- PECR regulation 22: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/22 (fetched 2026-11-07)
- GDPR: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679 (fetched 2026-11-07)
- CASL: https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-2.html (fetched 2026-11-07)

**Alternatives weighed.** A standard for each country, chosen by the user's location: rejected, because the site would have to collect and trust a location it does not hold. The US opt-out standard alone: rejected, because the UK, EU and Canada need prior consent. Relying on the UK soft opt-in: rejected, because it needs a sale or negotiations and a free invited account has neither (the researcher's inference; the ICO's position is unsourced).

## 2. Consent Counts at Once, Not by Double Opt-In

**Decision.** Ticking the box and pressing Save is the consent. The site sends no confirmation mail first.

**Reason.** No statute read requires double opt-in. The GDPR needs the controller to be able to demonstrate consent (Article 7), and the EDPB allows a record of consent statements as the proof (guidelines 05/2020, paragraph 108). CASL puts the burden of proof on the sender (section 13). The user is signed in, with a verified address, when they tick the box, so the address is already confirmed.

**This departs from M3AAWG best practice.** M3AAWG (an industry body) said in 2015 that "the best option is a 'double opt-in' process." The owner accepts the departure. The cost is that the record proves a signed-in account ticked a box, not that the address's holder read a mail. The signed-in, verified-address condition is what the decision rests on.

**Sources.**

- M3AAWG: https://www.m3aawg.org/news/updated-m3aawg-best-practices-for-senders-urge-opt-in-only-mailings-address-sender-transparency (fetched 2026-11-07)
- EDPB Guidelines 05/2020: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202005_consent_en.pdf (fetched 2026-11-07)
- CASL section 13: https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-2.html (fetched 2026-11-07)

**Alternatives weighed.** Double opt-in: stronger proof and best practice, but one more mail and one more step for every consenting user, and no law read requires it. Revisit if a complaint rate or a provider rule demands it.

## 3. A One-Time Prompt and the `/account` Setting

**Decision.** The site asks once, after sign-in, with an unticked box and two buttons, Save and No Thanks. The same choice lives permanently in a section of `/account`. Consent is never part of sign-up or the terms.

**Reason.** A box bundled with the terms is presumed not freely given (EDPB paragraph 13). A pre-ticked box or silence is not consent (GDPR Recital 32; the ICO and CRTC positions are search summaries). Granularity means a separate purpose gets a separate request (EDPB paragraph 44).

**Sources.**

- EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 (URL under decision 2)
- GDPR Recital 32 (URL under decision 1)
- ICO how-do-we-comply page: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/how-do-we-comply-with-the-pecr-electronic-mail-marketing-rules/ (search summary, 2026-11-07)
- CRTC Bulletin 2012-549: https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/2012/2012-549.htm (search summary, 2026-11-07)

**Alternatives weighed.** A box at sign-up: rejected as bundled. The `/account` setting alone: simpler, but most users never look, so the site would hold almost no consents. A prompt on every sign-in until answered: rejected as pressure that undermines "freely given".

## 4. The Wording, Versioned

**Decision.** The request wording is stored in the code with a version number. A grant records the version the user saw. A change to the wording is a new version.

**Reason.** The controller must be able to show "the information provided to the data subject at the time" (EDPB paragraph 108), and CASL's regulation requires the request to state the requester's name, a mailing address plus a contact method and a statement that consent can be withdrawn (SOR/2012-36 section 4). The wording must name the sender and the type of mail (ICO, search summary).

**Sources.** EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 (decision 2); SOR/2012-36: https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2012-36/FullText.html (fetched 2026-11-07).

**Alternatives weighed.** Storing the full wording text on each row: heavier and gives no extra proof once versions are kept in the repository. No version: cannot show what a user agreed to after the wording changes.

## 5. The Record

**Decision.** Each consent row holds who (the user), when, the wording version, and the source: `prompt`, `account`, `link` or `one-click`. A withdrawal records when, by whom and by which source.

**Reason.** The ICO says to record "who, when, how" (search summary); the EDPB says how and when consent was obtained and what the person was told (paragraph 108); CASL puts the proof on the sender (section 13).

**Sources.** ICO electronic mail marketing guide: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/ (search summary, 2026-11-07); EDPB (decision 2); CASL (decision 1).

**Alternatives weighed.** A single boolean on `users`: loses who, when and what wording. An append-only event log with no live row: complete history, but every send would have to reduce it; one live row with withdrawal columns, plus the audit trail the audited table already keeps, serves both.

## 6. An Audited Table Through `withAudit`

**Decision.** Consent lives in its own table that spreads `...auditColumns` and is written only through `withAudit`.

**Reason.** CLAUDE.md rule 3: all writes go through `withAudit(session, fn)`, which takes the audit ids from the session. A table task comes before the behaviour task (M22.1 before M22.3).

**Sources.** CLAUDE.md, "Non-negotiable architecture rules", rule 3, and "Conventions" ("A table task, then a behaviour task").

**Alternatives weighed.** A column on `users`: it does not hold the history and widens a hot table.

## 7. Withdrawal: the RFC 8058 Route, a Link and `/account`

**Decision.** A user can withdraw three ways: one click from the mail client (RFC 8058), a link in the mail body, and the `/account` setting. All three withdraw at once.

**Reason.** It must be as easy to withdraw as to give consent (GDPR Article 7; EDPB paragraph 114). Gmail requires RFC 8058 one-click for promotional mail from bulk senders, and exempts transactional mail. Honouring at once is inside every deadline read: CAN-SPAM 10 business days, CASL 10 business days, Yahoo 2 days, Gmail's recommended 48 hours. The mechanism must work for at least 30 days (CAN-SPAM) or 60 days (CASL), so the token lasts at least 60 days.

**Sources.**

- RFC 8058: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8058 (fetched 2026-11-07)
- RFC 2369: https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2369 (fetched 2026-11-07)
- Gmail: https://support.google.com/a/answer/14229414 (fetched 2026-11-07)
- Yahoo: https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/ (fetched 2026-11-07)
- CASL section 11: https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-2.html (fetched 2026-11-07)

**Alternatives weighed.** A `mailto:` header only: Gmail says it does not meet the one-click requirement. A link that needs sign-in: not one click, and it fails the "no cookies" rule of RFC 8058. The `/account` setting alone: not enough for Gmail's bulk rule.

## 8. No Promotional Mail to a Paused Account

**Decision.** An account with a live site-access pause receives no promotional mail. Its consent row is kept, and mail resumes when the pause lifts.

**Reason.** Transactional mail still reaches a paused account (`documents/privacy.md` says so), because the user needs to know. Promotional mail is optional for the sender, so there is no reason to send it to someone whose access the site has paused. Keeping the consent avoids asking again after a short pause. The owner's reason is the pause's purpose; no source applies.

**Sources.** None; an owner decision. See `claude-docs/design-decisions/m15.52-site-access-pause.md` for the pause.

**Alternatives weighed.** Send to paused accounts: rejected for the reason above. Withdraw consent when paused: rejected, because it erases the user's choice for a pause that may be a day.

## 9. A Sessionless `/api/unsubscribe` and the Rule-1 Exception

**Decision.** One-click unsubscribe is a `POST /api/unsubscribe` route that needs no session. A hashed token authorises it. It is written as a seeded system account through `withAudit`, rate limited, and never returns a redirect. CLAUDE.md rule 1 and DESIGN.md name it as a sessionless exception.

**Reason.** RFC 8058 says the POST "MUST NOT include cookies, HTTP authorization, or any other context information" and the sender "MUST NOT return an HTTPS redirect," so the call cannot go through the signed-in `/api/graphql` path. The closest existing model is `replyToSupportRequestByViewToken`: 32 random bytes, only the SHA-256 hash stored, rate limited, written as a seeded system account (CLAUDE.md rule 3). Rule 1 lists its sessionless exceptions by name, so a new one is a recorded change to CLAUDE.md and DESIGN.md, made by M22.5.

**Sources.** RFC 8058 (decision 7); CLAUDE.md rules 1 and 3, and "Invitation tokens" in the domain invariants.

**Alternatives weighed.** A GraphQL mutation: cannot satisfy RFC 8058's no-context rule. A server action: rule 1 forbids it. No one-click route (link only): fails Gmail's rule for promotional mail from a bulk sender.

## 10. The Postal Address Before the First Send

**Decision.** The postal address is set in an environment variable before the first promotional send. A promotional send is refused, with a named error, while it is unset.

**Reason.** CAN-SPAM: "Your message must include your valid physical postal address." A PO box or registered private mailbox qualifies. CASL's request also requires a mailing address. Refusing the send is a mechanical guard; a reminder would be only a hope.

**Sources.** FTC guide (decision 1); SOR/2012-36 section 4 (decision 4).

**Alternatives weighed.** A database setting a keeper edits: more screens for one fixed value. A hard-coded address in the template: wrong to change in code. The owner supplies the value, and it is never written into this repository.

## 11. Support Mail Settings

**Decision.** All four support mails can be switched off by the user from `/account`: the received mail, the resolved mail, the closing-soon mail and the reply notice. Each is on by default. Turning the reply notice off shows a warning that the user will not be emailed when staff reply. A signed-out sender has no account, so their support mails always send.

**Reason.** The owner's choice, for control over the user's own inbox. The warning exists because the reply notice is the only way a user learns of a reply other than opening the support page. These are transactional mails, so no law requires the switch; it is a courtesy that costs the site nothing in compliance.

**Sources.** None; an owner decision. The mails are described in [`email.md`](../email.md).

**Alternatives weighed.** Switch off the reply notice never: the owner chose the warning. One switch for all support mail: coarser than the owner wanted. Letting a signed-out sender switch mail off: there is no account to hold the switch.

## 12. Every Other Mail Always Sends

**Decision.** Verification, address change, account exists, password, lock, invitation, pause, time-out and document-update mail always send, and the account page lists them.

**Reason.** They are security or account-standing mail (FTC categories 2 and 3), and the user needs them. A paused account is still emailed, as `documents/privacy.md` says.

**Sources.** 16 CFR 316.3(c): https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/316.3 (fetched 2026-11-07).

**Alternatives weighed.** Letting users switch off pause or document mail: the user could miss a change to their access or the terms. Letting users switch off security mail: a security failure.

## 13. Refreshing Consent Is Left to a Later Call

**Decision.** The site does not re-ask for consent at intervals. The owner decides later whether to.

**Reason.** The EDPB only recommends refreshing "as a best practice" (paragraph 111). No law read sets a lifetime for consent. A later task can add it.

**Sources.** EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 (decision 2).

**Alternatives weighed.** Re-ask every 12 or 24 months: more friction and no legal duty found.

## 14. The Build Work Is Its Own Wave

**Decision.** The build tasks go on Wave 22, "Email consent and notifications", and not on Wave 21.

**Reason.** The owner's call of 2026-11-07. The work is seven tasks and has no dependency on billing, so it should not wait for or slow Wave 21. Wave 22 is set out in [`waves/wave-22.md`](../waves/wave-22.md) and [`tasks/m22.md`](../tasks/m22.md).

**Sources.** None; an owner decision.

**Alternatives weighed.** Wave 21: it would fold email work into billing.

## Proposed Tasks

The owner approved all seven on 2026-11-07, and they are minted on Wave 22. They are written in full in [`tasks/m22.md`](../tasks/m22.md) and [`waves/wave-22.md`](../waves/wave-22.md). Order: tables, then behaviour, then pages, then stories. The estimate is in each heading.

### M22.1 — Add the Email Consent Tables (2h)

_Story:_ As the site keeper, I want the consent record and the unsubscribe tokens to have tables of their own before any behaviour uses them, so that the consent service and the one-click route are built on a schema that is already reviewed.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- Both tables spread `...auditColumns`, carry the `updated_at` trigger in their own migration, and every unique index is partial (`WHERE deleted_at IS NULL`)
- `email_consents` allows one live row per user, and a CHECK limits the source columns to the four values
- `email_unsubscribe_tokens` stores only the hash, never the token, and holds an expiry
- The migration is expand-only: no destructive DDL, so no `.ack.md` sidecar
- Catalogue-introspection tests prove the audit columns, the trigger, the partial indexes and the CHECK, and a repository test proves a soft-deleted row is never returned

### M22.2 — Add the Mail Preferences Table (1h)

_Story:_ As the site keeper, I want a table for each user's support-mail switches, so that the preferences service has a reviewed schema to build on.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- `mail_preferences` spreads `...auditColumns`, carries the `updated_at` trigger, and has a partial unique index on the user
- All four booleans default to on and are NOT NULL
- The migration is expand-only
- Catalogue-introspection tests prove the audit columns, the trigger, the defaults and the partial index

### M22.3 — Record and Withdraw Promotional Email Consent (4h)

_Story:_ As a user, I want my agreement to promotional email recorded exactly and withdrawable at any time, so that the site can prove what I agreed to and stops when I say stop.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- `recordPromotionalConsent` and `withdrawPromotionalConsent` write through `withAudit` and take the user from the session, never from a request body
- A recipient finder filters on live consent, on a verified address and on no live site-access pause in SQL, and a paused account keeps its consent row
- A second grant while one is live changes nothing; a grant after a withdrawal makes a new row
- An authorisation test asserts a user cannot read or change another user's consent by direct id, and that user B could have, by showing the row exists
- A test proves an observer (fixture C) can consent and a signed-out caller is refused
- Tests cover every source value and the audit ids

### M22.4 — Honour Support Mail Preferences (3h)

_Story:_ As a user, I want to turn off the support mails I do not need, so that the site stops sending them.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- The preferences service works for the caller only; a direct-id attempt on another user's preferences is refused, and the test shows why it could have succeeded
- Each of the four jobs checks the switch when its step runs, not when it was queued, so a switch turned off after queueing stops the mail
- A signed-out sender receives received, resolved, closing-soon and reply mails whatever any account setting says
- A test proves each of the other templates still sends with all four switches off
- An observer may change their own switches

### M22.5 — Add One-Click Unsubscribe and the Promotional Mail Frame (6h)

_Story:_ As a user who agreed to promotional email, I want to stop it from the mail itself in one step, so that withdrawing is as easy as agreeing.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- The route accepts only a POST with a valid unexpired token, sets no cookie, reads no session, returns no redirect, and withdraws consent with source `one-click`
- Only the SHA-256 hash of a token is stored; a fresh token is minted for each promotional mail and the token appears in no response
- The route is rate limited and returns the same result for an unknown or used token as for a valid one, except for its status, as the support view-token route does
- The headers are covered by the DKIM signature the transport applies, and a test proves they are present on a promotional message and absent from every transactional one
- The promotional layout is never used by an existing template; a guard test fails if a transactional template imports it
- A promotional send throws a named error while the postal address variable is unset; the variable is named in `claude-docs/secrets.md` by name only
- CLAUDE.md rule 1 and DESIGN.md name the route, and the decision record states why it is not a bespoke route handler in the sense rule 1 forbids

### M22.6 — Ask for Consent and Show the Email Notifications Section (5h)

_Story:_ As a user, I want to be asked once whether I want promotional email, and to change my answer and my support-mail switches on my account page, so that I stay in control.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- The prompt shows once; Save with the box ticked records consent with source `prompt`, and No Thanks records nothing but the answer
- The box is unticked by default, separate from every other control, and not part of sign-up or the terms
- The `/account` section records and withdraws consent with source `account`, and its four switches save through the preferences service
- Turning the reply notice off shows a warning that says the user will not be emailed when staff reply
- The section lists the mails that always send
- The page is in `PAUSED_VIEW_PAGES` and works for a paused account
- Playwright with `@axe-core/playwright` finds no violation on the prompt and on the section, and component tests use role and label queries only

### M22.7 — Write the Consent and Notification Stories (4h)

_Story:_ As the site keeper, I want each consent and notification path proved in a browser, so that no path ships on a service call alone.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- A story opts in from the one-time prompt and the user then appears to the recipient finder
- A story dismisses the prompt with No Thanks and is never asked again
- A story withdraws on `/account`, and the recipient finder no longer returns the user
- A story withdraws by the one-click route from a promotional mail and the `/account` box then shows unticked
- A story turns off the resolved mail, resolves a request and proves no mail was sent, while a signed-out sender's resolved mail still sends
- Each story runs with `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET=local-dev-not-a-real-secret` on its command line, and a flaky story is listed as flaky

Total: 25 hours.
