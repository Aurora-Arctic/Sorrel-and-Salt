# M15.57 — the terms of use's working notes

The terms of use at `/terms` (`src/app/(public)/terms/page.tsx`) are the one document every account agrees to. This file is where each claim in them points to the file, `DESIGN.md` section or law it was read from, so the owner can check the text against the code, and a later change knows which sentence it makes wrong. The page cites its two laws in a Sources list at the end; everything else it says about the site it says without a footnote, as the privacy policy does.

The terms' values, set by the owner: fair, plain, protective of members, and taking no right the site has no need of. So the text claims no ownership of and no licence over a member's writing beyond storing it and showing it where the coven's settings allow, names the small set of enforcement tools the code has rather than reserving a general power to ban, and limits what the site promises without limiting what the law gives a member.

**No lawyer has reviewed this text.** It was drafted by Claude from the code and the two laws cited below. Before the site is offered to anyone outside the owner's circle, I recommend a lawyer reads it, with the privacy policy and the disclaimer, and in particular the paragraph "What the Site Promises, and What It Does Not", which is where a limitation of liability would normally be drafted and where this text deliberately stays plain.

The version and date live in `src/lib/terms-version.ts`: `TERMS_VERSION`, a date-form string, and `TERMS_UPDATED_ON`, the `Date` the page prints. The page and the agreement gate both read `TERMS_VERSION`, so bumping it is one edit (M15.57's criteria, "one constant that the page and the gate both read"). M15.59's scoping will move the documents into the database later; until then the constant is the version.

## For the owner

Each item is a decision I took conservatively, or a fact I could not establish. Nothing here blocks the code; every one changes a sentence of the text. Items answered on 2 November 2026 are kept with the answer, so the text can be read against them.

1. **The governing-law sentence is for a lawyer to check, and there is still no limitation of liability.** "Which Law Applies" names the State of Washington, on the owner's instruction, and adds that nothing in the terms takes away a protection the reader's own law gives and does not let them give up. Both sentences were drafted by Claude, not a lawyer: whether a choice-of-law clause in this form does what it says, whether a forum should be named beside it, and whether "What the Site Promises, and What It Does Not" should carry an exclusion of liability, are the three questions to put to a lawyer before launch. I left the exclusion out because it is the clause most likely to take a right the site has no need of.
2. **Thirteen, from COPPA.** Answered: the United States, Washington state. The age sentence now rests on 15 U.S.C. § 6501(1) and 16 C.F.R. part 312 (the figure, and the parental-consent mechanism), and says the site asks no parent's consent. It makes no claim that COPPA reaches the site: whether it is an "operator" of a "commercial" service is a lawyer's question, and the age is a sensible floor either way. The earlier sentence "the site relies on your consent", a statement about a lawful basis under the GDPR, is gone, and nothing else on the four pages assumed the United Kingdom: I checked `/privacy`, `/disclaimer` and `/code-of-ethics` and their notes, and found no UK law, no "consent" basis and no UK assumption in them. The privacy policy's notes still point the questions of controller, lawful basis and supervisory authority at M15.58, which is right.
3. **Whether the GDPR applies to EU visitors is not settled here.** The terms say nothing about the GDPR, and these notes do not claim it does or does not apply to a site run from Washington with visitors in the EU. That is M15.58's question (`claude-docs/tasks/m15.md`, M15.58: "Lawful basis and retention"), and the terms should be re-read when it answers.
4. **Washington's My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373): no, on its definitions, and a lawyer should confirm.** The Act regulates "consumer health data", defined in RCW 19.373.010(8)(a) as "personal information that is linked or reasonably linkable to a consumer" that identifies the consumer's past, present or future physical or mental health status, with examples in (8)(b) such as health conditions, treatments, medication, reproductive or sexual health, biometric and genetic data, and health-related inferences drawn from non-health data; a "regulated entity" (19.373.010(23)) is one that conducts business in Washington or targets Washington consumers and "alone or jointly with others, determines the purpose and means of collecting, processing, sharing, or selling" such data. The site stores what a person writes about ingredients, stock and spells, their name, address and sign-in details, and support requests holding what the sender chose to write (`m15.2-privacy-policy-notes.md`); none of it identifies a person's health status, and the compendium's safety notes are about the ingredient, not the reader. I did not stretch the Act to the case of a member writing about their own health in a spell or a support request; a lawyer should say whether that edge makes the site a regulated entity. I looked at no other Washington statute, and the terms cite none.
5. **Promises.** Answered: "sells nothing about you" and "never used to train anything" are true and are now stated as promises, in "The Short Version" ("That is a promise, not a description of today") and in "What Is Yours" ("We promise that …"). "Run by people in their spare time" is true today and the owner hopes it will change, so it is reworded as the present state, "Today the site is run by people in their spare time", in the terms and in the disclaimer alike. I kept it rather than cutting it because it is the honest reason the site cannot promise uptime, which the next sentence says.
6. **The Disclaimer and the Code of Ethics are "part of these terms".** Answered: kept. The terms now say which part of the Code of Ethics binds whom: "What Everyone Agrees To" binds every account, and the curator, admin and owner sections bind whoever holds that role. The staff sections are drafts for the owner's review, rule by rule (`m15.4-code-of-ethics-notes.md`, item 10).
7. **Enforcement is named, not reserved.** "How to Treat Each Other" lists the three tools the code has (remove from a coven, support time-out, revoke coven creation) and says closing an account is done by hand. It does not reserve a general right to suspend or ban, because no such mechanism exists and the owner's instruction is not to claim what the code does not do. When M15.51–M15.56 (account pauses) land, this paragraph should name the pause.
8. **The sentences marked [depends on build]** in "Agreeing to These Terms" and "Changes to These Terms" describe the gate from M15.57's entry, written before the gate existed. The code agent's build decides whether the acceptance page shows the text inline or links `/terms`, and whether existing accounts are seeded as agreed; each changes one sentence above, flagged in the claims table.
9. **The version is a date.** `TERMS_VERSION` is `'2026-11-02'`, the day the text was drafted, so a reader can tell versions apart without a counter and so the gate's comparison is a plain string equality. A bump means setting both constants in `src/lib/terms-version.ts` to the day the new text takes effect. If you prefer a counter (`'1'`, `'2'`), it is the one constant. The text was revised on 2 November 2026 before any account agreed to it, so the version was not bumped.
