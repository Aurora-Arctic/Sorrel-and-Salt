# M15.57 — the terms of use's working notes

The terms of use at `/terms` (`src/app/(public)/terms/page.tsx`) are the one document every account agrees to. This file is where each claim in them points to the file, `DESIGN.md` section or law it was read from, so the owner can check the text against the code, and a later change knows which sentence it makes wrong. The page cites its two laws in a Sources list at the end; everything else it says about the site it says without a footnote, as the privacy policy does.

The terms' values, set by the owner: fair, plain, protective of members, and taking no right the site has no need of. So the text claims no ownership of and no licence over a member's writing beyond storing it and showing it where the coven's settings allow, names the small set of enforcement tools the code has rather than reserving a general power to ban, and limits what the site promises without limiting what the law gives a member.

**No lawyer has reviewed this text.** It was drafted by Claude from the code and the two laws cited below. Before the site is offered to anyone outside the owner's circle, I recommend a lawyer reads it, with the privacy policy and the disclaimer, and in particular the paragraph "What the Site Promises, and What It Does Not", which is where a limitation of liability would normally be drafted and where this text deliberately stays plain.

The version and date live in `src/lib/terms-version.ts`: `TERMS_VERSION`, a date-form string, and `TERMS_UPDATED_ON`, the `Date` the page prints. The page and the agreement gate both read `TERMS_VERSION`, so bumping it is one edit (M15.57's criteria, "one constant that the page and the gate both read"). M15.59's scoping will move the documents into the database later; until then the constant is the version.

## For the owner

Each item is a decision I took conservatively, or a fact I could not establish. Nothing here blocks the code; every one changes a sentence of the text.

1. **No limitation of liability, and no lawyer.** The paragraph "What the Site Promises, and What It Does Not" says the site cannot promise uptime or correctness and "does not try to take any right from you that the law gives you". A conventional terms page would add an exclusion of liability and a governing-law clause. I left both out: an exclusion is the clause most likely to "take a right the site has no need of", and I could not establish the site's jurisdiction from the code or the docs (item 2). I recommend a lawyer reviews this document, the privacy policy and the disclaimer before launch, and decides whether a limitation of liability belongs here.
2. **Thirteen, and the United Kingdom.** The age clause rests on UK law because nothing in the repository names a jurisdiction, and the owner's time zone and spelling pointed there. If the site is run from elsewhere, the age and the law cited both change: GDPR art. 8 lets each EU member state set an age from 13 to 16, and the United States' COPPA (not cited, as I did not fetch it) sets 13 by a different mechanism. The sentence also says "the site relies on your consent", which is a statement about the site's lawful basis; M15.58's GDPR scoping will decide that, and the sentence should be re-read when it does. I need you to confirm the jurisdiction or tell me the age to use.
3. **Three commitments, not code facts.** "Sells nothing about you", "never used to train anything" and "run by people in their spare time" are promises the owner makes, not things the code enforces. They match the privacy policy's and the disclaimer's wording and their notes. Strike any you do not want to make.
4. **The Disclaimer and the Code of Ethics are "part of these terms".** This binds the three documents together so that agreeing to the terms is agreeing to all three. The alternative is to have the terms merely link them. I chose binding because the owner's values are in the Code of Ethics and the terms are the one document a member must agree to. If you prefer linking only, the sentence in "The Compendium" changes.
5. **Enforcement is named, not reserved.** "How to Treat Each Other" lists the three tools the code has (remove from a coven, support time-out, revoke coven creation) and says closing an account is done by hand. It does not reserve a general right to suspend or ban, because no such mechanism exists and the owner's instruction is not to claim what the code does not do. When M15.51–M15.56 (account pauses) land, this paragraph should name the pause.
6. **The sentences marked [depends on build]** in "Agreeing to These Terms" and "Changes to These Terms" describe the gate from M15.57's entry, written before the gate existed. The code agent's build decides whether the acceptance page shows the text inline or links `/terms`, and whether existing accounts are seeded as agreed; each changes one sentence above, flagged in the claims table.
7. **The version is a date.** `TERMS_VERSION` is `'2026-11-02'`, the day the text was drafted, so a reader can tell versions apart without a counter and so the gate's comparison is a plain string equality. A bump means setting both constants in `src/lib/terms-version.ts` to the day the new text takes effect. If you prefer a counter (`'1'`, `'2'`), it is the one constant.
8. **The Data Protection Act citation is to the as-enacted text.** The current section 9 is marked omitted (the rule moved into the retained UK GDPR at EU exit). I cited the as-enacted version because that is the page that carries the figure, and said so in the Sources note. A lawyer may prefer a citation to the UK GDPR art. 8 as retained; I did not fetch that text and so did not cite it.
