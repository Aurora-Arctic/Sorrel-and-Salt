### How the research was done

Helper agents researched three or four entries each, in parallel, to a brief that repeats the README's rules, and returned each batch as a literal fragment with an evidence file giving, for every value, the source, its locator, the file and line where it is stated (a line of the e-book's HTML, a page of a PDF's text, or a line of the Wikipedia revision's wikitext) and a short excerpt. The lead opened at least one book locator and one Wikipedia line from every batch and found each said what the evidence claimed, ran the guard over the assembled shard, and confirmed through the Wikipedia API that all 24 cited revisions exist under the titles cited. Every page and URL cited here was read during the task, on 2026-10-11.

**Plants of the World Online was tried once**, at 07:06 UTC on 2026-10-11 (`powo.science.kew.org/results?q=Galium%20verum`), and answered HTTP 403 with a Cloudflare challenge page, as it had for MB.240 and the pilot. So no formal name here was checked against it; each was checked against the taxobox of the Wikipedia revision cited for it and, where the plant has one, the binomial the _AHPA Botanical Safety Handbook_ heads its monograph with. **No formal name on the candidate list changed**; where a source prints another name, it is recorded under "Formal names" below and that source is not cited for the formal name. Grieve's _A Modern Herbal_ at botanical.com answered the same probe with HTTP 406 unless the request carried a browser's headers; it was not used, since every row here has an entry in Cunningham's or Kynes's encyclopedia, and Culpeper was not used either.

**Safety notes** come first from the _AHPA Botanical Safety Handbook_, then from NCCIH's pages where one exists, from Cunningham's health codes (his key, p. 266) and from Kynes's own cautions. Where no handbook covers a plant, Wikipedia's statements on its toxicity are used, and the entry's source line says so.

### Formal names

Where a source read prints a different name from the candidate list's, and how it was handled.

- **Lady's Mantle.** The roster's _Alchemilla vulgaris_ matches Wikipedia's taxobox (which cites POWO for _A. vulgaris_ L.), Cunningham and Kynes. AHPA heads its monograph _Alchemilla xanthochlora_ Rothm. with "syn: _Alchemilla vulgaris_ auct. non L.", i.e. the name as other authors misapplied it, not Linnaeus's species. The entry keeps _A. vulgaris_ and uses AHPA only for safety and form; AHPA does not support the formal name.
- **Lady's Slipper.** Cunningham heads the entry _Cypripedium pubescens_, which Kynes gives as a synonym of _C. parviflorum_ and Wikipedia gives as a homotypic synonym of _C. parviflorum_ var. _pubescens_. Cunningham is applied (same heading, Lady's Slipper) but does not support the formal name.
- **Lady's Bedstraw.** No conflict on the name; see (b) on Cunningham's "Bedstraw, Fragrant".

- **Lavender.** Cunningham prints "Lavendula officinale or L. vera"; Wikipedia's taxobox and AHPA p. 503 both list _Lavandula vera_ DC. and _L. officinalis_ as synonyms of _Lavandula angustifolia_, so the roster's reading holds. Cunningham is not cited for `canonicalName`.
- **Larkspur.** Cunningham prints "Delphinium ajacus, D. consolida, D. spp."; Wikipedia lists _Delphinium consolida_ L. as a synonym of _Consolida regalis_, and Kynes heads forking larkspur as _Consolida regalis_ syn. _Delphinum_ [sic] _consolida_. Cunningham's entry also takes in _D. ajacis_ (giant larkspur), which is applied to this entry only as the entry its heading names. Cunningham is not cited for `canonicalName`.
- **Larch.** Cunningham prints _Larix europaea_, Kynes treats _L. laricina_ and _L. decidua_ (syn. _L. europaea_); the entry is the genus, as the roster says, and Wikipedia's taxobox gives _Larix_.
- **Leek, for the lead.** Wikipedia's taxobox accepts _Allium ampeloprasum_ and lists Cunningham's _Allium porrum_ L. as a synonym, so the roster's name agrees with the revision read.
