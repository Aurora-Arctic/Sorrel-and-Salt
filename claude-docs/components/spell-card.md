# SpellCard

`src/components/SpellCard/` draws one spell in the grimoire's list. It is presentational: it takes props and works nothing out from the data, as [ingredient-card.md](ingredient-card.md) does. It shows the spell's title, its intent cut to three lines, a Draft or Private badge where one applies, the categories its author assigned, and a foot holding who last edited it and when, how many times it has been made, and its actions. Props are in `types.ts`.

## Contracts

- **Its name.** The `article` is named by its title heading through `aria-labelledby`, so a test or a screen reader finds a spell by `getByRole('article', { name: 'Hearth Warding Jar' })`. The list leaves its items unnamed, so the title is heard once. A null or blank title lists as "Untitled Spell" (`UNTITLED_SPELL`), since a draft may have none. `level` is the heading level, 2 by default.
- **Categories are the assigned ones**, drawn as `CategoryChips`, never the derived ones (DESIGN.md §5).
- **Only a draft is marked.** A draft carries a Draft badge; a complete card carries no status badge, since complete is the default and marking it says nothing. The badge is `.badge--draft`, whose palette in `$badge-palettes` (`src/scss/_variables.scss`) takes Retired's muted tint and contrast-checked inks: an unfinished spell is a fact about it, not a warning.
- **Only a private spell is marked.** A private spell carries a Private badge and a shared one none, for the same reason. The service lists a private spell to its author alone, so the badge is seen only by the person it is about, and tells them no one else in the coven sees it. It is the word "Private", so it does not rely on colour. `.badge--private` takes Draft's muted tint and inks, and sits after Draft when a spell is both. `SpellMarks` draws both ([entry-marks.md](entry-marks.md#spell-marks)), under the title as an ingredient card's marks sit under its name.
- **The foot line.** `lastEdit` (`LastEdit`, [last-edit.md](last-edit.md)) draws the editor and the time when the caller hands one in. `workingCount` above zero adds "Made once" or "Made 3 times" on the same line after a dot ("Edited 5 minutes ago · Made 3 times"); it counts the complete workings the viewer can see, from the count loader, and zero or none says nothing. `.spell-card__foot-line` is a wrapping flex row in `$last-edit-ink` at caption size, `space(5)` below the chips, with an `aria-hidden` dot between the two.
- **The link.** Given an `href`, the title is a `PendingLink` with a `LinkHint`, as IngredientCard's name is; the grimoire list passes each spell's recipe page. Without one the title is plain text and the card keeps the default cursor, since a press does nothing. The card has no drawer behind it.
- **The link covers the whole card.** The title's `<a>` stretches an empty `::after` over the article, so a press anywhere opens the recipe. While the link holds focus, the focus ring is drawn round the card on the article's own `::after`, which comes after the burn layers and so is not covered by the burnt corner. The intent, the chips' plain items, the made count and the foot's gaps pass the pointer through to the link. The foot's four actions, the last-edit line and the category chips' +N button sit above it and keep their own press, hover and focus. The foot passes the pointer through with `pointer-events: none`, which its children inherit, so the actions row and the last-edit line take it back with `pointer-events: auto`; without that, the last-edit line's tip opened on focus only, never on hover.
- **The foot's actions** sit outside the title link, so no interactive element nests in another. The Quick Look eye is at the bottom left (`specimen__actions-start`); Record a Working, the Edit pencil and the More menu are at the right of the `specimen__actions` row. Each is drawn only when the caller hands in what it needs. The row reuses IngredientCard's classes and size rules (`.spell-card .specimen__actions` is in the same selector lists), so the icons match in the grid, compact and phone layouts.
  - **Edit pencil.** `editHref` draws an `EditLink` (an icon link named "Edit <title>"), in text colour. The grimoire tab passes it only for a role that may update a spell (`rolePermits(role, { spell: ['update'] })`), so an observer's cards have none. It keeps the tab's filter, page and tab in the address, so closing the dialog returns to the list as it was.
  - **Quick Look eye.** `lookHref` draws a `QuickLookLink` (an icon link named "Quick Look <title>") opening the `look` modal place (`SPELL_LOOK_PLACE`), independent of the spell builder's dialog. The tab passes it for every role, since an observer may read the spell. Closing returns to the tab's address with focus back on the eye. The modal is `SpellLookDialog` ([spell-recipe.md](spell-recipe.md), "Quick Look").
  - **Record a Working and More** are the spell page's own parts, so each has one implementation: Record a Working is the green cauldron (`SpellCard/record-working.tsx`, opening the form the page's `RecordWorkingButton` opens), and `SpellMoreMenu` (`SpellRecipe/more-menu.tsx`) holds Duplicate and Delete Spell (destructive). The card passes its own `id`, `visibility` and name, and `canPrint={false}`: Print is the spell page's alone. The menu draws nothing when neither Duplicate nor Delete Spell is allowed. Each needs a query client, which the workshop does not provide, so the card has no story with them.
  - **What the tab hands in.** `recordWorking` goes to a complete spell when the role may record a working and read members (the spell page's rule), naming the recorder from `viewerOf()`. `more` goes when the role may create or delete a spell (`rolePermits`: steward and practitioner, never observer). `deletedHref` is the tab's address with its filter and page kept and `deleted=<name>` added. The list does not hold what the dialog needs: the button opens the dialog at once, and the dialog reads the spell's steps in one `RecordWorkingSetup` query through `/api/graphql` (`spell(workspaceId, id).steps`), so a list nobody records from costs no steps batch, and its participant box searches the members as the user types ([record-working.md](record-working.md)). While the query runs the dialog holds a `form` skeleton and one Loading status ([loading.md](../loading.md)); a refused or failed read is one error Notice. A linked step with no name to read is "Unavailable Ingredient", as on the spell page.

## Styling

The layout is the Foundations specimen (`.specimen` in `_primitives.scss`). The card is IngredientCard's sibling: the same surface, shadow, radius, padding, title size, medium badges and category chips, with the hover tint and lifted shadow plus the smoulder below, and no drawer. The intent stops at three lines with an ellipsis (`-webkit-line-clamp`, the unprefixed `line-clamp` beside it as an enhancement). Tokens only. The rules the two cards share come from `IngredientCard/_specimen-card.scss`: `specimen-card()` gives the title link stretched over the card, the pointer on a card whose title links, the title block, the badge row, the medium badge size and the plain chips passing a click through. It is included with `$ring: false`, as this card draws its focus ring on the article's own `::after`, which paints over the burnt corner where the link's overlay would not.

## Compact

Under a list's `data-density="compact"`, unless a stored Regular choice sits on `<html>` (`data-card-density`, [card-density-switch.md](card-density-switch.md)), `_compact.scss` includes IngredientCard's shared `specimen-card-compact()`: padding `space(3) space(4)`, gap `space(2)`, the title at h4's size, small badges, overline chips and a tighter foot, and the intent a step smaller. The card takes no density prop, so the look can switch before React runs.

## The smouldering edge

On hover, or with focus inside the card, the card's bottom edge burns, as parchment smouldering from its bottom-right corner. The face also takes IngredientCard's hover tint and lifted shadow, and nothing else: nothing moves the card.

The effect is in `_smoulder.scss`. Its colours, lengths, durations and the rim's flicker frames are `$burn-*` tokens at the end of `src/scss/_variables.scss`; the partial forwards the ones SpellPreview reads, and keeps `$candle` and `$ember` as its names for `$burn-candle` and `$burn-ember`.

- **A true hole.** The burnt part is gone, through the card's face and its shadow both, so the page itself shows through it. The face and shadow are not on the `article` but on a layer of their own, `.spell-card__face`: the face is the wide spread shadow of a hole-shaped box, so it is everything but the hole, and the card's shadow is a `drop-shadow()` of that, so it follows the burnt edge and shrinks with the burn instead of staying a rectangle. The hole runs along the bottom edge, hanging 0.5rem past the card and 1rem deep in all, so along the bottom it stays inside the card's padding. At the corner it bites deeper and curves about 1.25rem up the right edge (an elliptical corner radius, `$burn-corner-radius`), where no text runs.
- **An ember line, a char rim and a char line.** A thin bright line (`$burn-candle`, the dark theme's warning ochre, and `$burn-ember`, its secondary red) runs round the burn front, with a dark char rim (`--burn-char`) outside it, and outside that a thin soft near-black line (`--burn-char-line`) where the paper is charred but not yet alight. Both char colours are tokens per theme, taken from the theme's darkest ink, never pure black. One SVG filter, `#spell-card-burn` from `SpellCardFilters`, tears the hole in the face and the rim alike, so their ragged edges meet exactly.
- **One glow.** A single soft warm glow sits behind the face: it shows through the hole and spills onto the table below the card as one continuous layer, so there is no dark row between the glow inside the hole and the glow off the card. It runs `$burn-glow-lead` (1.5rem) past the ember front, at opacity 0.4 under a 1.25rem blur, kept below the ember line's brightness. Faint embers glow in the burnt part, brightest along the ember line and fading towards the corner.
- **Nothing warm sits under text.** With its rim and the tearing, the hole stays inside the card's padding along the bottom, and climbs higher only at the corner.
- **Timing.** The burn creeps along the edge for 1.3s and the glow grows with it. It then holds while the ember glows unevenly for three rounds (`$burn-flickers`), the glow and the embers breathing at their own paces for three rounds too. Each then holds its last frame, so a card left under the pointer or holding the focus settles rather than flickering. On leaving, the burn fades while the hole, the face's shadow and the glow shrink back to the corner, so a quick return burns on from where they had shrunk to.
- **Reduced motion** shows the finished burn at once, still. **Touch** gets no hover at all: the rules sit inside `@media (hover: hover)`, so a tap does not leave a card burning.
- **Layers and stacking.** The layers are three `aria-hidden` spans in the `article`, so the card's name is still its title alone: the glow and the face before the content, and the burn's rim after it. They stack by the order of the markup, with `position: relative` on each child and no `z-index` anywhere. A `z-index` on a layer behind the card would need a stacking context round the card, and one on the card itself would trap the category chips' tips beneath the next card.
- **Nothing transforms the card or any ancestor of it**, because a transformed ancestor becomes the containing block of the category chips' `position: fixed` tip bubbles.
- **Contrast.** Light theme: the page is `$parchment` (#efe9da) and the card `$parchment-raised` (#f9f5ea), lifted to #fcfaf4 on hover, a contrast of 1.11 and 1.16 against the page. That difference is the app's own tokens. The burnt part is the page itself. Below the hole, the warm glow on parchment, with the card's shadow, is slightly darker than the page, as any warm tint on parchment is. On the hovered card the intent measures 6.95:1 (light) and 7.99:1 (dark), the title 15.56:1 and 12.20:1.

### The burn filter

`SpellCardFilters` renders the filter once per page beside the list of cards (`GrimoireList`), not once per card: an id must be unique and one filter serves every card. Without it the burn front is a smooth curve. The filter uses coarse noise to displace the hole and its ember and char bands. The noise is still, since a burn line does not wriggle.

## Stories
