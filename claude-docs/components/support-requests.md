# Support Requests

`src/components/SupportRequests/` is the support page, `/keepers/support`, for a keeper and, narrowed, a curator. It shows a page of the requests people have sent, open ahead of resolved and newest first, with a filter, the shared [`Pager`](pager.md), and one request opened to its message in a modal at `?request=<id>`. It is render-only. The page reads the filter and cursor off its address (`support/filter.ts`), one page and its count through `listSupportRequests` and `countSupportRequests`, the people named on them through `usersNamedOnRequests`, and the opened request with its replies and its sender's time-outs (`support/reads.ts`). The support services are the guard; the page and GraphQL end at the same ones.

## Contracts

- **A row shows** its subject (a link that opens the request, in the body ink and underlined on hover and focus), status, sender, assignee, whether curators can see it, and the error number. `supportHref(filter, cursor, request?)` in `href.ts` builds every address, carrying `?view=` (omitted for the default, Needs Staff), `?about=`, `?error=`, one cursor and `?request=`. Each row link is named subject plus sender ("Wobbling Cauldron from Ada Wortley").
- **Six views** (`supportViewTabs` in `href.ts`): Needs Staff (all open, oldest sender activity first, for everyone), Mine (open, assigned to me), Unassigned (open, no assignee), Waiting on Sender, Resolved and Closed.
  - They are the shared `TabbedPage` row of links (`tab-links.tsx`, a `nav` named Support), on the Support heading's line. Each carries its count from `countSupportViews` as a `FacetCount` (muted, no brackets, 99+ from 100, the count in visually hidden text) and the address of its own view with the other filters kept. The open one is `aria-current="page"`, and a press shows the row's busy state. Tab order is one stop per link.
  - The counts honour the About, Error and Sender filters and a curator's scope. There is no Status select, since the view implies the status.
- **The filter** is [`FilterForm`](filter-form.md): an Error # search and a Kind select, and a Sender facet chip of radios (`ChoiceChip`, `sender=<group>`, applied in SQL) kept across the views. Its options are All and one per entry of `SUPPORT_SENDER_GROUPS` (`src/lib/support-path.ts`), each with its own label, icon (`SENDER_ICONS` in `filter.tsx`) and SQL condition (`senderArm` in `src/db/repository/support.ts`). Beta Testers, the sprout, is the one group; a further group is another entry.
- **Marks in a row.** The Status cell adds a "Sender Replied" label, and "Resolved by Sender" when `resolved_by` is the request's sender (`resolvedBySender` on the row); the dialog's Resolved fact then reads "by the sender". The Sender cell, after the name, shows a beta tester's sprout (`SproutIcon`, body ink), a status mark tipped and named "Beta Tester" whose press opens its tip, as `HeldMark` does. The Curators column heading is shortened from the dialog's switch label, Shared With Curators, and draws a check or cross with its text.
- **A curator is shown no sender's email.** The From fact shows the name alone for an account, and "Signed-out sender" with no address for a signed-out request; a keeper sees the address beside either. The service withholds it (`asRead` nulls `senderEmail`; `usersNamedOnRequests` blanks other people's addresses for a curator), and GraphQL `User.email` is self-or-admin already.
- **The sender's name is a link for a keeper** (the Sender column and the From fact). It opens the sender's user modal over this page, at `?user=<id>` beside the filter, the cursor and any open `?request=<id>`, so the browser stays on `/keepers/support`. The page reads it as the Users page does (`readOpenedUser`) and draws the same `UserDetail` dialog, Support Time-Out section included, in a second `RouteModal` place named `user`, after the request's. Both are native dialogs shown in turn, so the later one stacks above the earlier, which stays open beneath; Escape and Close act on the top one. Close of the user modal goes to the same address without `user`, back to the request or the list. A curator has no user modal: the name stays text and a hand-written `?user=` is ignored. A deleted account stays "Deleted account". The email beside the name stays muted text.
- **The opened request is a wide dialog of two columns**, stacked on a phone with the settings after the reply. Its head is an eyebrow ("Support · " and the kind), the subject and a status badge. The dialog opens with the focus on its title. Dates use `LocalTime` long.
  - **Left column**, in order: the facts (From on a row of its own; Sent with Resolved beside it; Page on a row of its own; Error as one line with the reference in monospace and an icon-only copy button, when the request has one). From and Page are `.support-detail__fact--wide` (`grid-column: 1 / -1`), so a long path stays on one line, breaking anywhere only past the full width, and never sits beside a date. Then the Issue section, then the thread (the sender's message first, tagged Sender, then each reply, tagged Staff) and the reply form.
  - **Right column, for a keeper alone**: Assignee, Shared With Curators and Time-Out. The Time-Out fold is closed by default and says "none", or shows a "Timed Out" badge with "until" the end or "with no end date".
- **Thread dates** sit flush right in each card's head (`margin-inline-start: auto` on the `<time>`), the name and tag at the left, and stay at the right when the head wraps on a phone. The admin dialog and the sender's dialog on `/account/support` share the thread.
- **The composer has two modes**, chosen in a "Send As" choice chip (`ChoiceChip`) that opens on Internal Note, so a reply is always chosen: Internal Note and Reply.
  - A note goes through `addSupportInternalNote`, which takes no notifier, so no email is sent and senders never receive it. It shows with a "Staff · Internal" tag, the word Internal in the warning ink (`.support-detail__internal`). On the Send As chip the whole Internal Note option is in that ink (`display` on the option; its `label` still names it).
  - A curator may note on any request they can read, not only an assigned one: the service reads the request as the curator's own visible read, so the form needs no assigned-to gate.
  - A curator who cannot answer a request (shared with them, or assigned to someone else) is shown a note-only form: "Your Note" and Add Note, with no mode choice, reply field, Resolve or Waiting on Sender (`ReplyForm`'s `noteOnly`).
- **Reply** is a required "Your Reply" field, disabled until there is a body, with **Resolve** opposite it (open requests only), for a keeper or a curator the request is assigned to.
- **Waiting on Sender** is a switch, flush left beside the reply box, on an open request in Reply mode only. On, the reply moves the request to waiting on the sender. While the sender has a standing time-out (`request.timeouts`, not lifted, with no end or ending in the future), a notice beside it says "The sender cannot answer until <time>." or "The sender cannot answer while timed out with no end date.", and Reply asks "Reply to a sender who is timed out?" before it sends. Send Reply sends it; Cancel keeps the text unsent. An internal note never asks.
- **Resolve asks first**, through `ConfirmedAction` (`inModal`), titled "Resolve This Request?". It says the sender sees the request as resolved on their support page, can reopen it by replying, and that it closes by itself after seven days without a reply. The confirm is plain (`btn btn--solid`), not red, since resolving is not destructive. No mail is sent on resolve. The page has no Close control.
- **An anonymised request** (`anonymised` on the row: its sender is the System account) shows no sender. `reads.ts` decides it on the server and loads no name for the System account, so the row's `sender` is null.
  - The Sender cell is empty, and as a narrow card it is dropped, label and all (`.support-requests__sender-cell:empty`). The subject link is named by the subject alone.
  - The dialog has no From fact, and its thread drops the sender's message and follow-ups, which anonymising emptied. It takes no change, so a keeper sees what a curator sees: the issue link read-only when there is one (`IssuePanel` with `canEdit` false), no Request Settings aside, and a notice in place of the reply form. The service refuses each of these (`refuseAnonymised`).
  - Anonymised requests come last in every view and filter, each group in the view's own order. The repository orders it (the first key of `orderOf`, through the cursor helper).
- **The Issue section** sits in the left column above the thread, for both roles: the linked issue as repository and number. A keeper has a pencil icon button named "Change Issue Address" before it, or the address field at once while none is linked. A curator sees the link alone, read-only, with no pencil and no address form, and nothing while none is linked.
  - Spacing for a keeper: the facts to its hairline, and its last line to the Thread heading, are each the column gap, `space(4)`. Its own `space(3)` of padding sits below its border, and its margin stays zero.
  - Spacing for a curator's read-only Issue (`support-detail__issue--plain`): no hairline and no padding. It stands `space(6)` from the facts and from the Thread heading, being the column gap `space(4)` plus a `space(2)` margin on the section.
- **There is no Create Issue link.** The keeper makes the GitHub issue by hand, so nothing from the request is copied into GitHub: the message could hold private information. The "GitHub Issue" field takes either of two shapes and stores both as the plain link `https://github.com/Aurora-Arctic/Sorrel-and-Salt/issues/<number>`: that link itself, or a link from any project board of the `Aurora-Arctic` organisation (`github.com/orgs/Aurora-Arctic/projects/<n>/...`), whose `issue` parameter is `owner|repo|number`, URL-encoded. `normaliseIssueLink` in `src/lib/support-path.ts` does it, for the form and the service alike. Anything else, or an issue of another repository, is refused beside the field with "Paste a GitHub issue link, like github.com/Aurora-Arctic/Sorrel-and-Salt/issues/1409, or a link opened from the project board."
- **Time-outs** have no heading of their own beyond the fold, and the panel (`timeout-panel.tsx`) is shared with the user modal, keyed by the user. The fields come from the site access pause's parts (`UserDetail/access-pause-parts.tsx`).
  - While one stands, the form is hidden. A card shows a "Timed Out" badge, "until {end}" or "with no end date", the note in muted text, "Set by {name}, {date}", and **Lift** beneath, which asks first ("Lift the Time-Out?", with a red "Lift Time-Out" confirm).
  - Otherwise the form asks **For** (1 Day, 1 Week, 1 Month, Until a Date or No End Date). Only Until a Date shows an **Until** day field, and the end is the end of that day in the admin's own zone. Then a required **Note** ("Seen by staff only, up to 500 characters."). **Time Out Sender** is a quiet button, off until a note is written, that asks first (heading "Time Out {name}?", body "They cannot send a support request until {end}. You can lift it sooner.", or "They cannot send a support request until you lift the time-out." with no end date), with a red confirm.
  - The request dialog keeps no ledger. When the sender has earlier time-outs it draws a history icon link beside the Time-Out heading (the fold's `info` slot), named "Earlier Time-Outs", to the sender's user modal over this page, where the ledger is.
  - A signed-out request has no Time-Out section: its sender is the stand-in account, which the service refuses to time out. An anonymised request shows a notice in place of the note and reply form.
- **The page announces itself.** A visually hidden `<output>` in the list gives the result count ("3 Requests") after a filter change, and one in the dialog gives each save's outcome ("Reply sent.", "Request assigned.", "Time-out lifted."). The Subject cell wraps long unbroken text inside a capped width. The GitHub Issue and Time-Out forms ask before discarding typed input.
- **Every action is a GraphQL mutation** (`use-support-action.ts`) and refreshes the page on success. A refusal shows as a notice, or beside its field where it names one.
- **A curator reaches the page through `requireCuratorSession()`** and is shown the admin-only controls by none: the `viewer` prop hides them, the services refuse them regardless, and the page gives a curator no assignee list. A request neither marked nor assigned to the curator shows the same "no support request has that address" notice as an unknown id. `tests/e2e/admin-support-curator.spec.ts` proves it.

## Request lifecycle

- Request lifecycle transitions: open -> waiting_on_sender (staff reply marked waiting, reply.ts); open/waiting -> resolved (staff, staff.ts; or the sender on their own request, resolveMySupportRequest); open/waiting/resolved -> closed (staff, closeSupportRequest); waiting/resolved -> open (the sender's public message, keeps the assignee); resolved -> closed (the request's job, seven days after resolving, retention.ts); closed -> new request (the sender's message opens one linked to it). Staff replies and internal notes change nothing else. Each move is one guarded update, so a refused move writes nothing.

- Retention stages: done once a day by the support-retention job as the System account: closing-soon notice from day 5, close on day 7, anonymising on the twelfth month after the close (keeps the row, topic, status and dates so counts survive; drops everything the sender wrote or is known by). The job reads due ids per stage a page at a time and hands each page to a batch; each batch re-reads its rows' status and dates before writing, so a batch run twice, two at once, or after a reopen changes nothing.

- Sender's own reply on own request: open request adds to thread; waiting or resolved reopens it, clearing resolved_at and keeping the assignee; closed opens a new request carrying topic and assignee, linked to the closed one. Time-outs refuse it as they refuse a send; only the new request counts toward the daily limit.

- Reply notice: the notice carries no reply text (read on the sender's support page behind sign-in); a signed-out sender with no such page is mailed the reply whole; either goes out through a retried job that stamps the reply once the mail is taken.

## Create Issue

- Create Issue prefill never carries the sender name or address; issueUrl is validated; a status change sends no mail
