# SignInDialog

`src/components/SignInDialog/` is a link to sign in that opens the sign-in panel in a dialog over the page it is on, so a visitor signs in or creates an account without leaving the page. The public bar's Sign In ([`public-frame.md`](public-frame.md)) and a signed-out invitation's two choices, Create Account and Sign In ([`invitation-acceptance.md`](invitation-acceptance.md)), are its users. Props: `SignInDialogProps` in `types.ts`. `/sign-in` stays a page of its own, for shared links and for a browser without the script.

## Contracts

- **It is a link first.** The trigger is a `PendingLink` to `signInPath(next)`, so a modifier or middle click (`opensElsewhere`) and a browser without the script follow the address to `/sign-in`. A plain press is prevented and opens the dialog instead; nothing navigates and the page's address stays as it was.
- **One return path, made safe once.** `next` passes through `safeReturnPath`, and the link and the panel in the dialog both use what comes out. From an auth page that is nothing, so the link is a bare `/sign-in` and a provider is asked for no return path: the account lands by its role ([`sign-in-panel.md`](sign-in-panel.md), "The `?next=` contract"). The bar never draws on an auth page, so in practice this is a guard.
- **The dialog is `Modal`** ([`modal.md`](modal.md)), its centred box on a phone as at every width. Close, Escape and a click outside close it at once and ask nothing, since nothing typed is lost that matters: the page underneath was never left. `returnTo` gives the focus back to the link.
- **The title is the mode.** "Sign In" or "Create an Account", from the `mode` it opened in (`sign-in` unless asked) and every switch the panel reports after. It opens again in the mode it was asked for, not the one it was closed in. In the email view the title is that view's heading, "Sign In with Email" or "Create an Account with Email" (`EMAIL_VIEW_TITLES`, exported by the panel beside `SIGN_IN_TITLES`), so the view has one heading and the dialog takes that name. The panel reports the view through `onEmailViewChange`, and the dialog turns on `Modal`'s `focusTitle`, which moves the focus to the title ([`modal.md`](modal.md)).
- **`ledes`** (`SignInLedes`, the type the panel takes for the page) are one sentence per mode, drawn under the title (`Modal`'s `under`). An invitation sent to an address passes two, `ADDRESS_LEDES` (`InvitationAcceptance/ledes.ts`, which `/sign-in` passes too for such an invitation), saying to use that address; a card and the bar pass none.
- **The body is `SignInPanel` with `inDialog`**, which draws no head of its own, holds its email view in its state rather than the address, draws no email-form heading (the title is it), and puts the two-factor step's heading at `h3` ([`sign-in-panel.md`](sign-in-panel.md), "In a dialog"). `signUpLandsOnNext` is passed through: an invitation sets it, so a new email account comes back to the invitation and is told to check its inbox there.
- **A new email account from anywhere else stays on the page.** Without `signUpLandsOnNext` the dialog gives the panel `onSignedUp`, so the sign-up, which signs the account in, navigates nowhere: the dialog turns to its inbox view, titled "Check Your Inbox", with one sentence under the title and the quiet **Confirm Your Email**, a link to the email page with the page as its `next` (`emailPagePath(next)`). That page's form sends the new message, with the wait between mails it reads on the server, so the dialog has no sender of its own. The verification mail carries the page as `next` like any sign-up, so its link lands on the email page's confirmed view, whose Continue comes back here; the terms gate applies on the way as anywhere. Closing the inbox view refreshes the route (`usePendingNavigation().refresh`), so the bar shows the account signed in and the page is otherwise as it was. An address that earned no session keeps the panel's own sentence in sign-in mode.
- **"Forgot Password?"** in the dialog stays a link that leaves for `/forgot-password`.
- **`configured`** is the providers with credentials here. The caller reads it on the server with `configuredProviders()`, since the module naming the env vars must never reach a client bundle.

## Styling

The trigger's class is the caller's, `.btn.btn--solid.btn--medium` by default. The panel inside is a bare `.sign-in-panel`, its column without `.panel`'s ground and padding, which the modal's box already gives. The inbox view's link is `.sign-in-dialog__inbox`, centred, and full width at or under `$shell-phone-width`.
