- **a**: Myrrh, Balm of Gilead, Guggul, Opoponax.
- **b**: Benzoin, Storax, Sweet Gum, Tolu Balsam.
- **c**: Camphor, Borneol Camphor, Mastic, Sandarac.
- **d**: Dragon's Blood, Copal, Kauri Gum, Labdanum.
- **e**: Agarwood, Makko, Red Sandalwood, Cedar.
- **f**: Asafoetida, Galbanum, Gum Arabic, Tragacanth.
- **g**: Sweetgrass, Golden Frankincense, Charcoal, Saltpetre.
- **h**: Alum, Ammonite, Mother-of-Pearl, Red Coral.
- **i**: Cat, Cowrie Shell, Egg, Snakeskin.
- **j**: Goofer Dust, Graveyard Dirt, Red Brick Dust, Wood Ash.
- **k**: Sugar, Vinegar, Whiskey.

Each helper returned a fragment of the literal, an evidence file giving, for every value, the file and line (or the Wikipedia revision and wikitext line) where it is stated, and notes on what it left out. The lane, the agent coordinating the shard, sent the helpers corrections at 07:20 UTC (no named sort folded in as a form; no citation of the pinned Mojo revision), ran the guard over every fragment and over the assembled shard, and checked the evidence: in every batch it opened at least one book locator and one Wikipedia line and found each said what the evidence claimed. Among them: Kynes under Myrrh (the deity and powers lines) and under Cedar (the powers line); Cunningham's _Magical Herbs_ under Benzoin, under Aloes, Wood, under Sandarac, Gum, under Dragon's Blood and under Tragacanth Gum; Cunningham's _Crystal, Gem & Metal Magic_ p. 130 for the egg; and Wikipedia "Commiphora gileadensis", "Agarwood", "Dryobalanops aromatica", "Cat", "Oomancy" and "Galbanum". All 67 cited Wikipedia revisions were saved as raw wikitext by the helper that cited them, and the lane matched every cited title and revision id to a saved file and confirmed through the Wikipedia API that all 67 revisions exist under the titles cited. Every page and URL cited here was read during the task, on 2026-10-11.

**Wikipedia's API rate-limited several helpers** at about 07:10 UTC, answering HTTP 429, "too many requests". The helpers waited and resumed with requests spaced several seconds apart. Batch f lost about ten minutes to the wait; batch j's retry failed too, so it stopped, and its two unfinished entries (Red Brick Dust and Wood Ash) were finished in a follow-up at 07:19 to 07:22 UTC.

**Plants of the World Online and Mindat were tried once each** by the lane on 2026-10-11 at 07:06 UTC (`powo.science.kew.org/results?q=Commiphora%20myrrha`, `www.mindat.org/min-1923.html`). Both returned HTTP 403 with a Cloudflare challenge, as they did for the pilot. So no formal name here was checked against either; each was checked against the taxobox or infobox of the Wikipedia revision cited for it, and, where the article has none (Opopanax (perfumery), Tolu balsam, Tragacanth), against its text, with AHPA's monograph heading for Tolu Balsam. **No formal name on the candidate list changed.** The discrepancies the helpers found, each recorded and the list's name kept:

- **Tolu Balsam.** Wikipedia "Myroxylon balsamum" lists _Myroxylon balsamum_ var. _balsamum_ among the species' synonyms in its speciesbox, while its Varieties section keeps var. _balsamum_ as the Tolu balsam tree; Wikipedia "Tolu balsam" and AHPA p. 591 both name the variety. Grieve heads the page "Myrospermum Toluiferum", which Wikipedia lists as a synonym.
- **Opoponax.** Wikipedia "Opopanax (perfumery)" names _Commiphora guidottii_ in its short description, lead and "Botanical origin" section. The pinned "Incense" revision captions opoponax as "_Commiphora opoponax_"; Kynes gives opopanax as a name of _C. erythraea_, which the perfumery article calls a historical misidentification; and Grieve's Opoponax page is _Opopanax chironium_, a different plant.
- **Labdanum.** The pinned "Incense" revision captions labdanum's source as "_Cistus villosus_"; Wikipedia "Labdanum" names two source species, _C. ladanifer_ and _C. creticus_.
- **Makko.** The pinned "Incense" revision files makko under _Persea_ ("such as _Persea thunbergii_"); the species article gives _Persea thunbergii_ as a synonym, and Wikipedia "Machilus" says the boundary between the two genera has been disputed. The description says so.
- **Sweetgrass.** The speciesbox gives _Hierochloe odorata_, but the lead also names _Anthoxanthum nitens_ and says some authors include _Hierochloe_ in _Anthoxanthum_. The description says so.
- **Asafoetida.** Wikipedia "Ferula assa-foetida" says the species is a source of asafoetida but not the main one, and Wikipedia "Asafoetida" names three species; Grieve gives _Ferula foetida_. The description says it is one of several sources.
- **Synonyms in the sources.** Cunningham, Kynes, Grieve and AHPA print _Cinnamomum camphora_ for Camphor and _Daemonorops draco_ for Dragon's Blood; Cunningham heads Gum Arabic's entries _Acacia senegal_ and Copal's _Bursera odorata_ and "_B. fugaroides_" (his misspelling of _fagaroides_); Grieve heads Galbanum _Ferula galbaniflua_ and Balsam of Gilead _Commiphora opobalsamum_. The Wikipedia revision cited for each entry lists each of these, bar the misspelling, as a synonym of the list's name. Grieve also calls Borneo camphor _Dryobalanops camphora_, which batch c records as a synonym.
- **Titles.** "Tetraclinis articulata" redirects to "Tetraclinis", cited under its real title. Wikipedia marks "cowrie" as a paraphyletic name that also covers some Ovulidae and Triviidae; the list's Cypraeidae is the family its lead names.

Tragacanth (_Astragalus_) and Red Coral (_Corallium_) are written as the genus alone, the README's rule for a `spp.` row. Mother-of-Pearl is seeded as `unknown` with no formal name, the owner's decision of 2026-10-11 for the seven `unknown` rows; its description says why in one sentence (Wikipedia files it under nacre, a material several mollusc groups make, and Cunningham names no animal).

### Decisions this shard took

The pilot's decisions hold here (one citation per work per row; a category only on the source's own word; a deity only as the curated row the source names; colours from `colours.ts` only; both books' values where they disagree). These are the lane's rulings and the helpers' choices on top of them.

- **Cunningham's Akasha is not `spirit`.** Cunningham gives Red Coral, Mother-of-Pearl and fossils (Ammonite) the element Akasha, his "fifth element". He never calls it spirit, so it is not mapped to the curated `spirit`, and those three entries do not carry it.
- **"Ancestors" is Ancestor Work; "spirits" is not Spirit Work.** "Ancestors" is a direct form of the category's word, so Cedar (Kynes) and Graveyard Dirt carry Ancestor Work. "Spirits", "spirit guides" and "spirits of the dead" are not filed as Spirit Work, following the pilot, which left Kynes's "spirit guides/spirits" unmapped.
- **Other categories filed on the source's own word.** Storax's Hex-Breaking, from Kynes's "to aid in breaking hexes"; Red Sandalwood's Cleansing, from Cunningham's "cleansed of negativity"; Tragacanth's Transformation, from Cunningham's "transformation in the subconscious"; Red Coral's Sleep, Nightmare Protection, Courage and Love, from Cunningham's magical-uses text rather than his powers line; Ammonite's Warding and Healing, from Wikipedia. Cunningham's incense "to attract customers to your place of business" is not Business, as the pilot did not file Bladderwrack's "place of business".
- **Egg loses its cleansing line.** The Wicca egg-cleansing sentence and the Cleansing category came from a line of Wikipedia "Oomancy" whose reference is a commercial site, so both were removed, as the pilot rejected Mugwort's lucid-dreaming line. The Latin American evil-eye sentence, cited to another reference, stays.
- **Agarwood drops Lolu and Mapou.** Cunningham's heading names two plants, _Cardia dichotoma_ and _Aquilaria agallocha_, and nothing read assigns those two folk names to one of them.
- **Balm of Gilead takes nothing from Cunningham.** His heading names four plants at once, and his magical uses concern "the buds", which Grieve identifies as the poplar's Balm of Gilead buds. So his celestial body, element, powers and uses are not applied to _Commiphora gileadensis_, and the entry's folk names that his heading shares with Grieve are cited to Grieve.
- **A named sort is its own entry, never a form.** A sort a source heads as its own entry is its own entry (the owner's rule of 2026-10-11). No entry here folds one in: Aztec and black copal, the other dragon's-blood plants, asafoetida's and galbanum's commercial grades appear only as prose in descriptions, if at all.

Kynes's Myrrh lists opopanax under _C. erythraea_; her correspondences go to Myrrh only. Cunningham's Liquidamber names both _L. orientalis_ and _L. styraciflua_ in its formal-name line, so its element, celestial body and power go to both Storax and Sweet Gum, and its folk names are split by the species it attaches them to.
