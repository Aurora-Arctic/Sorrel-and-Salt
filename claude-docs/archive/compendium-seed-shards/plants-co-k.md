### Decisions the lane made

On top of the [pilot's](pilot.md) "Decisions the lanes inherit":

- **Categories come from a source's powers list only** (Cunningham's "Powers:", Kynes's powers line), never from a spell or a use, and only by the category's own word or a direct form of it. Kynes's paired terms file under each category whose word they print ("justice/legal matters" is Justice and Legal Matters; "balance/harmony" is Harmony); "safety during travel" is Safe Travel; "ancestors" is Ancestor Work; "nightmares" is Nightmare Protection; "manifest" is Manifestation, but not where the word is used in another sense. "Spirit guides" and "spirits" stay unmapped, as in the pilot.
- **A named sort is its own ingredient, never a form** (the owner's rule): another species, a variety or a cultivar a source discusses under an entry is listed below as "to be its own entry" and not folded in.
- **A deity Wikipedia ties to a plant through myth or iconography** rather than magic is kept where the article says so plainly, and marked "mythological, for the owner" below.
- **With no safety source for the whole plant**, the entry carries no safety note; a caution a source gives one part sits on that form.
- **The toxic flag** (M14.1) is on where a source says in so many words that the plant, a part of it or a form is poisonous or toxic to people, pets or animals, as the literal's safety note records; its source line names the source with "the toxic flag". Cunningham's health code X (not for internal use), a safety class, a drug interaction or a pregnancy caution sets no flag.
- **Grieve's pages are cited as web pages**, `grieve-<slug>`, so every shard renders them alike.

### What is left out, and why

No candidate row in part 1's range is left out. What each entry leaves out, as the research recorded it: powers with no category of that word, deities not on the curated list, colours not on the list or not the ingredient's own, forms with no curated row or nothing attached, named sorts "to be its own entry", and weak or unsourced lines. Where the lane changed an entry after its research, the last line under it says how.

- **Coconut**
  - **Left out.** Powers with no category of that word: Cunningham's chastity; Kynes's consecrate/bless, family and home, hope, sex/sexuality, spirituality. Deities not on the curated list or spelt otherwise: Kynes's Ganesh (curated Ganesha) and Sarasvati (curated Saraswati). Wikipedia calls the coconut a Hindu wedding "symbol of prosperity" (line 435): a symbol, not a power, so not mapped. Colours: the ripe husk is brown (Kynes) but the flesh and oil are not, so no colour is written. Forms: coconut milk, dried meat and coir (Kynes, Wikipedia) have nothing attached of their own; Kynes's Bead is kept because his spells use it. Safety: no safety book has a coconut monograph (AHPA names _Cocos nucifera_ only in a reference title, 0197.txt:21); Wikipedia's cardiovascular caution on dietary coconut oil (line 316) was left out under the brief's rule that Wikipedia supplies safety only for a poisonous plant no book covers. Kynes's Herbal Remedies topical note sits on the Oil form only.
  - **Lakshmi.** Wikipedia says Lakshmi "is often shown holding a coconut": an iconographic rather than magical association, kept as a deity in the pilot's spirit (Honey's Zeus) but flagged for the lane.

  - **Left out.** Powers with no category of that word: Cunningham's visions; Kynes's family and home, negativity (money is the other half of the mapped prosperity/money).
