## The stones and metals shard (MB.248)

The minerals, rocks, metals and alloys of the [candidate list](candidates.md): the 136 rows whose shard is `stones-and-metals`. The literal is `src/db/seed/compendium/stones-and-metals.ts`, and it is the source of truth; this doc records where each entry's values come from, what was left out, and why. Three of the 136 (Iron, Amethyst and Hematite) were seeded by the pilot ([`pilot.md`](pilot.md)) and are not repeated here.

**This pull request is part 1 of the shard.** It seeds **58 entries with 91 forms** beneath them. They cover 58 of the 133 roster rows the shard researches: the agates and chalcedonies, the quartz varieties, the beryls, chrysoberyls, corundums, tourmalines, garnet, topaz, spinel, diamond and zircon, pipestone, the eleven metals and alloys, the four `unknown` rows (electrum, meteorite, lava stone, petrified wood), holey stone, flint, pumice and marble. The other 75 roster rows follow in a later pull request on the same task (MB.248), listed under "Not yet in this part" below. No roster row was left out of part 1: every row it set out to seed is seeded. Every page and revision cited in the literal was read during the task, on 11 October 2026.

### How the research was done

The lane split the 133 rows into 34 batches of three or four. Related stones went together, so that one batch read one Wikipedia article for all of its rows: the agates; the chalcedonies; carnelian with sard, onyx and sardonyx; the quartz varieties; the beryls; the chrysoberyls; the corundums; the metals. Each batch went to a helper agent working to one brief, which restates the README's rules: the owner's rule, the closed lists, a category only by the source's own word, a deity only as a curated row, and colours only from `colours.ts`. Each helper wrote its entries and an evidence file. For every value, the evidence file names the citation, the file and line it rests on, and a short excerpt from that line.

The lead ran MB.242's guard over each batch. For each helper batch it also opened at least one Cunningham page, one Hall entry and one Wikipedia line from the evidence file, to confirm that they say what the batch claims. A batch was accepted only after both checks. Five batches (gold, silver and copper; lead, tin and mercury; aluminium, antimony, brass and steel; the four `unknown` rows; holey stone, flint, pumice and marble) the lead researched itself, when the shared limit on concurrent helpers left no slot free; they rest on Cunningham's metal and stone pages and the Wikipedia revisions alone, since Hall has no entry for them. Where the lead corrected a batch, the correction is listed under "What is left out, and why" or "Decisions", below.

**Wikipedia.** The lead fetched every article the roster names before the helpers started, between 07:06 and 07:17 UTC on 11 October 2026. It looked up each article's current revision through the API (`action=query&prop=revisions&redirects=1`), then read that revision's wikitext through `index.php?…&oldid=…&action=raw`, pausing 1.5 seconds between requests. The few further articles a helper needed were fetched the same way. A citation's `url` is the revision permalink that was read.

**Mindat** was tried once, at 07:05:44 UTC on 11 October 2026 (`https://www.mindat.org/min-3337.html`). It returned HTTP 403 and a Cloudflare challenge page ("Just a moment..."), as it had for MB.240 and the pilot. No further request was made. Every formal name was therefore checked against the infobox or opening sentence of the Wikipedia revision cited for it, and against Cunningham's and Hall's own descriptions.

**Cross-shard substitutes are typed names.** A substitute links an identity only when that identity is in the pilot or in this shard. The other shards (the plants, and the resins, woods and other ingredients) were being written alongside this one, and the guard checks a link against the shards already in `COMPENDIUM`. So a substitute in another shard (coral, for instance) is written as typed text, `{ name }`, which the substitutes table allows.

**Time and cost.** A helper took one to two minutes an entry by its own clock, and 117,000 to 158,000 tokens for a batch of three or four, about 35,000 to 40,000 tokens an entry (the agents' usage reports; an estimate per entry), in line with the pilot's figure.

### Decisions and questions for the owner

- **Formal names were kept as the roster has them**, as the coordinator directed. The `Quartz var. …` names follow the `standard` fixture's convention, and the pilot has already seeded `Quartz var. amethyst`. Which sources print the variety form, or call the stone a variety, for the owner to revisit:
  - Wikipedia's infobox gives "Variety of quartz (chalcedony)" for Chrysoprase and Pietersite, and "Variety of corundum" for Ruby. No source read prints the `Corundum var. …` or `Quartz var. …` string itself.
  - Jasper: Wikipedia's infobox calls it an "aggregate rock (impure chalcedony variety)", and Cunningham a variety of chalcedony, which is a form of quartz (p. 104).
  - Tiger's Eye: no source read calls it a variety of quartz. Wikipedia calls it usually a metamorphic rock of the quartz group, and Cunningham calls it asbestos associated with quartz (p. 75).
  - Moss Agate: Wikipedia calls it a chalcedony and "not a true form of agate". Bloodstone: Wikipedia says it is mostly jasper rather than chalcedony. Both are still quartz.
  - Cat's Eye: Cunningham says the name usually means quartz holding asbestos, and only the ancient Asian stone was chrysoberyl (p. 81). Hall files chrysoberyl under "Beryl" and calls it a form of beryl, which Wikipedia contradicts.
  - Pipestone: Cunningham describes a circular brick-red stone with a natural hole (p. 126), but his Minnesota origin and pipe-making match catlinite, and Wikipedia says "pipestone" covers stones besides catlinite.
  - Tourmaline and Garnet are mineral groups on Wikipedia, not single species.
- **Category judgements**, each by a direct form of the category's word: Nightmare Protection from "anti-nightmare" or "halt nightmares"; Safe Travel from "guards its bearer while travelling" (Chalcedony) and from folk beliefs Wikipedia states (Topaz); Attraction from "attracts wealth" or "attract money"; Ancestor Work from "heal the ancestral line" (Bloodstone); Clarity from "clarifies"; Hex-Breaking from "defeating negative spells (hexes)" (Sard); Grief Work from "grief"; Friendship from "friends"; Psychic Work from "psychic awareness"; Confidence from "self-confidence"; Courage from "courageous"; Creativity from "creative"; Release from "releases"; Reconciliation from "reconcile". The owner may drop any.
- **Cautions that are not toxicity.** Smoky Quartz carries Hall's advice to avoid artificially irradiated stones, and Onyx carries Cunningham's warning against using it to repress sexual desire. Emerald carries Hall's advice not to wear it all the time. The owner may decide whether these belong in a safety note.
- **Dust and toxicity notes** come from Wikipedia: silica dust from working agate and quartz, beryllium in beryl, asbestos fibres in tiger's eye, and the toxicity of lead, mercury and antimony.
- **The toxic flag** (M14.1) is on where a source says in so many words that the plant, a part of it or a form is poisonous or toxic to people, pets or animals, as the literal's safety note records; its source line names the source with "the toxic flag". Cunningham's health code X (not for internal use), a safety class, a drug interaction or a pregnancy caution sets no flag.
- **Corrections the lead made to helper batches:** Emerald's deity Vishnu was restored (the curated row exists; the helper's search missed it); Black Tourmaline's folk names were trimmed to Shorl, Common Schorl and Iron Tourmaline, dropping Wikipedia's historical German spellings; two substitutes, Aventurine's Amazonite and Emerald's Peridot, are typed names until those rows are seeded in part 2.
- **Cunningham's "Akasha"**, his fifth element, is not mapped to `spirit` (Meteorite, Petrified Wood), as MB.247 decided.
