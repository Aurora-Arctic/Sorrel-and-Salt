**N1.1 — Sweep Expired Sign-In Rows** · 2h

_Story:_ As the person running the site, I want expired sessions, verifications and rate-limit counters removed on a schedule, so that the site keeps security data no longer than it needs.

The existing daily job (`src/lib/jobs`, M15.49) runs the sweep as the System account.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- These deletes are named exceptions in `.claude/rules/database.md`
- A test seeds rows on both sides of each cut-off
- The privacy policy states each retention period: sessions 7 days, and each other period exactly as coded

**N1.2 — Ask Before Remembering the Sign-In Method** · 2h

_Story:_ As a visitor, I want the site to remember which way I sign in only if I ask it to, so that it stores nothing on my device I did not agree to and never needs a consent banner.

The `better-auth.last_used_login_method` cookie (`src/lib/sign-in.ts`) shows a "last used" hint on the sign-in page. It is a convenience, not strictly necessary, so the EU rule (ePrivacy Directive article 5(3)) needs consent before it is set. The UK's rules from 5 February 2026 (PECR Schedule A1 paragraph 6) might exempt it, but the EU's do not. The owner chose on 2 November 2026 to keep the cookie and ask for consent on the sign-in page itself, where the cookie is used, rather than remove it or add a site-wide banner (`design-decisions/support.md`, "Decisions for the owner"). The `theme` and `card-density` local-storage items stay as they are, stated in the policy under the UK exemption (same file, "Cookies and browser storage").

_Acceptance criteria:_

- The sign-in page has a "Remember My Sign-In Method on This Device" checkbox, unticked by default and labelled for assistive technology
- Signing in with the box unticked sets no `better-auth.last_used_login_method` cookie and clears one already set; signing in with it ticked sets it
- The box shows ticked when the cookie is present, so a person can withdraw consent by unticking it and signing in
- The "last used" hint shows only when the cookie is present
- A test asserts that signing in with the box unticked sets no cookie outside the authentication set
- The privacy policy names the cookie as set only when the person ticks the box, says how to withdraw, and names every other item the site stores on a device: the short-lived sign-in handshake and two-factor cookies, the 30-day two-factor trust cookie, `admin_session`, and the `theme`, `card-density` and `autosave:*` local-storage items, each with its purpose
- The policy says a person can object to the preference items and how

**N1.3 — Add the Privacy Requests Table** · 4h

_Story:_ As a developer, I want one table that holds requests about a person's data, so that copies, coven exports and later erasure share a base.

A table task only: no service, no policy. Named `privacy_requests` rather than a name tied to copies, so N1.13 can add a kind.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- `privacy_requests` holds the kind, subject, coven and status, with `kind` as checked text that a later migration can widen (person copy, coven copy)
- The zip parts are `bytea`, declared with Drizzle's `customType`, since Drizzle has no `bytea` builder
- It holds the link token hash, the HMAC code hash with its expiry, tries and send count, and the download grant hash with its expiry; each is nullable so a kind may leave it unused
- It holds `not_before` and the lifecycle times (queued, built, mailed, cleared)
- It spreads `...auditColumns`, with the `updated_at` trigger in the same migration
- Checks tie the coven to the coven kind and forbid it on the person kind
- A partial unique index allows one waiting request per subject (and per coven for the coven kind), and every unique index is partial on `deleted_at IS NULL`
- The migration is forward-only and expand-only; the catalogue test passes

**N1.5 — Assemble a Person's Copy of Their Data** · 18h

_Story:_ As a member, I want a copy of everything the site holds that names me, so that I can see it and take my own writing with me.

The copy service across the modules, with the guards the scope sets. The classification map and the shared test fixtures are built for erasure to reuse and hold nothing erasure-specific.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- Part 1 holds the rows the person created in their current covens (spells with steps, assigned categories and workings; coven ingredients with variants, substitutes, folk names, deities and references; stock rows; annotations on compendium entries; their support requests and messages)
- Part 2 holds the account, covens, staff records, sign-in and mail, workings that name them, rows they last changed but did not create (kind, title and date), rows where they were only the actor (kind, id and date), and an "about" section linking the privacy policy
- Deleted rows are included and marked deleted
- A coven the person has left gives its membership row and counts only, in every section
- A deleted coven counts as current only if the person was a member when it was deleted
- Secrets and `rate_limits` rows are left out
- Other people appear by name only where the app already shows the name to the requester; internal support reply authors, `sessions.impersonated_by` and every name in a left coven take a role label
- The copy's finders are a named exception to CLAUDE.md rule 5 (they take the subject's id and filter on it in SQL; the private-spell finder also requires `created_by` to be the subject), written into CLAUDE.md and `.claude/rules/database.md`, with a guard test shaped like `tests/guards/invitation-token-rotation.test.ts` proving only the copy service reaches them
- A classification map marks every table and every user-referencing column as the subject's own, another person, secret or not personal; a catalogue test fails on any table or column missing from it
- A leak test over the finished file uses shared fixtures that fill every table for every fixture user, including a left coven, a coven deleted while a member, a coven left and then deleted, an internal support reply and an impersonated session; the copies for B and D contain no other user's id, email address, IP address, private spell title or foreign row id
- Direct-id tests show the service refuses any subject but the caller's own, each with its precondition
- `data-copy.md` describes the copy; the PR body says how each filter was shown to matter

_Story:_ As a member, I want the link to ask for a code mailed to me, so that a forwarded mail does not hand my data to someone else.

_Acceptance criteria:_

**N1.13 — Erase an Account in Place When Its Holder Asks** · 8h

_Story:_ As a keeper answering a privacy request, I want to erase everything that identifies the person behind an account while keeping the account, so that the site honours erasure under the rule that an account is paused, never closed.

Builds on N1.12's column and M15.46's request anonymiser. A request counts as verified when the account sends it while signed in: the account's own sign-in (password, passkey or a provider, plus two-factor where it is on) is the identity check. The `privacy` subject on `/support?support=privacy` records the sender as `created_by`, and a paused account can still sign in and reach support, so pausing never stops anyone from asking. A signed-out request carries only an address the sender typed, which proves nothing, so it is not acted on directly. The admin replies asking the person to sign in and send the request from their account. If the person cannot sign in any more, the admin mails the address held on the account, never the typed address, and acts once that mailbox replies. The site never asks for an identity document. The one-month clock (GDPR article 12(3)) starts once identity is confirmed; M15.58's scope confirms this against the ICO's guidance before the privacy policy cites it.

Builds after the N · Wave 01 tasks it may reuse ([`support.md`, "A way for people to take their own writing with them"](../../design-decisions/support.md#a-way-for-people-to-take-their-own-writing-with-them), "Built for reuse by erasure"): the classification map and its catalogue test (N1.5), the subject-scoped finders and the "fill every table" test fixtures (N1.5), the `privacy_requests` table (N1.3), the link-and-code confirmation (N1.8) and the keeper action that names a support request (N1.9). This task chooses how far to use them; the scope decides nothing about erasure.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- An admin-only `eraseAccount(userId, supportRequestId, note)` runs through `assertSiteAdmin`. A curator or member is refused, by direct id as well, and the test shows what the refusal stops
- It is refused, each with an explaining error, when:
  - the request was not sent while signed in by that same account (the identity rule above)
  - the account is the System account or the signed-out sender account
  - the account holds a keeper or curator role, which must be removed first so the ledger records it
  - the account owns a coven where it is the only steward
- In one `withAudit` transaction it:
  - sets `name`, `image`, `former_email` and `pending_email` to null or a placeholder
  - sets the email to `erased-<id>@invalid` and sets `erased_at`
  - hard-deletes the account's `sessions`, `accounts`, `passkeys`, `two_factors`, `sign_in_failures` and outstanding `verifications`
  - sets an open-ended site pause whose note names the request
  - anonymises the account's own support threads with M15.46's anonymiser
  - clears the address on any open invitation to it
  - clears a working-participant name that is the account's
- A pause on an erased account cannot be lifted, and the refusal explains why
- Content the account wrote in shared covens stays with each coven, credited to no name, and the privacy policy says so
- The person can sign up again with the same address and gets a new, empty account
- An Erase Account button with a confirmation dialog sits in the People page's user modal, admin only
- The privacy policy's "How to Ask for Removal" and the Terms of Use's "Leaving" section describe exactly this, and the policy gains a paragraph on how a person's identity is checked
- The privacy policy and the Terms of Use state how erasure fits the rule that an account is paused, never closed
- The privacy policy (M15.2) is corrected wherever the M15.58 scope finds it wrong, and gains the GDPR sections it lacks: who runs the site, the lawful basis for each kind of data, a person's rights, transfers and the processors' agreements, and how to complain to a supervisory authority. While the site is on Vercel Hobby, which has no data processing agreement (the owner's choice, 2 November 2026; M15.58 scope, "Decisions for the owner"), the policy names Vercel as a processor without claiming an agreement with it
- The erasure transaction also clears the account’s `privacy_requests` rows and their stored parts, codes and grants, and the daily job skips an erased account when it builds copies
- Proved by a browser story: a keeper erases an account from its signed-in request, the person can no longer sign in, and nothing shows the old name
