**M14.1 — The Compendium Seed Flags What Is Poisonous** · 6h

_Story: As a reader, I want every compendium ingredient and form that a source calls poisonous to be flagged, with the source named, so that I am warned before I handle, burn or take it._

M11.21 gives each ingredient and each of its forms a toxic flag of its own. The two flags are independent, the one exception to the union rule (DESIGN.md §5, "The toxic flag is the one exception"). The ingredient's flag is on when any part of it is poisonous, and a form's when that form is. Nothing seeded sets either flag today. This task gives the seed the flag, and sets it on the entries already seeded. The open shards set it on their own entries as they seed them (MB.243 to MB.246, MB.248). It waits for M11.21, whose columns it writes.

**The mechanism comes first, in its own pull request**, since the open shards' next parts write through it:

- `SeedCompendiumEntry` and `SeedCompendiumVariant` in `src/db/seed/types.ts` take `toxic?: true`. Leaving it out means not flagged, which is the column's default. It takes `true` alone, because a written `false` would claim the plant is not poisonous, and that claim would need a source of its own.
- `toxic` joins `CompendiumEntryField` and `CompendiumVariantField`, so a citation's `supports` may name it. The guard counts it as populated when it is on, so a flag no citation supports fails the shard, as any other field does.
- `seedCompendium` writes both flags.
- The seed README (`claude-docs/db/compendium-seed/README.md`) gains `toxic` on both sides of "What `supports` may name", and "An entry, field by field" says when each flag is on.

**When a flag is on.** Only where a source read during the task says in so many words that the plant, or a part of it, is poisonous or toxic, to people or to pets (the owner, 2026-10-16); to whom, and how, is the safety notes' to say. Cunningham's _Encyclopedia of Magical Herbs_ marks such an entry "Poison" beside its heading, and sometimes names the part: under "Mahogany, Mountain" it reads "Seed and Leaves: Poison". The AHPA handbook, LiverTox, NCCIH, Kynes's cautions and Wikipedia's toxicity sections stand where they stand for safety notes (the README, "Which sources stand where"). A safety class on its own, a drug interaction or a caution in pregnancy says nothing about poison and sets no flag. The ingredient's flag is on when a source says any part of it is poisonous. A form's flag is on only when a source says that form is, and only on a form the entry carries; no form is added just to carry a flag. A preparation that is toxic when the plant is not, an essential oil say, flags its own form and not the ingredient: an oil is not a part of the plant, and whether the ingredient takes the flag as well is left to an admin's or curator's discretion on the compendium page (the owner, 2026-10-16), never the seed's. The citation names `toxic` in its `supports`, on the entry or on the form. A flag that no read source supports stays off, and the shard doc says so under "What is left out, and why". The rule applies to every shard, stones and metals included.

**A safety note and the flag agree** (the owner, 2026-10-21). Some cautions are proof that the flag belongs on: where a sourced `safetyNotes` value says the entry, or a form, is poisonous, toxic or fatal if swallowed, that entry or form is flagged, cited with `toxic` in `supports`. The pass over the entries already seeded reads each note as well as the sources, and sets any flag a note proves; a note about a dose, a drug interaction or pregnancy on its own sets none. The shards' mechanism is the same rule, and MB.249 checks it across the whole seed.

**The entries already seeded** are the pilot's, MB.247's shard, and the first part of each of the five open shards. Each is read against its sources for poison, flagged in its literal, and given a source line in its shard doc. The seed writes an entry once (the README, "Write-once, and how a correction lands"), so the literal edit reaches no database that already holds the entry. Staging, and production wherever a release has seeded it, are brought in line by one of two routes, chosen and argued in the PR body as MB.249 chooses its own:

- the README's correction route, an admin turning the flags on and adding the citations on the admin compendium page;
- or a one-off step that sets the flags and links the citations on entries present by `seed_key` and unchanged by an admin since the seed wrote them.

If MB.249's repair has landed by then, use its route.

The task may ship in parts, each pull request `Refs` until the last, as the shards do: the mechanism first, then the flags on the seeded entries.

The 6 hours are an estimate: about 1 for the mechanism and its tests, on the guard MB.242 built; about 4 for reading the entries already seeded for poison, much lighter per entry than a shard's research since it is one field and the entries already cite their safety sources; and about 1 for bringing staging in line.

Tests are written first:

- `tests/db/seed/compendium.test.ts`: the fixture shard's flagged entry and flagged form are seeded flagged, and the unflagged ones are not

**M14.7 — The Seed Updates A File Marked For It** · 9h

_Story:_ As the owner, I want a change to a compendium file to reach the database only when I have marked that file for update, and never over a curator's or an editor's work, so that I can correct an entry from a file without losing anything a person did on the site.

The seed today skips every entry already present (`presentEntries` in `src/db/seed/compendium.ts`). This task gives it an update path, behind the hash from M14.6 and the columns from M14.5. It needs both.

**The refactor comes first, in its own commit, with no change in behaviour:** extract pure row builders (`entryRow`, `variantRows`, `childRows`, `referenceRows`) out of `seedCompendiumContent`, so that insert and update share them. Do not reuse `writeChildren` (`src/modules/ingredients/services/ingredient-rows.ts`): it is a `server-only` service writer that needs a `withAudit` session. Read it first and mirror how it keeps variant ids stable.

Then, for each `approved` file (the seed never writes another status):

| State                                                       | Action                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Absent (no `seed_key`, identity or slug match)              | Insert as today and store the hash                                                                                                                                                              |
| Present and soft-deleted                                    | `RETIRED`, exit 0: a curator removed it and the seed never revives it                                                                                                                           |
| Stored hash equals the file's and no field differs          | Skip                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Hash matches, a field differs, a person worked on the row   | Skip quietly, keeping the work (curator-reviewed, updated by a person, or a child touched or deleted); only `--force` (M14.26) overwrites it (M14.1)                                            |
| Hash matches, a field differs, nobody worked on the row     | Drift (M14.1): `PENDING` when `seedUpdate` is not the file's hash; applied, subject to the identity and form-in-use refusals, when it is                                                        |
| Stored hash is null                                         | Adopt it if the conversion manifest names it and the row and all its children are bootstrap-stamped, else `REFUSED: no baseline`                                                                |
| Hash differs and `seedUpdate` is not the file's hash        | `PENDING`, exit 0, counted in the summary                                                                                                                                                       |
| Hash differs and `seedUpdate` equals the file's hash        | Refuse, unless `overwriteEditor: true`, when the row is curator-reviewed, was updated by anyone but the bootstrap user, or has a child row touched by a person or soft-deleted; otherwise apply |
| The update would change `name`, `canonicalName` or the slug | `REFUSED: identity change: use the admin`                                                                                                                                                       |

An applied update runs inside the seed's one transaction, stamped with the bootstrap user. It updates the scalars and the hash; upserts variants by their folded form and never deletes and recreates one (stock and spell layers hold variant ids), soft-deleting a removed form only when it is unused and otherwise `REFUSED: form in use`; diffs folk names, deities and substitutes with soft delete; diffs categories with hard delete; and diffs reference links by reference, owner and locator, never deleting a reference row. An `overwriteEditor` update clears `curator_reviewed_at` and `curator_reviewed_by` in the same statement. This is an assumption the owner is asked to confirm (decision record, "Stated assumption").

**Reporting.** The run prints one line per `UPDATED`, `REFUSED`, `PENDING` and `RETIRED` entry, a `REFUSED` line followed by the fields that differ, then a totals line, and exits 1 if anything was refused (a refused entry is skipped and the rest of the run applies). **M14.11 — The Compendium Attribution Audit** · 5h

_Story:_ As the owner, I want every seeded entry checked for a source cited under a common name that could mean another species, so that no entry carries another plant's lore the way Aspen does.

Its inputs are the entry files, the sources and works, and the Wikipedia pages already fetched under `/home/node/.claude/projects/-app/seed-scratch/shards/*/wiki/<Title>.wikitext`. A citation's `headedAs` is derived from its locator (`under "Aspen"`) or the source's title. Each heuristic marks a candidate per entry and citation:

- **Corpus collision:** the heading is another file's name or folk name, for another formal name. Aspen is the first catch, and this needs no wikitext.
- **Hatnote:** the common-name page's wikitext has `{{about}}`, `{{for}}`, `{{other uses}}`, `{{redirect}}`, `{{distinguish}}` or `{{see also}}` naming two or more taxa other than the entry's, or is a disambiguation page (`{{disambiguation}}`, or "may refer to:" in its lead) whose list lines name two or more taxa other than the entry's.
- **No binomial:** `headedAs` contains no taxon name and the citation is not `stated` or `resolved`.
- **Own-taxon check:** a cited Wikipedia article whose `{{Speciesbox}}` or `{{Automatic taxobox}}` matches the entry's `canonicalName` is reported as `stated`; the audit does not write it.

The report is a ranked table: entry, source, heading, candidate formal names, the evidence and a score. `--apply`, run only after the owner has read the report, takes the candidates that name another formal name (`--all` takes every one), sets `status: needs-review` and appends one block to `#### Questions for the Curator` per flagged citation, each opening with `<!-- audit:<source>:<headedAs> -->`, so a second run adds nothing. The books are not available as text, so the audit finds candidates; a lane or the owner asserts `stated` or `resolved` from the page. The task adds the ratchet test that the `unaudited` list never grows, and the script's row in `claude-docs/commands.md`.

`--resolve` (with `--fetch` to fill its cache, `--dry-run` to write nothing) settles by Wikipedia what the owner would otherwise read one by one. A pending citation headed "X" becomes `nameBasis: resolved`, with `headedAs: X` and `resolvedBy` the entry's own Wikipedia source, only when Wikipedia's page "X" is, or redirects to, the article the entry cites for its own formal name (a source cited as `stated`, or whose fetched taxobox names the formal name). A disambiguation page, a genus page, another species' page, no page, or a heading another entry uses as a name resolves nothing. Redirect answers come from the MediaWiki API into a local cache file outside git (`seed-scratch/wiki-lookups.json`); the resolving run reads only local data. It never changes a `stated` citation.

Y", when the page is Y's own article and its infobox or first sentence says it is a variety of X. The audit's own-article check and `--resolve` share the one test, `pageNamesEntry`.
