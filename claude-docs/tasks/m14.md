### The compendium seed

This task is numbered M14 because it was minted for Wave 14, on the owner's call (2026-10-16), and its subject belongs with the compendium seed that the wave finishes: the seed sets the toxic flags M11.21 adds.

**M14.1 — The Compendium Seed Flags What Is Poisonous** · 6h

_Story: As a reader, I want every compendium ingredient and form that a source calls poisonous to be flagged, with the source named, so that I am warned before I handle, burn or take it._

M11.21 gives each ingredient and each of its forms a toxic flag of its own. The two flags are independent, the one exception to the union rule (DESIGN.md §5, "The toxic flag is the one exception"). The ingredient's flag is on when any part of it is poisonous, and a form's when that form is. Nothing seeded sets either flag today. This task gives the seed the flag, and sets it on the entries already seeded. The open shards set it on their own entries as they seed them (MB.243 to MB.246, MB.248). It waits for M11.21, whose columns it writes.

**The mechanism comes first, in its own pull request**, since the open shards' next parts write through it:

- `SeedCompendiumEntry` and `SeedCompendiumVariant` in `src/db/seed/types.ts` take `toxic?: true`. Leaving it out means not flagged, which is the column's default. It takes `true` alone, because a written `false` would claim the plant is not poisonous, and that claim would need a source of its own.
- `toxic` joins `CompendiumEntryField` and `CompendiumVariantField`, so a citation's `supports` may name it. The guard counts it as populated when it is on, so a flag no citation supports fails the shard, as any other field does.
- `seedCompendium` writes both flags.
- The seed README (`claude-docs/db/compendium-seed/README.md`) gains `toxic` on both sides of "What `supports` may name", and "An entry, field by field" says when each flag is on.

**When a flag is on.** Only where a source read during the task says in so many words that the plant, or a part of it, is poisonous or toxic, to people or to pets (the owner, 2026-10-16); to whom, and how, is the safety notes' to say. Cunningham's _Encyclopedia of Magical Herbs_ marks such an entry "Poison" beside its heading, and sometimes names the part: under "Mahogany, Mountain" it reads "Seed and Leaves: Poison". The AHPA handbook, LiverTox, NCCIH, Kynes's cautions and Wikipedia's toxicity sections stand where they stand for safety notes (the README, "Which sources stand where"). A safety class on its own, a drug interaction or a caution in pregnancy says nothing about poison and sets no flag. The ingredient's flag is on when a source says any part of it is poisonous. A form's flag is on only when a source says that form is, and only on a form the entry carries; no form is added just to carry a flag. A preparation that is toxic when the plant is not, an essential oil say, flags its own form and not the ingredient: an oil is not a part of the plant, and whether the ingredient takes the flag as well is left to an admin's or curator's discretion on the compendium page (the owner, 2026-10-16), never the seed's. The citation names `toxic` in its `supports`, on the entry or on the form. A flag that no read source supports stays off, and the shard doc says so under "What is left out, and why". The rule applies to every shard, stones and metals included.

**The entries already seeded** are the pilot's, MB.247's shard, and the first part of each of the five open shards. Each is read against its sources for poison, flagged in its literal, and given a source line in its shard doc. The seed writes an entry once (the README, "Write-once, and how a correction lands"), so the literal edit reaches no database that already holds the entry. Staging, and production wherever a release has seeded it, are brought in line by one of two routes, chosen and argued in the PR body as MB.249 chooses its own:

- the README's correction route, an admin turning the flags on and adding the citations on the admin compendium page;
- or a one-off step that sets the flags and links the citations on entries present by `seed_key` and unchanged by an admin since the seed wrote them.

If MB.249's repair has landed by then, use its route.

The task may ship in parts, each pull request `Refs` until the last, as the shards do: the mechanism first, then the flags on the seeded entries.

The 6 hours are an estimate: about 1 for the mechanism and its tests, on the guard MB.242 built; about 4 for reading the entries already seeded for poison, much lighter per entry than a shard's research since it is one field and the entries already cite their safety sources; and about 1 for bringing staging in line.

Tests are written first:

- `tests/db/seed/compendium-literals.test.ts`, with no database: a flagged entry and a flagged form that no citation supports each fail the guard, on an invented fixture
- `tests/db/seed/compendium.test.ts`: the fixture shard's flagged entry and flagged form are seeded flagged, and the unflagged ones are not

_Acceptance criteria:_

- A seeded entry or form is flagged exactly when its literal says `toxic: true`, and the guard fails any flag no citation supports
- Every entry already seeded is checked, each flag set on it is cited with `toxic` in `supports`, and its shard doc's source line names the source
- The seed README's field table and field rules name `toxic`
- On staging, each entry seeded before this task that a source calls poisonous carries its flags and their citations, and the PR body names the route taken
- `npm run db:seed:compendium` stays idempotent from empty and after `standard`
- Per-file coverage of the touched `src/` directories not below the previous run's `.reports/coverage/coverage-summary.json`, or the exception named in the PR body

### Physical uses

This task is numbered M14 because it was minted for Wave 14, on the owner's call (2026-10-18): what it decides is recorded and seeded beside the toxic flags M14.1 sets, so it sits after M14.1 and ahead of the open shards, whose later parts could then record uses.

**M14.2 — Decide How an Ingredient Records Its Physical Uses** · 1.5h

_Story:_ As a site admin, coven owner or member, I want to record what an ingredient does to the body (aphrodisiac, sedative, stimulant, digestive), so that we can find ingredients by their physical effect as well as by their magical intent.

A discovery task: its only output is a written recommendation, and it changes no code. The categories we have today describe magical intent. This task works out how a physical use is recorded instead: as a curated vocabulary the admin maintains (as the form vocabulary and its groups are), as free text, or as an extension of categories. It also decides whether a use belongs on the ingredient or on a variant, since MB.215 puts safety notes and categories per variant because the sources give cautions per part and preparation. And it settles how a claim about the body is sourced (MB.151) and how it sits beside the safety note (M8.13a) and the toxic flag (M11.21), so that it never reads as medical advice. The recommendation is recorded in `claude-docs/design-decisions/m14.2-physical-uses.md`.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- The record compares at least three ways to record a physical use, recommends one, and names the tables and code each would touch
- It draws the line between a physical use and a category, with examples from the seeded categories
- It says whether a use sits on the ingredient or on a variant, under MB.215
- It gives a starting list of physical uses, each with at least one Chicago-form source read during the task
- It says how a use is shown beside the safety note and the toxic flag, so it never reads as medical advice
- It says who may set a use in each tier (admins and curators on the compendium; owners and members on a coven's own entries), and whether a use can be searched or filtered by
- It proposes the follow-up build tasks, a table task separate from a behaviour task; minting them waits for the owner's review of the record
- No code changes

### Forced colours

This task is numbered M14 because it was minted for Wave 14, on the owner's call (2026-10-20): a pass over a whole section's look is a close-out, and it reads best once the section's other changes (the design reviews MB.120 and MB.121 among them) have landed.

**M14.3 — A Forced-Colours Pass Over the Compendium Section** · 5h

_Story:_ As a visitor who uses a forced-colours mode (Windows High Contrast, for one), I want every card, panel, menu and control in the compendium to show where it starts and ends and which state it is in, so that I can read and use the section as anyone else can.

Forced colours replaces a page's colours with the system's few, and drops shadows and background images. A shape drawn by a shadow or a background colour alone then vanishes. Today only the shared form primitives (`src/scss/_primitives.scss`) carry a rule for it, through the `forced-colors` mixin (`src/scss/_mixins.scss`), and the `PublicFrame` current-page mark has its own. MB.120's design review looked at the compendium section's components and found several that rely on a shadow or a fill, and wrote each one's finding into its doc under "Forced colours": `IngredientCard` (its edge is `modal-surface`'s shadow, so a card has no edge), `MenuButton` (an item's hover and chosen state is a background), `AddIcon` (its hover is a background wash), `EntryMarks`, `CategoryChips` and `FacetPanel` (each with no rule of its own, leaning on the border they keep). The owner chose to do the one pass over the section rather than a fix per component (2026-10-20).

The task surveys the section under emulated forced colours (Playwright's `forcedColors: 'active'`), lists every place a shape or a state is lost, and fixes each in the component's own stylesheet with the `forced-colors` mixin, using a border, an outline or the system colours (`Canvas`, `CanvasText`, `Highlight`, `LinkText`) and no new token. The section is the public compendium list and entry page, the coven's ingredient list and its cards, the Quick Look and details modals, the pinned search row, and the admin compendium list; the survey adds anything else it finds there. This is a pass over the compendium and its cards only: the cupboard, grimoire and account screens are not in it.

Spun off from MB.120's design review, 2026-10-20 (item 52 of [`design-decisions/mb.120-plan.md`](../design-decisions/mb.120-plan.md)). Needs nothing still open; it goes after the section's design reviews so it checks the finished look once.

The 5 hours are an estimate: about 1 for the survey, about 2.5 for the fixes, about 1 for the spec, and about 0.5 for the docs.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- Under forced colours every card, the pinned search row, a panel, a menu and a dialog in the section shows an edge
- Every state that is set by a background alone (a hover, a chosen item, a switched-on control) also shows by a border, an outline or text
- Focus is visible on every control in the section, and a disabled control still reads as disabled
- Each fix is in its component's own stylesheet through the `forced-colors` mixin, and `npm run lint:css` passes
- A gate spec under `forcedColors: 'active'` asserts the computed border or outline of a card, a menu, a panel and a chosen item (the repo takes no snapshots), and the section passes axe
- The "Forced colours" paragraph of each of `ingredient-card.md`, `menu-button.md`, `add-icon.md`, `entry-marks.md`, `category-chips.md` and `facet-panel.md` says what the pass did, and `claude-docs/styling.md` names the forced-colours rules the pass added
