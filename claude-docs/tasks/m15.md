### Support

This task is numbered M15 because it was minted for Wave 15, on the owner's call (2026-10-13, during the Wave 11 review), and its subject belongs with no one milestone: how a person using the site reaches whoever runs it. [Wave 15](../waves/wave-15.md) runs it first.

**M15.1 — Scope a support form reviewed by admins** · 3h

_Story: As a person using the site, I want to send a question, a problem or a bug report — an accessibility bug included — to whoever runs it from inside the site, so that I am not left searching for an address; and as an admin, I want those messages in one list I can work through, answer, and hand to an editor or another admin, so that nothing is lost in a mailbox._

A scoping task, in the shape of MB.175's: it decides, records and mints, and builds nothing itself. The site has no way to be contacted today. The shape the owner asked for is a form whose submissions are rows in a database table, reviewed by admins on the admin section rather than mailed to them. The record it produces is `claude-docs/design-decisions/m15.1-support-form.md`, and each decision below is answered there, with its reason, before the build tasks are minted.

The decisions to make:

- **Who can submit.** The compendium is the one public surface (MB.80), so a signed-out reader may have a question too. Decide whether the form takes submissions from a signed-out visitor, with the spam exposure that brings, or only from a signed-in account, which is what every other write here requires.
- **The kinds of request.** A question, a bug, and an accessibility bug at least, added on the owner's call (2026-10-15) so that the code of ethics (M15.4) can point to this form as the way an accessibility bug is reported. Decide whether the kind is a column the sender picks, and whether an accessibility bug is listed ahead of the rest.
- **Data requests (an option, not decided here).** Requests under GDPR, the EU's data protection law, such as a copy of one's data or its deletion, may be one more kind on this form. The GDPR scoping task decides whether they are, or have a path of their own; this record notes the option and leaves the kind out until that task chooses.
- **The table.** Its name, and its columns: the sender (an account, or an address when signed out), the kind, a subject, the message, the page it was sent from, a status (open or resolved), who it is assigned to, whether editors may see it, who resolved it and when, and the audit columns every table spreads. Whether replies are rows of their own table, which would be a table task of its own. Soft-deleted or hard-deleted, and whether a resolved row is kept. A support row is written by the person sending it, so decide how it goes through `withAudit` (rule 3) when the sender holds no session.
- **The form.** A modal, the owner's call (2026-10-15), opened from the account menu and the shared footer, and already set to a kind when the link that opens it says so. Decide whether the modal also has an address of its own, so that a link to it works when shared or opened without JavaScript, and what it asks for; the length limits; and whether it says anything back beyond "received".
- **The admin list.** A page under `/admin`, paged through the M3.6 helper (rule 8), newest first, open requests ahead of resolved ones, with the actions an admin needs: reply, assign to an editor or an admin, mark viewable by editors, and mark resolved. The owner settled that an admin replies (2026-10-15); how the reply reaches the sender — mailed, shown on the site, or both, and what a signed-out sender receives — is the decision.
- **The editor view.** An editor (M11.20's role) sees only the requests an admin has marked viewable by editors or assigned to them, the owner's call, and never the rest, refused by direct id as well as absent from the list. Decide whether an editor can reply and resolve what is assigned to them, and whether this is the admin page filtered or a page of its own, since M11.20 keeps editors out of the people page.
- **Abuse.** A limit on how many submissions one account or address may make in a period, and whether a signed-out submission is held for a check before it is listed.
- **What it is not.** Not the help and FAQ articles MB.175 scopes, and not a notes or moderation system (§13). Not a way to request a correction to a compendium entry either: the owner deferred corrections with compendium suggestions (2026-10-15), so the form names no entry and the ingredient detail page carries no link to it.

Then mint the build, each task its own PR, in the order the rules fix: the table task first (`CREATE TABLE` and its migration, nothing else), then the service and mutation under rule 1 and rule 3, then the form modal, then the admin page, then the editor view, with the component docs and stories each page needs. Place them in Wave 15's row before M11.8, whose axe sweep scans every page, and before MB.124, the last design review, so that both cover them. Update `DESIGN.md` in the same PR: the table in §5, the mutation in §8's SDL, the routes in §9.

_Acceptance criteria:_

### Legal

**M15.2 — A Public Privacy Policy Says What Is Kept and Who Sees It** · 4h

_Story:_ As a person using the site, signed in or not, I want to read what personal data Sorrel & Salt keeps, who processes it, how long it is kept and who can see it, so that I can decide whether to trust the site with it.

The site has no privacy policy today, and no place for documents like it. This task builds that place: a public page layout for documents, reachable without an account and linked from the footer of both the signed-out and the signed-in layouts (the public shell and `AppShell`). The later document tasks (legal disclaimers, the code of ethics and the terms of use) reuse it. It then publishes the privacy policy on it.

The policy's text is drafted from the code and `DESIGN.md`, and the owner reviews it. Every claim traces to a file, so the policy describes what the site does rather than what a template says. It covers:

- **What is stored:** the account fields from the sign-in provider, the address the site writes to, session rows (including the IP address and browser details Better Auth records), the audit columns recording who wrote what, and what a coven holds.
- **Who processes it:** the hosting, database, mail and sign-in providers, each named, never a configured value.
- **How long it is kept:** soft deletes, where a deleted row is marked rather than removed, so "deleted" does not mean erased today.
- **How to ask for removal:** until the GDPR scoping task's builds land, by contacting whoever runs the site.
- **Who can see what:** a table for coven owners, members, viewers, editors (as M11.20 defines them), site admins and developers with database access. Each cell is taken from the authorization rules in the services, not from the screens.

The page may be indexed by search engines, but the site's own search does not list it. It runs after M11.20, so the editor role it describes exists.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- The privacy policy page answers 200 to a signed-out visitor, with no session required, and carries no `noindex`
- Every page in both layouts links to it from the footer, which is shared by the later document pages
- The compendium search and every other in-site search return nothing for it
- The who-can-see-what table names each role's access to each kind of data, and each cell cites the service or rule it comes from in the task's working notes
- Every other claim in the policy cites its source file or `DESIGN.md` section in those notes; nothing is stated that the code doesn't do
- The page shows when the policy was last updated
- An axe check in Playwright passes on the page, signed in and signed out
- The owner has reviewed and approved the text before the PR is ready
- `DESIGN.md` §9 lists the route, and `CLAUDE.md`'s "the compendium is the one public surface" line says that public document pages carry no data and are not an exception to it

**M15.3 — A Disclaimer Says the Site Is Not Advice, Beside Every Safety Note** · 2h

_Story:_ As a person reading about an ingredient, I want to be told plainly that the site's information is not medical or professional advice and that its safety notes are not complete, so that I don't rely on it for a decision it can't support.

The site describes ingredients that can be harmful. It shows safety notes, but nothing says what those notes are or aren't. This task publishes a disclaimer page on the document page and footer M15.2 builds. The page says:

- the site's information is for information only, not medical or professional advice
- the site comes with no warranty
- safety notes are not exhaustive, and an ingredient with no safety note is not thereby safe
- an ingredient can be harmful in a way its usual use doesn't suggest: something safe to eat may give off toxic smoke when burned, or irritate the skin as an oil. This is a warning, not medical advice

It also adds a short notice beside the safety notes on the ingredient detail page (built in M8.19), linking to the full page. The notice appears on both compendium and coven entries, and on each form's section as well as the ingredient's own. The text is drafted with every claim sourced, and the owner reviews it. It runs after M15.2, whose document page and footer it uses.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- The disclaimer page answers 200 to a signed-out visitor, with no session required, and carries no `noindex`
- The shared footer links to it from every page in both layouts
- The compendium search and every other in-site search return nothing for it
- The warning about unforeseen uses gives at least one real example, cited to a source in Chicago form as the compendium's references are, so the example is sourced rather than invented
- On the ingredient detail page, every safety-notes section, the ingredient's own and each form's, for a compendium entry and for a coven's entry, carries a link to the disclaimer page
- The notice appears even where an ingredient has no safety notes, since that absence is exactly the case the disclaimer covers
- The page shows when it was last updated
- An axe check in Playwright passes on the page and on an ingredient detail page carrying the notice
- The owner has reviewed and approved the text before the PR is ready

**M15.4 — The Code of Ethics Says What Sorrel & Salt Stands For** · 1h

_Story:_ As a person using the site, I want to read what Sorrel & Salt values and how it handles the cultures, sourcing and access questions its subject raises, so that I know what kind of place I'm joining.

This task publishes the code of ethics on the document page and footer that M15.2 builds. Claude drafts an outline of the topics; the owner writes the text and reviews the page. The outline covers:

- what Sorrel & Salt values
- closed practices, and the cultures that ingredients and correspondences come from
- ethical and sustainable sourcing, such as endangered plants and animal parts
- why the compendium cites its sources

It does not build the two fast-follow warnings in the backlog (a closed practice warning, and ethical warnings on ingredients); the page may describe them as planned. It runs after M15.3, and after the support form page that M15.1's build tasks create.
