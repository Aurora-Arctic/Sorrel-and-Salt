**M15.1 — Scope a support form reviewed by admins** · 3h

_Story: As a person using the site, I want to send a question, a problem or a bug report — an accessibility bug included — to whoever runs it from inside the site, so that I am not left searching for an address; and as an admin, I want those messages in one list I can work through, answer, and hand to a curator or another admin, so that nothing is lost in a mailbox._

A scoping task, in the shape of MB.175's: it decides, records and mints, and builds nothing itself. The site has no way to be contacted today. The shape the owner asked for is a form whose submissions are rows in a database table, reviewed by admins on the admin section rather than mailed to them. The record it produces is `claude-docs/design-decisions/m15.1-support-form.md`, and each decision below is answered there, with its reason, before the build tasks are minted.

The decisions to make:

- **Who can submit.** The compendium is the one public surface (MB.80), so a signed-out reader may have a question too. Decide whether the form takes submissions from a signed-out visitor, with the spam exposure that brings, or only from a signed-in account, which is what every other write here requires.
- **The kinds of request.** A question, a bug, and an accessibility bug at least, added on the owner's call (2026-10-15) so that the code of ethics (M15.4) can point to this form as the way an accessibility bug is reported. Decide whether the kind is a column the sender picks, and whether an accessibility bug is listed ahead of the rest.
- **Data requests (an option, not decided here).** Requests under GDPR, the EU's data protection law, such as a copy of one's data or its deletion, may be one more kind on this form. The GDPR scoping task decides whether they are, or have a path of their own; this record notes the option and leaves the kind out until that task chooses.
- **The table.** Its name, and its columns: the sender (an account, or an address when signed out), the kind, a subject, the message, the page it was sent from, a status (open or resolved), who it is assigned to, whether curators may see it, who resolved it and when, and the audit columns every table spreads. Whether replies are rows of their own table, which would be a table task of its own. Soft-deleted or hard-deleted, and whether a resolved row is kept. A support row is written by the person sending it, so decide how it goes through `withAudit` (rule 3) when the sender holds no session.
- **The form.** A modal, the owner's call (2026-10-15), opened from the account menu and the shared footer, and already set to a kind when the link that opens it says so. Decide whether the modal also has an address of its own, so that a link to it works when shared or opened without JavaScript, and what it asks for; the length limits; and whether it says anything back beyond "received".
- **The admin list.** A page under `/admin`, paged through the M3.6 helper (rule 8), newest first, open requests ahead of resolved ones, with the actions an admin needs: reply, assign to a curator or an admin, mark viewable by curators, and mark resolved. The owner settled that an admin replies (2026-10-15); how the reply reaches the sender — mailed, shown on the site, or both, and what a signed-out sender receives — is the decision.
- **The curator view.** A curator (M11.20's role) sees only the requests an admin has marked viewable by curators or assigned to them, the owner's call, and never the rest, refused by direct id as well as absent from the list. Decide whether a curator can reply and resolve what is assigned to them, and whether this is the admin page filtered or a page of its own, since M11.20 keeps curators out of the people page.
- **Abuse.** A limit on how many submissions one account or address may make in a period, and whether a signed-out submission is held for a check before it is listed.
- **What it is not.** Not the help and FAQ articles MB.175 scopes, and not a notes or moderation system (§13). Not a way to request a correction to a compendium entry either: the owner deferred corrections with compendium suggestions (2026-10-15), so the form names no entry and the ingredient detail page carries no link to it.

Then mint the build, each task its own PR, in the order the rules fix: the table task first (`CREATE TABLE` and its migration, nothing else), then the service and mutation under rule 1 and rule 3, then the form modal, then the admin page, then the curator view, with the component docs and stories each page needs. Place them in Wave 15's row before M11.8, whose axe sweep scans every page, and before MB.124, the last design review, so that both cover them. Update `DESIGN.md` in the same PR: the table in §5, the mutation in §8's SDL, the routes in §9.

**M15.26 — The `support` Module and the `support_requests` Table** · 2h

_Story:_ As a developer, I want the support module and its first table to exist before any behaviour does, so that the services, the form and the admin page each have a table to build on.

**M15.28 — Support Services and GraphQL** · 5h

_Story:_ As a person with a question or a bug, I want to send a request and read the answer, and as an admin or curator, I want to work through the requests I may see, so that nothing sent is lost.

Prerequisite: M15.27. The services in `src/modules/support/services/`, each opening with `import 'server-only'`, the repository finders and writers beneath them in `src/db/repository/`, and the resolvers on the shared builder. Every write goes through `withAudit` (rule 3), every list through `t.pagedConnection` and `findPage` (rule 8), every nested read through a DataLoader (rule 9).

- **`sendSupportRequest`** — requires a session whose account has a verified address. Takes a kind, a subject of 1 to 120 characters, a message of 1 to 4,000, an optional page path and an optional `errorCode`. Stores the path with the segment after `/coven/` replaced by a fixed placeholder. Refuses, each with an explaining error naming the reason and not a bare `Forbidden`: an eleventh request inside 24 hours (counted in SQL on `support_requests` by `createdBy`), and a send while a time-out is active (the error names the time it ends). A viewer, and an account in no coven, may send.
- **`mySupportRequests`** — a paged query of the session's own requests, newest first, each with its replies, and never its `issueUrl`: the issue address is staff-only, so the sender's services blank it and the field needs a curator scope (2026-10-31, the owner's call). The finder ANDs `createdBy` itself. A request by id for another sender is refused.
- **`supportRequests`** — a paged query for a curator or an admin, open ahead of resolved, newest first within each. An admin reads all; a curator reads only requests marked `curatorVisible` or assigned to them, filtered in SQL.
- **`replyToSupportRequest`** — body of 1 to 4,000 characters. An admin may reply to any request; a curator only to one assigned to them. Mails the sender the notice from the new template `src/emails/support-reply-notice.tsx` through `send()`; the notice says a reply is waiting and links to `/account/support`, and carries no reply text.
- **`assignSupportRequest`** and **`setSupportRequestCuratorVisible`** — admin only.
- **`resolveSupportRequest`** — an admin on any request, a curator on one assigned to them. Records `resolvedBy` and `resolvedAt`.
- **`setSupportRequestIssueUrl`** — admin only. The address must be an issue on `Aurora-Arctic/Sorrel-and-Salt`; any other host, repository or path is refused with an explaining error.
- **`timeOutSupportSender`** and **`liftSupportTimeout`** — admin only. A time-out takes a user, an end in the future and an optional note; lifting sets `liftedAt`. A curator is refused both.

The SDL additions are the ones `DESIGN.md` §7 lists. `claude-docs/email.md`'s "What is sent" row for the notice is made true.

**M15.55 — An Owner Pauses and Lifts a Member's Coven Access** · 3h

_Story:_ As a coven owner, I want to pause a member's or a viewer's access to my coven, dated or open-ended, and lift it early, so that I can stop them causing issues in my coven.

Adds the service and GraphQL mutations for the coven pause, under a new owner-only `permission`. While a pause stands, `assertMembership` refuses the user for that workspace. The user keeps their seat, and their other covens are unchanged.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- An owner can pause a member or a viewer of their coven, dated or open-ended, with an optional note, and can lift the pause. Tested by direct id: a member, a viewer, an owner of another coven and a site admin are each refused.
- Pausing an owner is refused with an explaining error.
- While a pause stands, every read and write of that coven by the paused user is refused. Tested through `assertMembership`, by direct id, with the test also proving the same call succeeds once the pause is lifted.
- The paused user's seat and their access to their other covens are unchanged.
- A dated pause ends by itself when `until` passes.
- Every write goes through `withAudit`.
- `DESIGN.md` §5 and §8 describe the coven pause.

**M15.56 — Pause and Lift From a Coven's Members Page** · 3h

_Story:_ As a coven owner, I want to pause and lift a member's access from the members page and see earlier pauses there, and I want a paused member to be told why they cannot open the coven.

Adds Pause Access and Lift Pause to each member and viewer row on `/coven/[slug]/members`, and a list of earlier pauses showing who set and lifted each one. A paused member who opens the coven's pages sees a page saying their access to this coven is paused and, for a dated pause, until when. This is not a 404, because they hold a seat.

_Acceptance criteria:_

- An owner can set a dated or an open-ended pause and lift it. The row then shows the state it leaves.
- The actions do not appear on owner rows, and do not appear to a member or a viewer.
- A paused member who opens any page of that coven sees the paused page. A non-member still gets a 404.
- A Playwright spec walks pause, the paused page, lift and access restored. axe passes on the dialog and on the paused page.
