## Using the Site

### Who May Use the Site

Anyone may read the public compendium without an account. Signing in by any method gives you an account and a viewer's seat in the demo coven.

You must be at least thirteen years old. The site is run from the United States, whose Children's Online Privacy Protection Act treats anyone under thirteen as a child whose personal information an online service may not collect without a parent's consent, and the site does not ask for a parent's consent.[^1][^2]

Your account is yours alone. Keep your sign-in details to yourself, and tell us through the [support form](?support=account) if you think someone else has used them without your permission.

### What Is Yours

Everything you write in a coven stays yours: your ingredients, your stock, your spells, their steps and their workings. The site claims no ownership over any of it, and takes no right to use it beyond storing it and showing it to the people your coven settings allow. We promise that it is never sold, never used to train anything and never shown outside the coven it was written in.

A spell marked private is shown to you alone. Once a spell is complete and shared with your coven, it cannot be made private again, because the people who have seen it may already rely on it. To have a private version of a shared spell again, use Duplicate: it makes a private draft copy that only you can see and edit, and the shared spell stays as it was.

A coven's owner may delete the coven, and may remove any member from it. Any member may leave a coven at any time. The demo coven, where every account begins, cannot be deleted.

### The Compendium

The compendium is written and checked by the site's admins and curators. Every entry records where its information came from, and an entry with no source stays on the curators' list of work to do. It is a reference for people who practise, and it is offered as one, as the [Disclaimer](/disclaimer) sets out.

The [Code of Ethics](/code-of-ethics) says what the site stands for. Its section "What Everyone Agrees To" binds every account, and agreeing to these terms is agreeing to it. In short: no bigotry, in any form, towards anyone.

The Code of Ethics goes on to ask more of the people who run the site. Its sections for curators, for admins and for the site's owner each add to the one before, and bind whoever holds that role for as long as they hold it.

The tools the site has today for keeping to this are small, and we name them rather than promise more. A coven's owner may remove a member from the coven. The owner may also pause a member's access to the coven for as long as the owner decides. A paused member keeps their place in the coven but cannot open it. Their other covens are not affected. A site admin may stop a person sending support requests for as long as an admin decides, and may take away the right to create covens. Accounts are never closed. A site admin may pause an account's access to the site for as long as an admin decides. A paused account can still sign in to ask for support, but cannot open anything else. The compendium stays open to it, as it does to everyone.

## Disputes

### Which Law Applies

The site is run from the State of Washington, in the United States, and these terms are governed by the law of that state. Nothing in them takes away a protection that the law of the place you live gives you and does not let you give up.

### Settling a Dispute

If you and the site's owner have a dispute about these terms or the site, each of you first tries arbitration: a neutral person hears both sides and gives a decision. The arbitration is non-binding, which means the decision is advice, not an order, and either of you may reject it.[^5] Nobody gives up the right to go to court, or the right to bring a claim together with other people, by agreeing to these terms.

Whoever asks for the arbitration pays the arbitrator. The site's owner does not pay for an arbitration a member starts, and a member does not pay for one the owner starts.

A person who cannot afford that may skip arbitration and go straight to court. The test is the one Washington's courts use when they waive their own filing fees for people who cannot pay them, General Rule 34: you qualify if you receive help from a needs-based programme such as TANF, SSI, poverty-related veterans' benefits or food stamps; or your household income is at or below 125 per cent of the federal poverty guideline; or it is above that line but your recurring basic living expenses leave you unable to pay; or other compelling circumstances show you cannot pay.[^6] You check yourself against those guidelines; you need no order from anyone first. The court looks at the same question when you ask it to waive your filing fees.[^6] If the claim of hardship was false, the owner may raise that in the same case. The point of this is simple: a person with a real claim is never priced out of court.

If arbitration does not settle the dispute, either of you may take it to the state courts of Washington in Spokane County, where the Spokane County Superior Court sits, and you and the site's owner each agree to that court hearing it.[^7]

If a dispute goes to court, the judge can make one side pay the other side's reasonable costs, including lawyer's fees. This happens only when the judge finds that side's claim or defence frivolous, meaning it had no real basis and was pursued without good reason.[^8] It works both ways: the judge can order it against the site's owner just as against you. Only the judge can order this, not the arbitrator.
